"Read it later" is a simple job with a famous failure mode: the saving works, the reading never happens, and the app becomes a guilt pile with a search box. When Pocket — the app that defined the category — shut down, it left a real gap, and a lot of people rethinking what they actually want from this kind of tool.

Marqly's answer: the problem isn't saving too much, it's having no way to decide what's worth reading. So Marqly pairs one-click saving with AI summaries that let you triage the backlog — skim what a piece says, then read it properly or archive it without guilt. Saving stays effortless; deciding becomes fast.

## One click to save, zero effort to file

The save step has to be free or you'll stop doing it. With the Marqly extension (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari), saving is one click from the toolbar — and there's no second step. No folder to choose, no tags to type: **AI tags every save automatically**, so a longread about batteries files itself under the things it's about.

When "later" arrives, you're not staring at a wall of identical links. Saves are tagged, summarized, and searchable. And if your reading backlog currently lives in 30 open tabs, the tab saver captures [all your open tabs at once](/faq/how-do-i-save-all-my-open-tabs) — the fastest honest way to declare tab bankruptcy without losing anything.

## AI summaries turn the pile into a triage list

This is the piece most read-it-later apps never had. Marqly generates AI summaries of your saved articles, which changes the daily interaction: instead of scrolling a list of titles wondering what's behind each one, you skim summaries and sort your queue into three piles — read now, read on the weekend, never (archive without opening).

The guilt pile was always a decision problem in disguise. A backlog of 200 unread articles is unmanageable; 200 summaries is twenty minutes of triage. For how Marqly compares to other apps in the category — including on this point — see [the best read-it-later apps in 2026](/blog/best-read-it-later-apps-2026).

## Read actively: highlight and keep what mattered

Reading later is only worth it if something survives the reading. Marqly's highlighter lets you select text on any website, highlight in one of **six colors**, and attach notes. Highlights persist on the page when you revisit — come back to the article next year and your markup is still there — and they sync to your library, where they're searchable on their own.

That means the output of your reading isn't a vague memory; it's a growing collection of passages you chose to keep. The full walkthrough is in [how to highlight text on any website](/blog/how-to-highlight-text-on-any-website).

When a run of reading turns into a genuine collection — the ten best pieces on a topic — boards give it a shape: group the links and highlights deliberately, and share the board as a public page anyone can view without signing up. A reading list you can hand to someone is a different thing from a backlog.

## Find the article you half-remember

Every long-time read-it-later user knows this moment: you read something months ago — an essay about, roughly, why cities got expensive — and now you need it. Title? Gone. Author? No idea.

Marqly's semantic search is built for exactly that memory. Describe the piece the way you remember it, and search matches by meaning across titles, content, and your highlights. "The essay about zoning making housing expensive" finds the right save even if none of those words appear in its headline. Your archive stops being write-only.

Videos fit the same workflow, by the way — if your "later" queue is half YouTube, Marqly [saves videos with their transcripts attached](/blog/save-youtube-videos-watch-later), and search covers what's said in them.

The honest limitation first: **Marqly has no offline reading mode.** If most of your reading happens on planes or underground, a dedicated offline reader serves that specific need better, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

Where Marqly fits is the larger version of the job: you save more than you can read, you want help deciding what deserves attention, and you want what you've read — and highlighted — to stay findable for years. If that's the actual problem, try it free at [app.marqly.com](https://app.marqly.com).
