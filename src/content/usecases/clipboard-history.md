The system clipboard holds exactly one thing. Copy a second, and the first is gone — no warning, no undo. Everyone has done the small, stupid version of this: copy an address, copy a tracking number to check something, go to paste the address, paste the tracking number. And the bigger version, the one that costs real time: an afternoon of research where you copied thirty useful sentences and kept only the last one.

Marqly's **Clipboard History**, part of the Chrome and Edge extensions, fixes the one-slot problem for anything you copy on the web. Every snippet is kept instead of overwritten, and the pile becomes something you can search, filter, tag, and pin.

## Every copy is kept, not overwritten

Copy text from a webpage and it lands in your Clipboard History automatically. There's no "save this snippet" step to remember, which matters, because the moment you copy something you almost never know yet whether you'll want it back. The capture has to be free or it doesn't happen.

What ends up in there is the connective tissue of a workday: quotes from an article, an address, a price, a shipping code, a config line from a documentation page, a name you keep misspelling, the phrasing from a support thread that finally explained the thing. None of it is worth a full bookmark. All of it is annoying to lose.

## Search, filter, tag, pin, favorite

A long clipboard history that you can only scroll is barely better than no history at all. The Clipboard workspace gives the pile structure:

- **Search** the text of your snippets to pull one back by what it said.
- **Filter** the list once it's grown past what you can scan.
- **Tag** items to build your own grouping — `client-x`, `quotes`, `snippets` — as loose or as strict as you like.
- **Favorite** the items that matter beyond today.
- **Pin** the two or three you're pasting over and over this week to keep them at the top.

The result is a small working memory beside your library rather than an archive you'll never open. Most items age out of relevance and that's fine — the ones you tag or pin are the ones you actually meant to keep.

## Clipboard snippets vs page highlights

Marqly has two ways to keep text, and picking the right one saves you friction later.

**Clipboard History is for text in transit.** You copied it because you're about to use it somewhere, and it's useful to still have it an hour from now.

**[Highlights](/blog/how-to-highlight-text-on-any-website) are for text in context.** Select a passage on a page, mark it in one of six colors, attach a note — and it stays on that page. Reopen the article next month and your marks are still there, notes included, in position. That's the tool for passages you'll want to find again *as part of the source*, and the mechanics are in [how do I highlight text on a website](/faq/how-do-i-highlight-text-on-a-website).

Rough rule: if you'd be sad to lose the surrounding page, highlight it. If you just need the words, copy it and let the clipboard hold them.

Clipboard History isn't a standalone utility bolted on — it lives in the same extension that saves pages in one click, captures every open tab at once, and runs [semantic search](/faq/how-do-i-search-my-bookmarks-by-meaning) across your library. So the snippet you copied and the article you copied it from end up in the same tool, reachable without a context switch.

## Where it works, plainly

Clipboard History is available in the **Chrome and Microsoft Edge extensions only.** It is not in the Firefox or Safari extensions today — [Firefox](/faq/does-marqly-work-on-firefox) and [Safari](/faq/does-marqly-work-on-safari) get the rest of Marqly, including one-click saving, the highlighter, AI tagging, and semantic search, but not this feature.

One more boundary worth stating up front: it captures what you copy **from webpages in the browser.** It is not a system-wide clipboard manager, so text you copy in a desktop app doesn't appear in it.

On sync: capture works in the browser on any plan, and **Marqly Pro adds account sync for clipboard items**, so what you copied on one machine is available on the other.
