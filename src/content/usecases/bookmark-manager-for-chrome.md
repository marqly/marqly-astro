Chrome's built-in bookmark manager has barely changed in a decade: a star, a folder tree, and a search box that only matches titles and URLs. That's fine for 20 links. It fails completely at 2,000, which is roughly where every long-time Chrome user ends up. Marqly is an AI bookmark manager delivered as a Chrome extension — free to install from the [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/marqly-all-in-one-bookmar/kcadneobjofkppmekgadodnaojoehemc) — that replaces filing with automatic AI tagging and replaces title search with semantic search that finds saves by what you remember about them.

## Where Chrome's built-in bookmarks stop

The star button captures a URL and a title. Nothing else. Which means Chrome's bookmark search can only ever match a URL or a title — and three months later, you don't remember either. You remember that the article was about pricing psychology, or that the video explained sourdough starters. Chrome has no way to take that as a query.

Folders make it worse, not better. Every save demands a filing decision, most saves end up in the bookmarks-bar junk drawer anyway, and the taxonomy you built in January doesn't match how you think in June. And while Chrome does sync bookmarks between machines, that sync lives inside your Google account and only reaches other Chromes — your saves are locked to one browser vendor.

## What the Marqly extension adds to Chrome

Marqly keeps the part Chrome got right — saving must be instant — and rebuilds everything after the click. One click on the toolbar saves the page; add tags at save time if you want, or don't, because AI auto-tagging labels every save without you filing anything. Each saved article also gets an AI summary, which turns a backlog review from re-reading into skimming.

The side panel is the piece that changes daily use: your whole library — browse and search — opens beside the current page, so checking "did I already save something about this?" doesn't cost you a tab switch. Chrome's tab hoarders get a dedicated mercy feature too: the tab saver captures every open tab in one action.

Two Chrome-extension features go beyond bookmarks entirely. On every YouTube watch page, Marqly adds an AI card with a streaming summary tab (TL;DR, key sections, even books mentioned in the video), a transcript tab synced to playback, and — on Pro — a chat tab that answers questions from the video's transcript; a bookmark button in YouTube's own action row saves the video with its transcript attached. And the highlighter lets you select text on any website, mark it in one of six colors with optional notes, and have those highlights persist on the page and sync to your library. There's even save-as-PDF: capture any page as a clean PDF matching the on-screen layout, processed locally in your browser.

Retrieval is where it all pays off. Semantic search matches meaning across titles, content, highlights, and transcripts — described in detail in our guide to [searching bookmarks with AI](/blog/how-to-search-bookmarks-with-ai). "That video about sourdough starters" finds the right save without the title.

## Bring your existing Chrome bookmarks with you

You don't start from zero. Chrome exports your entire bookmark tree as a standard HTML file (Bookmark manager → Export bookmarks), and Marqly imports it directly — the full walkthrough is in [how to import Chrome bookmarks into an AI bookmark manager](/blog/how-to-import-chrome-bookmarks-to-ai). After import, the AI tags your whole backlog, which is the moment years of dead folders become a searchable library. Coming from elsewhere? Pocket exports and Raindrop.io collections import too.

## Getting started

1. Open the [Marqly listing on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/marqly-all-in-one-bookmar/kcadneobjofkppmekgadodnaojoehemc) and click "Add to Chrome" — step-by-step help lives in [our install FAQ](/faq/how-do-i-install-the-chrome-extension).
2. Pin Marqly to the toolbar so the one-click save is actually one click.
3. Sign up free at [app.marqly.com](https://app.marqly.com); no card required. Pro — $72 a year (about $6 a month billed annually) or $9 monthly, with a 3-day free trial — unlocks features like chatting with your saves.
4. Import your Chrome bookmarks HTML and let the AI process the backlog. Boards are worth setting up early too — they group related links and highlights by project, and any board can be shared as a public page viewable without an account.
