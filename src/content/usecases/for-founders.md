Founders do competitive research in stolen moments — a rival's launch post skimmed between standups, a pricing teardown read at midnight, a market report someone dropped in a group chat. None of it gets filed, because filing is exactly the kind of work that loses to shipping. Then a fundraise or a positioning decision arrives, and reconstructing what you knew about the market costs a full day. Marqly is built for that reality: saving takes one click, AI does all the organizing, and months of accumulated intel becomes something you can query.

## Track competitors without a spreadsheet ritual

The competitor spreadsheet is where founder discipline goes to die — updated enthusiastically for two weeks, then abandoned. Marqly replaces the ritual with a reflex: see something relevant, click save, done. Auto-tagging files it; a quick manual tag per competitor makes their whole history one click to pull up. After a diligence binge, the tab saver banks every open tab in one action. Review threads deserve the same reflex — the complaints users post about a competitor today are raw material for your positioning tomorrow.

The underrated weapon is save-as-PDF. Competitor pricing pages change without announcements, and the old version disappears with the evidence. Marqly captures any page as a clean PDF matching the on-screen layout — lazy-loaded images included, processed locally in your browser — so you accumulate dated snapshots of what rivals charged and claimed, quarter over quarter. When someone asks "didn't they use to have a free tier?", you have receipts.

We compete in this category ourselves, for what it's worth — [our comparison of AI bookmark managers](/blog/best-ai-bookmark-manager-2026) is written the way we'd want a competitor's to be: conceding the rows others win.

## Market research that compounds instead of evaporating

Individual saves are cheap; the asset is the accumulation. Six months of saved market reports, analyst posts, and user-complaint threads becomes genuinely valuable the day you can retrieve from it, and that's what [semantic search](/faq/what-is-semantic-search) delivers: meaning-based matching across titles, content, highlights, and video transcripts. "The post arguing SMB churn is a pricing problem, not a product problem" is a real query that finds a real save — see [how AI bookmark search behaves](/blog/how-to-search-bookmarks-with-ai) with exactly this kind of half-memory.

The library also travels: the extension, web app, and iOS app share one pool, so the thread saved from your phone while waiting for coffee is searchable from your laptop an hour later. Nothing about the system assumes research happens at scheduled times, because for founders it never does.

Highlights make the compounding steeper. Mark the market-size figure, the competitor's admission in a forum thread, the line from a customer review that belongs in your positioning — with a note attached — and search gets sharper while your evidence base builds itself in the background of normal reading.

## Long-form intel, compressed

A disproportionate share of startup insight hides in long-form video: founder interviews, product teardowns, conference talks. Marqly's AI card on every YouTube page gives you the summary and key sections up front — enough to decide whether the two-hour interview holds fifteen relevant minutes — plus a playback-synced transcript and even the books mentioned in the video. Saving through Marqly's button in YouTube's action row attaches the transcript, and on Pro, [chatting with the video](/blog/chat-with-youtube-videos) gets specific questions answered straight from it: "what does she say about pricing before product-market fit?"

The same applies to text. AI summaries triage the reading queue, and Pro's chat over your saved content means positioning docs and investor-update prep can start with [questions asked of your own research](/blog/how-to-chat-with-your-saved-articles) — grounded in sources you chose, not whatever the open web serves up under deadline.

## Getting started

Install the extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari — it's rated 4.8 on the Chrome Web Store. Import the research you've already scattered: standard browser bookmark HTML exports, Pocket exports, and Raindrop.io collections all import cleanly. Let the AI tag the backlog once, and the graveyard of "competitors???" folders becomes a searchable market memory. From there the system runs on reflex saves, with boards for anything deserving structure — one per competitor, one for the fundraise reading, one for positioning references.

Start [free at app.marqly.com](https://app.marqly.com); no card is required, and the full capture-and-search loop works on the free tier.

## Who this isn't for

Marqly won't monitor competitors for you — there are no alerts, no automated page-change tracking, no crawling; it organizes what you save, when you save it. It's also single-player: no team features means your co-founder can't work inside your library, and sharing is limited to public, view-only board pages. Founders wanting automated competitive-intelligence tooling or a shared team wiki need different software. Founders whose market knowledge currently lives in dead tabs and memory — which is most of them — will feel the difference in a week.
