The default new tab page is a search box and a grid of thumbnails you didn't choose. You open it dozens of times a day, and it gives you nothing back — which is why the whole category of new tab extensions exists, and why most of them stop at a pretty photograph.

**Marqly Home** takes the same real estate and makes it useful: your saved library is searchable from it, your own quick links and folders sit where you put them, and notes, to-dos, weather, and saved sessions are all in view before you type anything.

## Search your library from the blank page

This is the part other new tab extensions can't do, because they aren't attached to a library. Marqly Home searches everything you've saved, right from the new tab — no opening the web app, no remembering where you filed something.

And it's [semantic search](/faq/how-do-i-search-my-bookmarks-by-meaning), so you don't need the title. Describe the thing — "the guide about sourdough hydration" — and the save comes back on meaning rather than exact keywords, across titles, page content, highlights, and video transcripts. Making retrieval this cheap is what turns a pile of saves into something you use daily; the longer argument is in [how to search bookmarks with AI](/blog/how-to-search-bookmarks-with-ai).

The practical effect: the reflex of opening a tab and typing a URL from memory gets replaced by opening a tab and asking for what you actually want.

## Quick links and folders, arranged your way

Add the sites you genuinely use, drag them into the order that matches how you work, and group the rest into folders so the page stays calm. A configurable **dock** holds what you reach for constantly, and a **background** you choose makes the page yours.

None of this is revolutionary on its own — it's just that the default new tab won't let you do any of it. Ten seconds of arranging beats a top-sites grid that ranks by an algorithm you can't see.

## Notes, to-dos, and stickies that stay visible

Marqly Home uses a desktop-style layout, with draggable, resizable windows:

- **Notes** — a scratch pad for the paragraph you're drafting or the number you need in a minute.
- **To-dos** — a short list for the things you're doing right now, not your whole project management system.
- **Stickies** — left directly on the desktop for the one line you must not lose today.

The value is that this material stays *visible*. Small, temporary context is exactly what a notes app swallows: you write it down, close the app, and never see it again. On the new tab page, it's in front of you fifty times a day whether you go looking or not.

**Weather** sits alongside them, for the same reason — a glance-level fact you'd otherwise open a tab to check.

## Saved sessions, back in one move

If you regularly work in a cluster of tabs for a project, save the group and bring it back from the dashboard later instead of reconstructing it from browser history. Combined with [saving all your open tabs](/faq/how-do-i-save-all-my-open-tabs) into your library in one click, it covers both halves of the tab problem: the group you'll return to on Thursday, and the forty tabs you'll never reopen but are afraid to close.

More on the second half in [the tab saver lander](/tab-manager), which explains why closing a window stops being risky once finding things is easy.

## The rest of the extension is right there

Because Marqly Home is part of the Marqly extension rather than a standalone new tab app, everything else is one click away from the same page: one-click saving with AI auto-tagging, the [six-color highlighter](/web-highlighter), the side panel that puts your library next to whatever you're reading, and — in Chrome and Edge — [Clipboard History](/clipboard-history) for the snippets you copy while researching.

That's the argument for a new tab page attached to a library instead of a widget board: the page you look at fifty times a day is also the front door to everything you've kept.

## Where it works

Marqly Home is available in **Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Firefox.** It is **not** available in Safari today — [Safari](/faq/does-marqly-work-on-safari) gets one-click saving, the highlighter, AI tagging, and semantic search, but not the New Tab dashboard.

There's no Android app, and the new tab dashboard is a desktop-browser feature by nature; on iOS, the [Marqly app](/faq/is-there-an-ios-app) covers your library rather than your browser's new tab.

And if you have no saved library at all, you're getting the widget half of the value and skipping the half that makes it distinct.
