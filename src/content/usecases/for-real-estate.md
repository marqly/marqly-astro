A working agent's browser is a mess by Tuesday: portal listings for three different buyers, a market report for a listing presentation, neighborhood articles a client asked about, and comps you'll need again in a month. The research is genuinely valuable — and genuinely scattered. Bookmarks folders don't survive the pace, and "I'll find it again" costs you twenty minutes in front of a client.

Marqly is an AI bookmark manager that handles this without asking you to file anything. Save any page in one click, let AI tag it automatically, and find it later by describing what you remember — "the craftsman with the big backyard near the elementary school" — instead of scrolling a hundred saved links.

## Save a whole browsing session at once

Listing research doesn't happen one page at a time. You open twelve tabs from the portal, cross-reference two comps, then a school-ratings page, then a flood-zone map. Marqly's extension (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) matches that pattern:

- **One-click save** for the single listing that matters, with tags if you want them.
- **Tab saver** to capture the entire session — [save all open tabs at once](/faq/how-do-i-save-all-my-open-tabs) when you're done browsing, and sort it out later. Except you won't need to sort: AI tags every save automatically.
- **Side-panel library** to check whether you already saved a listing without leaving the portal.

## One board per client, one per neighborhood

Boards are where the research becomes client-facing. Group links and highlights into a board per buyer — the shortlist, the comps, the neighborhood articles — or per farm area you're tracking.

Then use the feature that changes the workflow: **share a board as a public page**. Your client opens a clean page of everything you've curated for them — no signup, no app install, no forwarded links in a text thread. When they say "what was that house with the ADU," it's on the page you already sent. For a general framework on structuring boards and tags, see [how to organize bookmarks](/blog/how-to-organize-bookmarks).

## Keep listings after the portal pulls them

Listings die. The house sells, the page 404s, and your comp research goes with it. Marqly's **Save as PDF** captures any page as a clean PDF matching the on-screen layout — lazy-loaded photos included — processed locally in your browser. Capture the listing while it's live and the PDF stays in your library after the portal takes the page down. Six months later, when a seller asks what the comparable on Maple Street actually listed for, you have the full page, photos and all.

## Find "that house" by describing it

Marqly's semantic search works across titles, page content, and your highlights — by meaning, not keywords. You don't need the address or the MLS number; "the mid-century with the weird kitchen the Hendersons liked" is a valid query. That's the difference between a bookmark pile and a research library: you retrieve by whatever detail stuck.

Highlights make market reports usable too. Select the number that matters — median days on market, the absorption rate — highlight it in one of six colors, and add a note ("use in Oakdale listing presentation"). Highlights persist on the page when you revisit and sync to your library, so the stat is findable without rereading the report. Here's [how highlighting works on any website](/blog/how-to-highlight-text-on-any-website).

AI summaries earn their keep on long reports: before committing twenty minutes to a quarterly market analysis, read the summary and decide if it's relevant to your farm area at all. The same goes for video — when a local market update or a walkthrough lives on YouTube, Marqly's extension saves the video with its transcript attached, so what was said in it becomes searchable alongside your listings and reports.

Your library travels, too: the iOS app puts your saves and boards on your phone, which is where you are between showings. Pull up the client's board from the car, not from memory.

## Is Marqly the right fit?

Straight answer: Marqly organizes web research — it is not real-estate software. No MLS integration, no CRM sync (there's no public API), no transaction management, and no Android app (the web app works fine in Android browsers). Board sharing is view-only, so clients browse what you curate; they don't collaborate on it.
