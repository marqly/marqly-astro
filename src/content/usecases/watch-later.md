YouTube's own Watch Later has a well-earned reputation: videos go in, nothing comes out. The list grows into hundreds of thumbnails, there's no way to remember why you saved anything, and search only matches titles — useless when what you remember is something the person *said*.

Marqly treats watch-later as a research problem, not a playlist problem. Save a video from the watch page and it goes into your library **with its transcript attached**. From there, AI summaries let you triage the queue, and semantic search finds videos by what's said in them. The queue becomes something you actually process.

## Save from the watch page, transcript included

Install the Marqly extension (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari) and a bookmark button appears in YouTube's own action row — right where like and share live. One click saves the video to your library with the transcript attached, and AI tags it automatically like any other save.

That transcript is the difference between bookmarking a video and actually capturing it. A saved link tells you a video exists; a saved transcript means its content is in your library — searchable, summarizable, quotable. The full workflow is in [how to save YouTube videos for later](/blog/save-youtube-videos-watch-later).

## Summaries tell you what's worth 40 minutes

The reason watch-later lists rot is cost: an article takes five minutes to evaluate, a video demands 40 minutes of linear watching just to find out if it was worth it. So nothing gets evaluated, and the list just grows.

Marqly's AI card on every YouTube watch page removes that cost. The **AI Summary tab** gives you a streaming TL;DR plus the video's key sections — and even books mentioned in the video. Read the summary, then make a real decision: watch in full, jump to the one section that matters, or archive without guilt. Twenty minutes of summary-skimming can honestly clear a queue that's been growing for a year. More on the summarizer itself in [summarize YouTube videos with AI](/blog/summarize-youtube-videos-with-ai).

There's also a **Transcript tab** — playback-synced, with one-click copy — for when you'd rather read the video than watch it. Pro users get a **Chat tab** too: ask a question and get an answer drawn from that video's transcript, which beats scrubbing the timeline hunting for the moment they discussed pricing.

## Search the queue by what's said in the videos

Here's where saved transcripts pay off long-term. Marqly's semantic search works across titles, content, highlights, **and transcripts** — by meaning, not keywords. "The video where he explains why heat pumps work in cold climates" finds the right save even if the title is "HVAC Q&A #12."

No native watch-later feature on any platform searches inside your videos like this. Once transcripts live in your library, your video queue gets the same retrieval quality as your saved articles — describe what you remember, get the video back. And because videos sit in the same library as everything else you save, one search covers both: research a topic once and the relevant article, the highlighted passage, and the conference talk all come back together instead of living in separate apps.

## Boards keep queues from becoming one big pile

One watch-later list is a junk drawer. Marqly's boards let you group saves deliberately: a board for the woodworking series you're following, one for conference talks, one for the topic you're researching this month — videos and articles side by side, since boards hold any links plus highlights.

Boards can be shared as public pages, viewable without signup — handy when someone asks for "that list of videos you mentioned." And if your video research sessions end with a dozen YouTube tabs open, the tab saver [captures all open tabs at once](/faq/how-do-i-save-all-my-open-tabs).
