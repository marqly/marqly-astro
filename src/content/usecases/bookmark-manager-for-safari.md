Safari's bookmarks are like most things Apple: polished, quiet, and sealed inside the ecosystem. iCloud carries them between your Mac, iPhone, and iPad flawlessly — and drops them at the boundary of any non-Apple machine, which for many people means the computer they sit at eight hours a day. Marqly treats Safari as a first-class citizen — it's one of the four browsers with a Marqly extension, paired with a native iOS app — while storing your library in your account, so the article you save in Safari tonight opens in Edge at the office tomorrow.

## Safari bookmarks stop at the Apple wall

Inside the walled garden, Safari's system is fine: bookmarks and Reading List sync via iCloud with zero configuration. The trouble is what it can't do. There's no tagging, search matches names rather than content, and Reading List is a flat queue with no organization at all. Most limiting: none of it exists outside Apple hardware in any practical sense. A Windows work PC, a Chromebook, a borrowed laptop — your carefully kept Safari bookmarks might as well be on paper.

That's a structural limitation, not a settings problem, and it's why Safari users tend to run a second, sadder bookmark system at work — emailing themselves links, or keeping a "to sort" folder in a browser they don't otherwise use.

## The Safari extension plus the iOS app

Marqly's answer is a pairing. In Safari on your Mac, the extension does what it does in every supported browser: saves pages into your library, where AI auto-tagging labels every save without manual filing and AI summaries make backlogs skimmable. The highlighter marks passages in six colors with notes that persist on the page and sync to your library. Boards group links and highlights by project or topic — and can be shared as public pages that anyone can view, no account needed.

Capture is built to be effortless too: saving a page is one click, tags are optional because the AI applies them anyway, and the tab saver commits every open tab to your library in one action — the humane way to end a Safari window with nineteen tabs you were "getting to."

On your iPhone, the [Marqly iOS app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/marqly-ai-bookmark-manager/id6758905385) carries the same library natively — details in [our iOS FAQ](/faq/is-there-an-ios-app). The pairing matters because Safari usage is split across Mac and iPhone more than any other browser's; Marqly keeps both halves in one account rather than treating mobile as an afterthought.

Then there's retrieval, the part Safari never attempts: semantic search finds saves by meaning — "that piece about attention and note-taking" — matching across titles, content, highlights, and transcripts. Our guide to [searching bookmarks with AI](/blog/how-to-search-bookmarks-with-ai) shows the difference against a real backlog. On Pro, chat with your saves answers questions from your own saved content.

## Your library, outside Safari too

The account model is the escape hatch from iCloud's boundary. Everything you save [syncs across devices](/faq/can-i-sync-across-devices) — the same library appears in the Chrome, Edge, and Firefox extensions, in any browser through the web app at app.marqly.com, and in the iOS app. Save from Safari at home; retrieve from Edge at work; nothing is stranded.

Migration is one file: Safari exports bookmarks to standard HTML (File → Export → Bookmarks), and Marqly imports that format directly, then AI-tags the whole backlog — years of Safari folders become findable by description in one sitting. Pocket exports and Raindrop.io collections import as well, if your links are scattered further afield.
