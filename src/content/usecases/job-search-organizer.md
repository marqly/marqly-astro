A serious job search generates a research pile fast: two dozen postings across four job boards, each company's about page and engineering blog, salary threads, interview-experience posts, notes from coffee chats. The pile lives in 40 open tabs — until the browser crashes, or a posting you loved gets taken down before you finished the cover letter.

Marqly's job is to hold all of it: save postings before they vanish, turn company research into highlights and notes that stay attached to their sources, and surface any of it later by describing what you remember. The night before an interview, everything you've learned about the company is in one searchable place.

## Save postings before they disappear

Job postings are the most perishable pages on the internet — filled, pulled, or reposted with different requirements, often while you're mid-application. Save every posting you're considering to Marqly with one click. For the ones you're serious about, capture the page as a PDF: the capture matches the on-screen layout, images and all, processed locally in your browser. When the listing vanishes, you still hold the exact responsibilities, requirements, and compensation language you applied against — which matters again at negotiation time. The capture capability is covered in [how to save a webpage as a PDF](/blog/save-webpage-as-pdf).

Job-board sessions end with a wall of tabs; the [tab saver](/faq/how-do-i-save-all-my-open-tabs) closes that out in one click, sweeping every open posting and company page into your library at once. AI tags each save automatically, so nothing needs filing while you're in application mode.

## Research becomes prep notes, right on the page

The difference between generic research and interview-ready prep is specificity: the line in the engineering blog you want to ask about, the value on the careers page you can genuinely connect to, the red flag in a review thread worth probing.

Marqly's highlighter keeps that specificity attached to its source. Select the passage, highlight it — six colors, so you can run a simple code like green for talking points, red for concerns, blue for questions to ask — and add a note with your actual thought. Highlights persist on the page when you come back to it and sync to your library. The night before the interview, your prep isn't a loose doc of paraphrases; it's the original sources with your thinking layered on top.

The side-panel library keeps that research within reach while you work: writing a cover letter or filling an application, you open the panel next to the form and search your saves without switching contexts — the posting's exact language is right there while you're writing to it.

## One board per company

Group everything for each application into a [board](/faq/what-are-boards): the posting, the company pages, the salary threads, your highlighted prep. When Company A schedules the next round, you open Company A's board and the full picture is there — no re-searching, no "which tab was that in," no cross-contamination with the other five processes you're running.

## Find it by describing it, ask questions when it counts

Three weeks and forty saves into a search, memory gets fuzzy: which company had the four-day-week trial? Where was that thread about the take-home task? Marqly's semantic search answers from descriptions — "the startup with the unusual equity refresh policy" finds the save without the company name, searching across titles, page content, and your highlights. How that works is in [how to search bookmarks with AI](/blog/how-to-search-bookmarks-with-ai).

Two more AI layers help at crunch time. AI summaries triage long saves — a 3,000-word engineering post gets a summary, so you decide fast whether it's worth full prep attention. And on Pro, chat with your saves turns the pile into answers: ask "what do I know about this company's interview process?" and get a response drawn from your own saved research.

## Who this is not for

Marqly is not an application tracker. There are no pipeline columns, status fields, deadlines, or follow-up reminders — the Teal/Huntr-style kanban of "applied → phone screen → onsite" is a different tool, and if tracking state is your main pain, get one (or a spreadsheet) and let Marqly sit underneath it as the research layer. It also has no team features, so shared recruiting pipelines are out of scope.

The fit: you're running a real search across multiple companies, the research is the part drowning you, and you need every posting, thread, and prep note captured once and findable the night it matters. That's the job this does.
