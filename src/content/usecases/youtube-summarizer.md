The job: a 47-minute video might contain the exact answer you need — or three usable minutes padded with sponsor reads. You want to know which before you commit, and YouTube gives you nothing but a thumbnail and a title written to maximize clicks.

Marqly solves this where the problem actually happens: on the watch page. Install the extension and an AI card appears on every YouTube video with three tabs — an AI summary, the full transcript, and a chat that answers questions from the video. No copying URLs into a separate summarizer site, no tab juggling. Open video, read TL;DR, decide.

## The AI card lives on the watch page

Most YouTube summarizers make you do the work backwards: find the video, copy the link, open another site, paste, wait, read, go back. That friction means you only summarize videos you were already invested in — the opposite of triage.

Marqly's card is just *there*, on the watch page, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari. The **AI Summary tab** gives you:

- a **TL;DR that streams in** as it's generated — you're reading within seconds, not staring at a spinner;
- the video's **key sections**, so if only one segment matters, you jump straight to it;
- **books mentioned in the video** — quietly one of the most-used parts of the card, because interviews and podcasts are where reading recommendations fly by uncaptured.

The result is a real decision in under a minute: watch it all, watch one section, or close the tab informed instead of curious. The longer walkthrough is in [summarize YouTube videos with AI](/blog/summarize-youtube-videos-with-ai).

## The transcript tab: read the video instead

Sometimes a summary isn't enough and watching is too slow — you want the actual words. The **Transcript tab** puts the full transcript beside the player, **playback-synced**, so the text follows the video and clicking around the transcript is how you navigate. **One-click copy** grabs the whole thing when you need it in your notes or anywhere else.

Reading a transcript takes a fraction of the video's runtime — for dense technical content or interviews, it's usually the better format anyway. Fifteen minutes of reading versus an hour of watching is not a close call.

## The chat tab: ask the video a question

Third tab: **Chat** (a Pro feature). Ask a question — "what did she say about pricing?", "which study does he cite on sleep?" — and get an answer drawn from that video's transcript. It replaces the worst version of this job: scrubbing back and forth across a timeline hunting for a half-remembered moment.

Summary answers "is this worth watching?"; chat answers "what did they actually say about X?" — different questions, same card.

## Save it, and the transcript comes along

The card handles right-now; the bookmark button handles later. Marqly adds a **bookmark button to YouTube's own action row**, and saving a video attaches its **transcript** to the save in your library.

That upgrades everything downstream. Marqly's semantic search works across transcripts, so "the video where they compare index funds to real estate" finds the right save by what was *said*, not by its title. Your video library becomes searchable the way your articles are — and your watch-later queue becomes [something you can triage instead of a graveyard](/blog/save-youtube-videos-watch-later). AI tags are applied automatically on save, like everything else in Marqly, so there's no filing either.

Saved videos also work with boards: group the talks for a project alongside the articles and highlights that go with them, and share the board as a public page anyone can open without signing up — a topic's best videos and best writing on one link.
