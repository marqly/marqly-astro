Writing runs on borrowed material: the statistic that anchors the lede, the expert quote in paragraph four, the structural trick you noticed in someone else's essay and swore you'd try. The trouble is that writers collect this material while *reading*, and need it while *writing* — sometimes months apart. Between those two moments, the browser eats it. A quote you didn't capture precisely is a quote you can't use; a brilliant essay you can't relocate might as well not exist.

Marqly closes that gap. It's an AI bookmark manager where clipping takes one click, quotes get captured exactly with the highlighter, everything is tagged automatically, and retrieval works from the fuzzy description that's all you'll remember at drafting time.

## Quotes captured exactly, with your intent attached

Paraphrasing from memory is how misquotes happen. Marqly's [highlighter](/blog/how-to-highlight-text-on-any-website) captures passages verbatim from any web page — select, pick one of six colors, done — and every highlight carries its source with it. The notes field is where working writers get the real value: attach "possible opener for the burnout piece" or "she contradicts this in the second interview" while the thought exists. Highlights persist on the original page when you revisit and sync to your library, so your quote bank builds itself as a byproduct of reading.

A color convention keeps the bank sorted at a glance: quotes you'll use verbatim in one color, facts to verify in another, phrasing you admire in a third. None of this requires discipline beyond the selection itself.

Sources on the web are also less permanent than deadlines assume — pieces get paywalled, revised, or quietly deleted. For anything you may need to cite months out, save-as-PDF captures the page as a clean PDF matching its on-screen layout, lazy-loaded images included, processed locally in your browser. The article you built an argument on stays exactly as you read it.

## A swipe file that files itself

Every writer keeps some version of a swipe file — headlines that pulled you in, ledes that earned the second paragraph, endings that landed. Most swipe files die of filing overhead. In Marqly, AI auto-tagging labels every save the moment you make it ([how that works](/faq/how-does-ai-auto-tagging-work)), so the swipe file accumulates structure with zero maintenance: save the essay, keep reading, and "profiles," "essays," and "newsletters" emerge as tags on their own.

Boards then layer project structure on top — group links and highlights per assignment, per book chapter, or per standing beat. Research for the piece due Thursday stays separate from the slow-burn book material, and each board reads like a curated folder you never had to curate. The broader method here is the one described in [how to build a second brain](/blog/how-to-build-a-second-brain): capture without friction, organize automatically, retrieve on demand.

## Retrieval at the speed of drafting

Drafting has a rhythm, and leaving the document to spelunk through browser history breaks it. Marqly's semantic search takes the query you actually have — "the piece about grief where the writer kept returning to the kitchen table" — and finds the save, because it matches meaning across titles, page content, and your highlights rather than exact keywords. Mid-draft, the side-panel library means you can pull a source without leaving the tab you're writing in.

Two more tools speed up the research phase itself. AI summaries let you triage a stack of background reading before committing hours to it. And on Pro, chat with your saves answers questions from your own research — "what did my sources say about the 2019 policy change?" — which, at deadline, beats re-reading everything you clipped. Interviews and talks on YouTube slot in too: Marqly's card on every watch page includes a playback-synced transcript with one-click copy, and bookmarking the video attaches the transcript, so a subject's exact wording at minute 31 is quotable text, not a memory.

## Getting started

1. **Install the extension** for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari and sign up free at [app.marqly.com](https://app.marqly.com) — no card, and [the free tier](/faq/is-marqly-free) covers the core clipping workflow.
2. **Import your research backlog.** Standard browser bookmark exports, Pocket archives, and Raindrop.io collections all import directly, so the material from past pieces comes with you.
3. **Let auto-tagging sort the pile.** The AI tags the whole import and every future save. Your job reduces to the click and the highlight; the library handles the rest.

Where the fit ends: Marqly is not a drafting environment — you'll still write in your word processor or plain-text editor of choice — and it has no offline mode, so research reading on a signal-dead train needs a different tool. There's no Android app either, though the web app works in Android browsers. What it owns is everything between "I should keep this" and "where was that line?" — the stretch of the writing process where good material usually disappears.
