Ask Marqly a question in plain language and get an answer grounded in your saved content — the articles, highlights, and video transcripts already in your library. Instead of re-opening five saves to hunt down one figure or half-remembered argument, you ask for it and the answer is drawn from what you actually kept. Asking AI about your saves is a Marqly Pro feature.

## One question at a time

This isn't an open-ended conversation — it's one question in, one grounded answer out. Marqly doesn't keep a running chat history, so it won't remember an earlier question when you ask the next one; each question stands on its own and should include whatever context it needs. If you want to go deeper on a topic, ask a more specific follow-up question rather than expecting Marqly to recall what you asked before. (Chatting with a YouTube video's transcript is different — that one does hold a running conversation. See [how do I chat with a YouTube video](/faq/how-do-i-chat-with-a-youtube-video).)

## Answers come from your library

The point of asking AI about your saves is grounding: answers are based on the content you saved, not generic web knowledge. You curated the sources — the answer reflects *your* reading. That's what separates this from asking a general chatbot, which has never seen the specific articles you trusted enough to keep. As with any AI output, [verify critical facts against the source](/faq/how-accurate-are-ai-summaries) before you rely on them.

## Ask AI and search are different tools

[Semantic search](/faq/how-do-i-search-my-bookmarks-by-meaning) finds *the save* — "that article about pricing psychology." Asking AI answers *the question* — "what did that article say charm pricing does to perceived quality?" Use search when you want to open and read something; use Ask AI when you want the specific answer buried inside things you've already read. Together they make the library a working reference instead of a pile.
