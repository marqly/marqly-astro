A read-it-later app saves articles and videos you find during the day so you can get to them when you actually have time. Instead of leaving forty tabs open or emailing yourself links, you save with one click and the app keeps a queue. Pocket defined the category for over a decade; its shutdown in July 2025 pushed millions of readers to find a replacement and reshaped what these apps are expected to do.

## The core loop

Save, queue, read, archive. The loop works because saving is instant — you don't break focus to deal with an interesting link, you just capture it. The classic failure mode is equally well known: the queue grows faster than you read, and the app becomes a guilt pile of 800 unread articles you're afraid to open.

## The category after Pocket

Pocket's shutdown did more than orphan its users — it reset expectations. A plain reading queue is no longer enough; the tools competing for former Pocket users differentiate on triage (can the app tell you what's in the backlog?) and retrieval (can you find that article from March without remembering its title?).
