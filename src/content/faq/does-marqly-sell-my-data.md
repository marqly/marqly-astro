No. Marqly does not sell your data — not your bookmarks, not your highlights, not your reading habits. The business model makes this simple to verify: revenue comes from Pro subscriptions at $48/yr or $8/mo, not from advertising or data brokers. When users pay for the product directly, there's no economic reason to monetize their data on the side. The [privacy policy](/privacypolicy) is the binding version of this commitment.

## Follow the money

The pattern with "free" tools is old and well-documented: if the product costs nothing and there's no paid tier funding it, your attention and data usually are the product. Marqly's free tier is funded the boring way — by paying Pro subscribers who want chat with their saves and YouTube video chat. That's the entire revenue model. There are no ads in the product, so there's no ad-targeting machinery that would even want your browsing profile.

## Why this matters for a bookmark manager

A bookmark manager sees an unusually honest picture of you: what you research, what you read, what you're curious about at 1 a.m. That's exactly the kind of data ad networks pay for, which is why the funding model of the tool you pick matters more here than for, say, a calculator app. Choosing a subscription-funded tool is the structural fix, not a promise you have to take on faith.

## Your controls

You're not locked into trusting this page:

- Read the [privacy policy](/privacypolicy) — it's the document that legally governs data handling.
- [Export your data](/faq/can-i-export-my-data) whenever you want a copy.
- [Delete your account](/faq/how-do-i-delete-my-account) and its data entirely if you leave.

Questions the policy doesn't answer? Email **support@marqly.com** and ask directly.
