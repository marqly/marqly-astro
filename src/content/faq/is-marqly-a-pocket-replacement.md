Yes, for the core Pocket workflow: save an article with one click, find it later, and work through your backlog. Marqly adds what Pocket never had — AI auto-tagging, article summaries, and semantic search — and it imports Pocket export files directly, so your archive comes with you. One honest caveat: Marqly has no offline reading mode, so if downloaded articles for flights were your main Pocket habit, it isn't a one-to-one match.

## What carries over from Pocket

The save-for-later loop survives intact. The browser extension (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) saves the current page in one click, your library lives in the web app, and there's an iOS app for saving and reading on the phone. Your existing archive isn't stranded either — Marqly [imports the export file Pocket gave you](/faq/how-do-i-import-from-pocket).

## What Marqly adds

Pocket stored articles; finding them again was on you. Marqly does the organizing itself: every save is tagged automatically by AI, saved articles get AI summaries so you can triage a backlog fast, and semantic search finds saves by meaning — "that article about burnout from a founder blog" works even when the title doesn't contain any of those words. Search runs across titles, content, highlights, and video transcripts.

## The honest gap: offline reading

Pocket downloaded articles to your device; Marqly doesn't. There is [no offline reading mode](/faq/does-marqly-work-offline), so reading requires a connection. For most people this is a footnote; for plane-and-subway readers it's the deciding factor, and you should weigh it before switching.
