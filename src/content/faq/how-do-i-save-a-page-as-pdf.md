Use Marqly's save-as-PDF to capture the page you're on as a clean PDF that matches the on-screen layout. It includes lazy-loaded images — the ones browser print-to-PDF usually drops because they never loaded — and the processing happens locally in your browser, so the page's content isn't shipped off to a server to be rendered.

## What "clean" means here

The browser's built-in print-to-PDF was designed for printing, and it shows: broken multi-column layouts, clipped elements, and image gaps wherever the page lazy-loads content as you scroll. Marqly's capture produces a PDF that matches what you actually see on screen, with lazy-loaded images included — the document reads like the page did.

## Processed locally

The PDF is generated in your browser, not on Marqly's servers. That matters when the page is sensitive — an invoice, an order confirmation, an internal doc — because capturing it doesn't mean transmitting it for rendering. For the broader picture on how Marqly handles your data, see [is Marqly safe](/faq/is-marqly-safe).

## When a PDF beats a bookmark

A bookmark points at a page; a PDF preserves it. Reach for PDF when:

- the page might change or disappear — pricing pages, terms, listings, news that gets edited;
- you need a record of what the page said *at this moment* — receipts, confirmations, published claims;
- you want a fixed document you can file or forward.

For everything you simply want to read again or reference, a normal Marqly save is lighter — and [highlighting the key passages](/faq/how-do-i-highlight-text-on-a-website) keeps the parts that matter.
