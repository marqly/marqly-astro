Yes. Your data is portable — email **support@marqly.com** from your account's email address and request a full export of your saves. Data portability is a baseline commitment: the bookmarks, highlights, and notes you've collected are yours, and being able to take them with you is part of what makes trusting a tool with years of saves reasonable in the first place.

## How to request an export

Send an email to **support@marqly.com** with:

- The email address tied to your Marqly account
- A note that you want a full export of your data

Support will get your export to you. Requesting one doesn't change anything about your account — it's a copy, not a withdrawal, and you can keep using Marqly exactly as before.

## Why lock-in is the thing to check before you commit

The real cost of a bookmark manager isn't the subscription — it's the years of curation you'd lose if you couldn't leave. Marqly is built on the other side of that trade: it imports from Pocket, Raindrop.io, and standard browser bookmark exports precisely because people deserve to move *in* freely, and the same logic applies on the way out. If you've been through a forced migration before — the Pocket shutdown left millions scrambling, as covered in [how to export and migrate your Pocket data](/blog/how-to-export-migrate-pocket-data) — you already know why this matters.

## Common reasons to export

- **Peace of mind.** A periodic backup of a library you've spent years building.
- **Downgrading or leaving.** Note that [canceling Pro doesn't delete anything](/faq/what-happens-to-my-bookmarks-if-i-cancel) — your saves stay accessible on the free tier — so exporting is optional, not urgent.
- **Closing your account.** If you're deleting your account, export *first*, because [deletion removes your saves](/faq/how-do-i-delete-my-account) permanently.

For how your data is handled while it's in Marqly, see the [privacy policy](/privacypolicy).
