Browser bookmarks store a title and a URL in folders you maintain by hand, locked inside one browser's sync system. Marqly saves the page, tags it automatically with AI, summarizes articles, and lets you search by meaning — "that piece about pricing psychology" — across everything you've saved. And because the library lives in a web app with extensions for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari plus an iOS app, it isn't tied to any single browser.

## No folders to maintain

Browser bookmarks make organization your job, and the job never ends: pick a folder at save time, prune dead links, restructure when the taxonomy stops fitting. Marqly removes the filing step — AI assigns tags to every save automatically, so saving is one click and the structure builds itself. [Why folder-based bookmark organizing is obsolete](/blog/stop-organizing-bookmarks-folders-obsolete) covers how folders fail as a library grows.

## Search that works on memory, not titles

Your browser's bookmark search matches literal text in titles and URLs. That fails exactly when you need it: the title was clickbait, or "Untitled Document," or you simply remember the idea rather than the words. Marqly's [semantic search](/faq/what-is-semantic-search) matches meaning across titles, page content, your highlights, and video transcripts, so a half-remembered description is enough to surface the save.

## More than a link

A browser bookmark is a pointer. A Marqly save can carry more: AI summaries of articles for fast triage, highlights in six colors with notes attached that persist on the page when you revisit, and boards that group related links and highlights — shareable as a public page if you want.

## Your existing bookmarks come along

Switching doesn't mean starting over. Every major browser exports bookmarks as a standard HTML file, and Marqly imports it — see [how to import Chrome bookmarks](/faq/how-do-i-import-chrome-bookmarks). Once imported, the old pile gets the same treatment as new saves: auto-tags and meaning-based search instead of folders you no longer remember building.
