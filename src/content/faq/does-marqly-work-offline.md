No. Marqly needs an internet connection — there is no offline reading mode. Your bookmarks, highlights, and summaries live in your Marqly account, so saving a page, running a search, or opening your library all happen online. The trade-off works in your favor the rest of the time: everything you save is accessible from any signed-in device — the browser extension, the web app, or the iOS app.

## What needs a connection

Every core Marqly feature talks to your account: one-click saving from the toolbar, AI auto-tagging, AI summaries, semantic search, and chatting with your saves. If your connection drops, you won't be able to pull up a saved article or search your library until you're back online.

We'd rather say that plainly than let you find out on a plane. If guaranteed offline reading is your top requirement, Marqly isn't built for that today — and we don't claim otherwise.

## What you get instead

Because your library lives in your account rather than on one device, nothing is trapped in a single browser profile. Save an article from Chrome at your desk, and it's already in the [web app](https://app.marqly.com) and on your iPhone. There's no sync button to press and no local database to back up. See [can I sync across devices](/faq/can-i-sync-across-devices) for how that works across the extension, web app, and iOS app.

## If you need a permanent local copy

For pages you want to keep regardless of connectivity, use Marqly's save-as-PDF feature: it captures any page as a clean PDF that matches the on-screen layout, including lazy-loaded images, and the capture is processed locally in your browser. The resulting PDF is a regular file — store it wherever you like and open it with no connection at all. Details in [how do I save a page as PDF](/faq/how-do-i-save-a-page-as-pdf).

## The short version

Marqly is an online service by design: your saves, tags, highlights, and summaries follow your account everywhere, but you need a connection to reach them. For offline keepsakes, save the page as a PDF.
