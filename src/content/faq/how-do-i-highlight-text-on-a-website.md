Select the text you want to keep on any website, then highlight it with Marqly. You pick from six colors and can attach a note for context. The highlight persists on the page — it's still there when you come back — and it also syncs to your Marqly library, so the passage is saved even if you never revisit the page.

## Six colors, your system

Six colors are enough to build a simple code without turning highlighting into a chore: one color for key claims, one for numbers and evidence, one for things you disagree with, one for follow-ups. Whatever scheme you pick, it works the same on every site, because Marqly highlights work on any website — not just inside one reading app.

## Highlights that don't disappear

The usual problem with marking up the web is that nothing sticks: you close the tab and your careful selection is gone. Marqly highlights [stay on the page when you revisit](/faq/do-highlights-stay-on-the-page) and sync to your library, so each one exists in two useful places — in context on the original page, and in your library where it's searchable alongside everything else you've saved.

## Add your thinking, then organize

A highlight marks *what* mattered; [a note attached to it](/faq/how-do-i-add-notes-to-highlights) records *why* — which is the part you'll have forgotten in six months. When highlights start piling up around a topic, group them with related links into a [board](/faq/what-are-boards) so the research for one project lives in one place.
