Yes. Marqly generates AI summaries of the articles you save, so you can see what a piece actually says before committing to a full read. That changes how a reading backlog works: save liberally during the day, then scan summaries later and decide what deserves your time. YouTube videos get the same treatment through an AI Summary tab with a TL;DR, key sections, and books mentioned in the video.

## Summaries are for triage

A read-it-later queue fails when the only way to assess an article is to read it — the backlog compounds and eventually you declare bankruptcy. Summaries break that cycle. Twenty saved articles become twenty short digests you can scan in a few minutes, sorting the must-reads from the skims from the deletes. It's the difference between a library you use and [a queue you feel guilty about](/blog/best-read-it-later-apps-2026).

## YouTube videos too

On every YouTube watch page, Marqly's extension adds an AI card. The Summary tab streams a TL;DR with key sections and any books mentioned in the video — often enough to decide whether a 40-minute video is worth 40 minutes. There's also a playback-synced transcript tab with one-click copy. Details in [how to summarize a YouTube video](/faq/how-do-i-summarize-a-youtube-video).

## Beyond summaries: ask questions

A summary tells you what's in a save; sometimes you want a specific answer out of it. On the Pro plan you can [chat with your saved content](/faq/how-do-i-chat-with-my-saved-articles) — ask a question and get an answer drawn from the articles and videos you've saved, instead of re-reading them yourself.

## How much should you trust them

Summaries are AI-generated, which means good but not infallible — see [how accurate are AI summaries](/faq/how-accurate-are-ai-summaries) for the honest version. The workflow that works: trust summaries for triage decisions, read the source for anything you're going to quote or act on.
