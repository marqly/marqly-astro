One of these is a reading service; the other is a capture tool for a vault you control. Matter manages the whole consumption pipeline on Apple devices — articles parsed beautifully, newsletters delivered via Gmail or a dedicated address, YouTube and podcasts transcribed with time-synced text, everything listenable in narration that sets the category standard. Obsidian Web Clipper does one job with unusual craft: it turns pages, selections, or highlighted passages into clean Markdown files on your own disk, auto-titled and tagged by per-site templates, with an optional Interpreter running clip-time AI prompts through your own key or a free local Ollama model.

**Choose Matter if:**

- Reading and listening are the point, and iPhone, iPad, or Mac is where it happens
- You want a maintained queue with progress, offline reading, and zero configuration
- Newsletters and transcribed audio belong in the same inbox as your articles

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Your knowledge base is an Obsidian vault and clips should arrive as portable, private Markdown
- Free and local-first matter more than polish — no subscription, no cloud, no lock-in
- Template-driven automation at capture appeals to the tinkerer in you

Bottom line: the integration removes most of the tension. Matter's Premium tier syncs highlights straight into Obsidian, so vault-keepers can read in comfort and still own the residue. What remains is a clean split: Matter has no Android app, no API, and holds your library in its cloud; the clipper has no queue, no audio, and no sync without paying for Obsidian Sync. Comfort in Matter, custody in Obsidian — or the popular answer, both.
