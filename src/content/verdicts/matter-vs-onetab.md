OneTab and Matter meet at the same painful moment — forty open tabs — and take it in opposite directions. OneTab's answer is subtraction: one click and every tab collapses into a plain list, reclaiming up to 95% of browser memory by the developer's estimate, all local, all free, no account anywhere in sight. The 2026 v2 rewrite added folders, search, stars, and dark mode to what had been a deliberately static tool. Matter's answer is conversion: the articles among those tabs become a proper reading queue on iPhone, iPad, Mac, and web, parsed for typography, available offline, narrated by the category's best text-to-speech, and joined by newsletters via Gmail and transcribed YouTube videos and podcasts.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You need instant, free tab relief on desktop with nothing to configure
- Your parked tabs are references and maybes, not committed reading
- Local-only storage is acceptable — or preferable — for what you save

**Choose Matter if:**

- The tabs you keep open are articles you actually intend to finish
- You read on your phone or listen on the move, so desktop-only storage is useless
- Highlights, notes, and their export to Notion or Obsidian matter downstream

Bottom line: respect what each refuses to do. OneTab will never save page content, sync across devices, or exist on your phone — and it warns you that uninstalling deletes everything. Matter will never manage your browser sessions or park a hundred miscellaneous tabs. The winning setup for tab-hoarding readers is boring and obvious: OneTab as the pressure valve, Matter as the library.
