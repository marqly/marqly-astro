Pocket's death broke its users' libraries twice: the service disappeared, and every page that had since gone offline disappeared with it, because Pocket stored pointers, not pages. Linkwarden is built on the opposite premise. Every link you save is automatically preserved as a screenshot, a PDF, and a readable text copy — and can be pushed to the Wayback Machine — so your library outlives both the original sites and, if you self-host, the company itself.

**Choose Pocket if**

- There's nothing to choose — it has been gone since July 2025, with all unexported data erased that October.
- Its role in this comparison is cautionary, not competitive.

**Choose Linkwarden if**

- Link rot is your nightmare scenario: full-page archiving on every save is the core feature, not a paid add-on.
- You want ownership with a hosted fallback — self-host the AGPL code for free with every feature included, or pay $3/month per user (billed yearly) for Cloud after a 14-day trial.
- You work with others: collaborative collections with per-user permissions and public sharing are rare in this category.

The mismatch to understand before switching: Linkwarden is a preservation and research tool, not a reading app. There's no reader queue, no text-to-speech, no reading progress — the things that made Pocket pleasant on a commute simply aren't the job here. Its iOS and Android apps only arrived in late 2025 and still trail the web experience. Former Pocket users who mostly read should pair their export with Instapaper or Matter instead; those who mostly collected research and references they cannot afford to lose will find Linkwarden strictly better than Pocket ever was — it imports Pocket files directly, full-text searches the archived copies rather than just titles, and offers optional AI tagging that can even run on local models.
