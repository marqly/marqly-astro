Both are beautiful, opinionated, personal products — and nearly non-overlapping. Matter assumes your saves are things to get through: an article queue with superb parsing, newsletters delivered via Gmail or a dedicated address, YouTube and podcasts transcribed with synced text, and narration strong enough to replace reading entirely. mymind assumes your saves are things to keep around: screenshots, products, quotes, and links become cards on a single canvas with no folders, where AI recognizes what's inside images and semantic search retrieves them from a half-remembered description. One is a pipeline; the other is a pool.

**Choose Matter if:**

- Text is your material — articles, newsletters, essays — and audio is how you get through half of it
- You highlight while reading and want those highlights in Notion, Obsidian, or Readwise
- An unlimited free library beats a 100-card cap

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save visually: moodboards, design references, products, screenshots
- Zero-filing appeals — AI tags everything, there are no folders, and search works by meaning
- Privacy is a feature you'd pay for: no ads, no tracking, no social layer, membership-funded

Bottom line: the sharp edges matter as much as the strengths. mymind refuses bulk import and sharing on principle, and its export is a one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge. Matter has no Android app, no API, and no AI organization at all. If your saved life is mostly words, choose Matter. Mostly images and sparks, mymind. There's little middle ground to argue over.
