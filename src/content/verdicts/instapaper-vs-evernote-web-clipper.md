The verb decides this one: read or archive. Instapaper takes a page, discards everything but the words, and queues it for a quiet moment — offline on your phone, in the category's best typography, since 2008. Evernote Web Clipper takes the same page and files it: full layout or simplified article, screenshot with annotations if you like, tagged into a notebook where OCR, document search, and an AI assistant will excavate it years later.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- The goal is working through a reading list, not building an institutional memory — friction-free saving and offline comfort win here
- Money matters: unlimited free saving versus Evernote's 50-note, single-device free plan, and $59.99/year versus up to $249.99
- Kindle delivery, speed reading, and text-to-speech playlists suit how you actually consume articles

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Clipped pages are reference material — research, receipts, documentation — that belongs beside notes, PDFs, and meeting records
- Capture flexibility earns its keep: five browsers, full-page or selection clips, annotated screenshots, tags applied at save time
- You will use the platform's depth — AI chat across your notes, semantic search, offline notebooks — enough to justify its price

Bottom line: Instapaper is a scalpel and Evernote is a warehouse with a forklift. For the common case — someone saving articles they mean to read this month — the warehouse is expensive overkill, and Instapaper's free tier settles the question. But no amount of reading polish turns a queue into an archive: if your clips are evidence rather than entertainment, Evernote's retrieval machinery is what you are really paying for.
