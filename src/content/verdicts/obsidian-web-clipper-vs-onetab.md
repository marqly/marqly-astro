Rare in a comparison: these two share values and split cleanly on function. Both cost nothing, keep data local, and treat privacy as non-negotiable — OneTab never phones home; Obsidian writes files to your own disk. The split is what happens to a page you act on. OneTab remembers that a tab existed, storing its URL and title in a list. Obsidian Web Clipper keeps what the page said, converting it — or just the passages you highlighted — into Markdown inside your vault.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- Volume is the enemy: dozens of tabs need parking now, and clipping each one into a note system would be absurd overhead.
- You want a tool with no dependencies — no companion app required, whereas Obsidian's clipper is only useful if you run Obsidian itself.
- Restoration is the use case: parked sessions reopen individually or wholesale, a workflow a notes vault cannot replicate.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Content deserves to outlive its tab — articles, documentation, and reference pages saved as durable, searchable Markdown.
- You annotate as you read: highlighter mode captures exactly the passages you marked, with per-site templates handling titles and tags.
- Your phone is part of the pipeline — the Safari extension on iOS clips to your vault, while OneTab has no mobile presence at all.

Bottom line: this odd couple is best resolved with "and," not "or." Let OneTab absorb the daily tab flood, accepting that its list is disposable by design. Route the few pages carrying real information through Obsidian's clipper into files you own permanently. Since both are free, the only cost of running both is learning which click each moment deserves.
