Toby and Raindrop.io both catch what falls out of your browser, but they serve different rhythms of work. Toby lives in the new-tab page and thinks in sessions: save this working set, restore it tomorrow, share the space with teammates. Raindrop thinks in permanence: file the link, tag it, find it years later. Toby's team features — shared spaces, invites, org-wide collections, SSO — have no real counterpart in Raindrop, while Raindrop's depth as a library embarrasses Toby's link-only collections.

**Choose Raindrop.io if:**

- You are one person with many bookmarks — unlimited free saves against Toby's 60-tab ceiling settles it before pricing even enters
- Longevity matters: Pro's permanent page copies and full-text search protect against link rot, which Toby's bare links cannot survive
- You want Safari support and a real API; Toby offers neither

**Choose Toby if:**

- A team shares reference links — collaborative spaces with member management and no-account-needed public links are Toby's genuine moat
- Your workflow is session-based: open a project's tabs, work, stash them, restore next time, with Toby AI grouping strays automatically
- The new-tab page as home base appeals more than a separate app you have to visit

Bottom line: for personal bookmarking, Raindrop is better and cheaper at every tier, and the free-plan gap is enormous. Toby's per-member pricing and 60-tab cap make it an awkward solo tool — its 4.2-star Chrome rating traces back to exactly that freemium squeeze — but for a team working out of shared tab collections, nothing in Raindrop replicates what Toby does. Decide based on headcount.
