If Pocket's discontinuation in July 2025 taught one lesson, it's the one Obsidian Web Clipper is built on: content you rent can vanish; files you own can't. The official Obsidian extension saves pages, selections, or highlighted passages as clean Markdown in a vault on your own disk — readable by any text editor, importable anywhere, impossible to shut down. Pocket's servers took sixteen years of libraries with them; a folder of Markdown files has no servers to lose.

**Choose Pocket if**

- That door closed in 2025 — first the service in July, then the data in October.
- Its absence is precisely the argument for local-first tools.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if**

- You want permanent ownership: every clip is plain Markdown you keep forever, with zero lock-in and zero required subscription — free even for commercial use.
- You'll exploit the automation: per-site templates auto-title, tag, and file clips from page metadata, and highlighter mode captures just the passages you mark rather than whole pages.
- You want clip-time AI on your terms — the Interpreter can summarize, extract, or translate using your own API key or a free local Ollama model.

The trade-offs mirror the philosophy. Nothing is automatic across devices: sync means Obsidian Sync at $4/month or a DIY setup, there's no web app, and organizing and finding clips is manual — no auto-tagging, no semantic search. It's also only useful if you actually run Obsidian. Note the migration gap too: there's no bulk import, so a rescued Pocket export belongs in Wallabag, Raindrop.io, or Instapaper instead. For the local-first reader who wants tomorrow's saves to be genuinely theirs, though, this is the strongest free answer the Pocket shutdown ever received.
