These two tools answer different questions. Readwise Reader asks "how do you get through everything you meant to read?" — it folds articles, RSS feeds, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, and YouTube videos into one triage inbox, layers Ghostreader AI on top for summaries and in-text questions, and pipes your highlights into Notion, Obsidian, and Roam. Linkwarden asks "how do you make sure a saved page still exists in five years?" — an open-source (AGPL) bookmark manager that archives every link as a screenshot, PDF, and readable copy, organizes them into nested collections you can share publicly or edit with teammates, and runs free on your own server or for $3/month per user in the cloud.

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- You read at volume across formats — newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, YouTube transcripts — and want it all in one keyboard-driven queue
- Highlights matter: daily review plus exports to Notion, Obsidian, and Roam is the best workflow in the category
- You'll pay $119.88/year for Ghostreader summaries and active, well-funded development

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- Preservation is the point: screenshot, PDF, and text copies mean link rot can't touch your library
- You want open-source ownership — self-hosting is free with full feature parity, including optional AI tagging via local models
- You collaborate: shared collections with permissions and public links, which Reader doesn't offer at all

The bottom line: price and philosophy separate them more than quality. Reader has no free tier and costs roughly eighty dollars more per year than Linkwarden Cloud — money well spent if its reading workflow becomes a daily habit, and wasted if you mostly file links away. Linkwarden's new mobile apps still trail its web experience, but as a permanent, searchable archive it does something Reader never attempts.
