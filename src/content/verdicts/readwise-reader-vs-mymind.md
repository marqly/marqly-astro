Readwise Reader and mymind barely compete — they serve opposite halves of the saving brain. Reader is engineered for textual throughput: a keyboard-first inbox that swallows articles, newsletters, RSS, PDFs, ePubs, and YouTube transcripts, with Ghostreader AI answering questions against the text and every highlight flowing to Notion, Obsidian, and Roam. mymind is engineered for visual memory: everything lands as a card on one canvas, there are no folders, and AI does all the filing — recognizing objects, colors, brands, and even text inside images so you can retrieve a save by describing what you remember.

Their philosophies clash too. Reader is a productivity machine with a real learning curve; mymind is deliberately calm, private, and single-player — no sharing, no collaboration, no social features, and by design no bulk import from Pocket, Raindrop, or your browser.

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- Your saves are things to read, and your reading generates highlights and notes you want in other tools
- YouTube transcripts, ePub support, and a documented API earn their keep in your workflow
- You want spaced daily review of what you've marked

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save more screenshots, products, and inspiration than articles, and searching for a half-remembered image should just work
- Zero-filing appeals: semantic search plus AI tagging instead of manual organization
- Privacy and aesthetics beat features — no ads, no tracking, membership-funded

Bottom line: at the top tiers they cost about the same ($119.88/year for Reader, $129/year for mymind's Mastermind), so choose by material. Text pipelines belong in Reader. Visual libraries belong in mymind — just know its export is a one-way CSV, and there's no path to bring an existing collection in.
