These are the two most design-conscious apps in read-it-later, separated by which sense they serve. Instapaper, saving articles since 2008, is built around the eye: the cleanest typography in the category, tilt scrolling, speed reading, dependable offline sync. Matter is built increasingly around the ear — its narration-quality voices turn any article, newsletter, or transcribed YouTube video into listenable audio, with the text tracking word by word so you can switch between modes mid-article.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- You read on Android or Kindle — Matter supports neither, while Instapaper covers both plus iOS and the web
- Longevity reassures you: eighteen years of continuous operation, an independent owner, and a documented developer API
- Silent, focused reading is the point, and Premium extras like the PDF reader and permanent archive round out the library

**Choose Matter if:**

- Commutes, workouts, or chores are your reading time — HD voices with word tracking make audio the primary mode, not an afterthought
- Newsletters matter: Gmail integration or a dedicated Matter address flows them straight into your queue
- You annotate on iPad; Apple Pencil highlighting and highlight sync to Notion, Obsidian, and Readwise fit a notes workflow

Bottom line: at essentially the same annual price ($59.99 versus $60 on the web — avoid Matter's $79.99 App Store rate), this comes down to platform and mode. Apple-only listeners should take Matter without hesitation. Everyone else should weigh Matter's missing Android app and absent public API against Instapaper's occasionally dated feel. Neither offers AI organization or semantic search; both bet that a well-made queue beats a clever one.
