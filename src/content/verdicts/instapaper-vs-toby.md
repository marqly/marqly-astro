Reading queue meets team workspace. Instapaper, the original read-it-later service, captures article text for calm offline reading on iPhone, Android, Kindle, and the web — a private, deliberately minimal pipeline from "interesting" to "read." Toby lives where work happens: the new-tab page, where open tabs organize into visual collections, whole sessions stash and restore in a click, and spaces are shared across a team, with Toby AI grouping strays automatically.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- The saves are articles and the goal is finishing them — typography, highlights, offline sync, and speed reading serve exactly that
- You are one person; Toby's per-member pricing and 60-tab free cap make solo use expensive for what you get
- Content should outlive its URL — Premium's permanent archive keeps article text even after pages change or vanish

**Choose Toby if:**

- A team runs on shared link collections — onboarding docs, client resources, project tabs — and needs invites, permissions, and SSO
- Your browser is the workspace and sessions are the unit of work: save today's fifteen tabs, restore them tomorrow exactly as they were
- Public sharing without recipient accounts matters; any Toby collection becomes a viewable link

Bottom line: confusion between these two costs real money. Toby as a personal article stash hits its 60-tab wall within weeks and never lets you read offline or search the text; Instapaper as a team resource hub simply cannot share anything. The tell is the pronoun — if the saves are "mine, to read," take Instapaper's free tier; if they are "ours, to work from," Toby is the only one of the pair that even plays the game.
