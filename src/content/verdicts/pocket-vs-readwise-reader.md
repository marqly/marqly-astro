Pocket was casual by design — a pleasant, free place to stash articles and read a few on the weekend. Readwise Reader is a professional reading pipeline: a triage inbox that swallows articles, RSS feeds, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, Twitter threads, and YouTube videos, then feeds every highlight into Notion, Obsidian, or Roam with spaced-repetition review on top. Pocket stopped working in July 2025, so the only question left is whether Reader's ambition fits the way you actually read.

**Choose Pocket if**

- Impossible — Mozilla discontinued it, the export window ended October 8, 2025, and remaining data was wiped.
- No fork, mirror, or archive of the service exists.

**Choose Readwise Reader if**

- You read in volume and want one keyboard-first inbox for every content type, not just saved web articles.
- You'll use the AI: Ghostreader summarizes, defines terms, and answers questions against the text, and YouTube videos arrive with synced transcripts you can highlight like prose.
- Your highlights need to go somewhere — the Readwise loop into Notion and Obsidian is the best in the business.

The catch is money and weight. Reader has no free tier at all: after a 30-day trial it's $9.99/month billed annually ($119.88/year) or $12.99 monthly, and the interface is dense enough that casual readers routinely bounce off it. A Pocket user who saved five articles a week will find that both overpriced and overbuilt — Instapaper's free tier is a gentler landing. But a Pocket power user who always wished for more gets dramatically more here than Pocket ever offered. Migration is painless if you exported before the 2025 deadline: Reader ingests Pocket files directly, and its own exit doors — continuous exports and a documented API — mean you'll never face a Pocket-style data lockout again.
