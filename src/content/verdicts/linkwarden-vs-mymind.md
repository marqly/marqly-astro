One of these tools wants to be infrastructure; the other wants to be a sanctuary. Linkwarden is AGPL-licensed software you can run yourself, built to preserve: every bookmark becomes a screenshot, a PDF, and a readable copy, filed into nested collections you can share publicly or edit with teammates. mymind is a membership-funded, deliberately closed canvas: everything you save becomes a card, AI tags it and reads the objects, colors, brands, and text inside images, and nothing — by design — can ever be shared with anyone.

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- Your library is an asset: archived copies outlive dead pages, and exports (HTML, JSON, API) mean you're never trapped.
- Others need in — collaborative collections with per-user permissions, or public links.
- You're migrating: it imports from Pocket and browser bookmark HTML, and costs $3/month hosted or nothing self-hosted.

**Choose mymind if:**

- You think in images: finding that screenshot with the blue gradient by describing it is the core magic.
- Filing anything manually feels like failure — no folders exist here, and AI handles everything.
- Privacy is emotional, not just technical: no ads, no tracking, no social layer, nothing performative.

Each tool's limits are the other's strengths. Linkwarden has no semantic search, no summaries, and no reading experience to speak of — it's a filing-and-preservation system with keyword search. mymind's constraints are chosen and firm: no bulk import from anywhere, no sharing of any kind, a 100-card free cap, AI gated at $7.99/month and summaries at $12.99/month, and export limited to a one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge. A researcher building a permanent, portable reference library should take Linkwarden and never look back. A designer feeding a private inspiration stream will find Linkwarden joyless and mymind worth every dollar.
