mymind and Evernote Web Clipper disagree about what saving is for. In mymind, saving is remembering: every bookmark, image, and quote becomes a card on one private visual canvas, AI tags it automatically, and folders simply do not exist. In Evernote, saving is organizing: the clipper captures pages, articles, or annotated screenshots with tags and a destination notebook chosen up front, feeding a forty-year-old idea of the filing cabinet executed with real depth — OCR, PDF search, and now an AI assistant.

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save inspiration more than information — images, products, and ideas that its AI indexes by objects, colors, and brands.
- Filing feels like friction: the zero-organization philosophy means you never touch a notebook, folder, or tag by hand.
- Privacy is a dealbreaker — no ads, no tracking, no social features, and a business funded only by memberships.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Your archive is built from articles, PDFs, and screenshots you need to search inside, not just look at.
- Clip-time control matters — five browsers, multiple capture modes, and tags, remarks, and notebooks assigned before you save.
- You want to interrogate your archive: the v11 assistant chats across everything you have clipped, something mymind explicitly lacks.

Bottom line: price and philosophy travel together here. Evernote asks up to $249.99/year and rewards it with the deepest clip-and-search stack in the category — overkill if you mainly save links and pictures. mymind tops out at $12.99/month, but locks summaries to that tier, refuses bulk imports, and shares nothing with anyone. Buy Evernote as a research engine; buy mymind as a second memory.
