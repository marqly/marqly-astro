Pocket went dark on July 8, 2025, so Evernote's clipper wins this matchup by simply existing — but the two were never the same species. Pocket saved articles to read later; Evernote Web Clipper captures pages into a full note platform, where they sit beside meeting notes and PDFs under genuinely deep search: OCR reads text inside images, paid plans search inside documents, and the v11 release added an AI assistant that chats across your notes plus semantic search.

**Choose Pocket if**

- The option expired in 2025 — service dead in July, data deleted after the October export cutoff.
- Not even its browser extensions remain installable.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if**

- You already live in Evernote: clipping full pages, simplified articles, selections, or annotated screenshots into notebooks — with tags and remarks at capture time — is the richest clip-time workflow of any major clipper.
- Your saves are research, not reading: contracts, receipts, reference pages, and screenshots that must be findable years later benefit from Evernote's search depth.
- You clip from everywhere — it's the only major clipper covering Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, and Opera.

The costs are the story. Evernote's free plan is effectively a demo — 50 notes, one notebook, one device — so sustained clipping means Starter at $14.99/month ($99/year) or Advanced at $249.99/year, with prices repeatedly raised under owner Bending Spoons. And there's no Pocket importer: the archive you rescued in 2025 should live somewhere with a dedicated import, like Raindrop.io or Instapaper. The practical read: former Pocket users who just want to save and revisit links will find Evernote heavy and expensive for the job; people who need a searchable filing cabinet for everything they encounter will find it earns its keep.
