Raindrop.io and Matter optimize for opposite ends of the save-to-consume funnel. Raindrop is the archive: polished, cross-platform, built to organize thousands of links into collections you'll browse for years. Matter is the moment of consumption: a lovingly designed Apple reading app whose human-like text-to-speech — with word-by-word tracking — is the finest listening experience in the category. One fact cuts through everything: Matter runs only on iPhone, iPad, Mac, and the web. There is no Android app.

**Choose Raindrop.io if**

- You need your library everywhere — native apps on Android and Mac plus every major browser extension, against Matter's Apple-plus-web footprint.
- The library is the point: visual collections, public sharing, an open API, and HTML/CSV export on the free plan make it the safer home for a permanent archive.
- You want maximum value: an effectively unlimited free plan, with Pro at $28/year versus Matter Premium's $60/year on the web.

**Choose Matter if**

- You listen more than you read: commutes and chores turn an article backlog into narration no rival matches.
- Newsletters and video are part of your diet — Gmail integration pulls newsletters in, and YouTube videos and podcasts get time-synced transcripts you can highlight.
- You annotate on Apple hardware: Apple Pencil highlighting on iPad, with highlights syncing to Notion, Obsidian, and Readwise.

The caution flags differ by side. Matter has no public API and limited full-library export, so treat it as a reading queue rather than a vault — and watch the App Store's $79.99/year price against $60 on the web. Raindrop's reader is basic with no true offline mode, so it's a mediocre place to finish long articles. Audio-first Apple users should take Matter and enjoy every commute. Everyone else — Android owners, archivists, sharers — lands on Raindrop.
