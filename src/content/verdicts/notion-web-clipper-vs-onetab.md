An honest framing: this pairing compares a broom to a bookshelf. OneTab exists to make an out-of-control browser survivable — every open tab collapses into a plain list with one click, memory frees up, and your machine breathes again. Notion Web Clipper exists to feed a workspace — a page you choose gets converted into a database entry that lives among your notes, tasks, and projects, synced to every device you own. Neither can perform the other's trick.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You need bulk relief, not curation — parking forty tabs at once is precisely what it does and precisely what Notion's one-page-at-a-time clipper cannot.
- Zero setup is the requirement: no account, no workspace, no destination decisions, and total local privacy.
- Your parked tabs are short-lived — things to reopen this week, not references to keep.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- Saved pages deserve a future: clips become real workspace pages with tags, properties, and views, reachable from your phone via share-sheet saving.
- You already live in Notion, so clipped articles land next to the projects that prompted them.
- Content preservation matters — imperfect as Notion's conversion can be, it beats OneTab's bare URL when the original page vanishes.

Bottom line: both are free, so cost decides nothing — workflow does. Run OneTab as the daily pressure valve; its 2026 v2 rewrite (folders, search, stars, dark mode) keeps the old workhorse pleasant. Send the handful of pages that actually matter through Notion's clipper into your workspace. The failure mode is asymmetric: OneTab as your archive loses data eventually; Notion as your tab parker is just tediously slow.
