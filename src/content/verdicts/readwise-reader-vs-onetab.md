Comparing these two is really a diagnosis of your tab problem. If open tabs are clutter — research sessions you might revisit, pages held open just in case — OneTab is the correct tool: one click collapses everything into a list, cutting browser memory by up to 95% by the developer's estimate, with no account, no limits, and no cost. The 2026 v2 rewrite even added folders, in-list search, stars, and dark mode. But if your tabs are unread articles, they're not clutter, they're a backlog — and Readwise Reader is built to clear backlogs: a triage inbox with keyboard-first flow, offline mobile apps, Ghostreader AI summaries, YouTube transcripts, and highlights exporting to Notion and Obsidian.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You need tab relief now, free, with zero setup — no account, and nothing transmitted anywhere
- URLs and titles are enough; you don't need saved content or search
- Everything happens on one desktop browser anyway

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- Those tabs deserve to be read, not just parked — on your phone, offline, in a queue with progress
- You want the page contents preserved and searchable, not a link that may rot
- Highlights and notes should end up in your knowledge tools

Bottom line: the failure modes tell the story. OneTab's worst case is losing your list — it lives in one browser profile, and uninstalling the extension deletes it. Reader's worst case is paying $119.88/year for articles you never open. Park with OneTab, read with Reader, and be honest with yourself about which one your tabs actually need.
