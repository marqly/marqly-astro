On paper this is a walkover — Pocket ceased operating in July 2025 and mymind is alive and thriving — but it's the rare case where the living tool is a poor fit for the dead tool's refugees. mymind is a private visual canvas: everything you save becomes a card, AI tags it automatically (recognizing objects, colors, brands, and text inside images), and you retrieve things by describing what you remember. It's gorgeous, private, and philosophically opposed to the one thing most Pocket survivors need: bringing an archive with them.

**Choose Pocket if**

- You're out of luck either way — the apps went dark in July 2025 and the data followed that October.
- Nothing about it can be resurrected, subscribed to, or downloaded.

**Choose mymind if**

- You're willing to start from zero. There is no bulk import from anywhere, by design.
- You save inspiration more than articles: images, screenshots, quotes, and ideas benefit most from the visual canvas and AI-powered recall.
- Privacy is a feature you'll pay for — no ads, no tracking, no social layer, funded purely by memberships from $4.99 to $12.99/month, with a free 100-card guest plan to test the waters.

Know what you're giving up from the Pocket playbook: there's no offline reading, no highlights, nothing shareable, and the meaningful AI — tagging from $7.99/month, summaries and reading mode only on the $12.99/month Mastermind tier — sits behind the higher plans. Export is a one-way CSV plus files, workable only from desktop Chrome or Edge. The bottom line splits cleanly: if your Pocket export represents years of reading you want to keep, take it to Raindrop.io, Instapaper, or Wallabag. If the shutdown felt strangely liberating, mymind is the most beautiful blank slate in the category.
