A tab parker and a bookmark manager get compared constantly because both rescue you from fifty open tabs — but they diverge the moment the tabs close. OneTab collapses everything into a local list of URLs, instantly and for free, with no account and a strict privacy posture. Raindrop.io turns those same tabs into a permanent, synced, organized library with collections, tags, and apps on every platform. One is a pressure valve; the other is storage.

**Choose Raindrop.io if:**

- Anything you save might matter next month — synced bookmarks with export beat a list that dies if the extension is uninstalled
- You need your links on a phone; OneTab has no mobile apps and no web account whatsoever
- Organization is the goal: nested collections, tags, and full-text search (on Pro) versus a chronological pile of URL groups

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You want zero friction and zero commitment — no account, no limits, no upsell, and tab URLs never leave your machine
- Memory relief is the actual problem; one click frees the resources of every open tab, cutting usage by up to 95% per the developer
- The 2026 v2 rewrite's folders, search, and stars cover all the structure you need for short-lived research sessions

Bottom line: OneTab earns its two million users by doing one thing perfectly and charging nothing, but it stores no page content, syncs nowhere, and carries genuine data-loss risk. Treat it as a workbench, not a warehouse. The moment a parked tab becomes something you would be upset to lose, it belongs in a real bookmark manager — and Raindrop's unlimited free plan removes any excuse not to move it.
