A tab parker and a bookmark manager get compared constantly because both rescue you from fifty open tabs — but they diverge the moment the tabs close. OneTab collapses everything into a local list of URLs, instantly and for free; encrypted cloud backup and sync is an optional separate service. Raindrop.io turns those same tabs into a permanent, synced, organized library with collections, tags, and apps on every platform. One is a pressure valve; the other is storage.

**Choose Raindrop.io if:**

- Anything you save might matter next month — synced bookmarks with export beat a list that dies if the extension is uninstalled
- You need your links on a phone; OneTab has no mobile apps and no web account whatsoever
- Organization is the goal: nested collections, tags, and full-text search (on Pro) versus a chronological pile of URL groups

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You want zero friction for local tab parking — no account is required for the core extension, while optional encrypted cloud backup and sync is separate
- Memory relief is the actual problem; one click frees the resources of every open tab, cutting usage by up to 95% per the developer
- The 2026 v2 rewrite's folders, search, and stars cover all the structure you need for short-lived research sessions

Bottom line: OneTab earns its audience by doing one thing well and charging nothing for core tab parking, but it stores no page content and its default local data carries genuine loss risk. Optional encrypted backup and sync reduces that risk, subject to separate service terms. Treat it as a workbench rather than a full archive. The moment a parked tab becomes something you would be upset to lose, it belongs in a real bookmark manager — and Raindrop's unlimited free plan removes any excuse not to move it.
