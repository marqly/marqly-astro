Same job title, opposite priorities. Evernote Web Clipper pours its effort into the capture moment: five browsers, five clip modes, annotated screenshots, and tags plus notebook assigned before the page is even saved. Notion Web Clipper barely has a capture moment — grab page, pick database, done — because Notion's bet is that value accrues afterward, when clips become database rows with properties, views, and filters inside the workspace where your projects already live.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Capture quality drives your workflow — clipping from Firefox or Edge, marking up screenshots, or filing precisely at save time.
- You search inside things: OCR reads text in images, and paid plans search inside PDFs and documents — territory Notion's clipper does not touch, since it cannot save PDFs at all.
- An archive is worth paying for, and $99–$249.99/year buys you the deepest clip-and-retrieve stack in the category.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- Notion is already your workspace — a reading list beside your tasks and wiki beats a better one in a separate app you must remember to open.
- Free matters: the clipper plus Notion's free plan handles a personal reading list without spending anything, while Evernote's 50-note free cap is effectively a demo.
- You organize with databases anyway, and clips gaining tags, statuses, and custom views is exactly the point.

The bottom line is a question of where you want the effort to happen. Evernote front-loads it into rich capture and charges accordingly; Notion defers it into manual database curation and charges nothing. Neither auto-files a single clip — if hands-off organization is what you are really after, look outside both ecosystems.
