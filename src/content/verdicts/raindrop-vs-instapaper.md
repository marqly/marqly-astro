This is the cleanest philosophical split in the bookmarking world: Raindrop.io is a place to keep links, Instapaper is a place to read them. Raindrop treats every save as an object to be filed — nested collections, cover art, tags, multiple view modes — and scales gracefully to a five-figure library. Instapaper treats every save as an article to be consumed, stripping it to beautifully set text and then getting out of the way.

**Choose Raindrop.io if**

- You collect broadly — tools, recipes, references, design inspiration — and need structure more than a reading flow; the free plan's unlimited bookmarks and collections are unmatched.
- You share: public collections turn any curated list into a link, and an open API plus 2,600+ integrations wire your library into everything else.
- You want true everywhere-coverage — web, Mac, iOS, Android, and every major browser extension.

**Choose Instapaper if**

- Long-form reading is the habit: offline reading on iPhone, iPad, and Android has been dependable since 2008, and the typography is still the category's best.
- You own a Kindle — Premium's send-to-Kindle delivery alone justifies it for many readers.
- You want extras built for readers, not organizers: speed reading, text-to-speech playlists, and a permanent article archive.

On price, Raindrop is the better deal: Pro runs $28/year against Instapaper Premium's $59.99/year (doubled from $30 in late 2023), and both paywall full-text search — free Instapaper additionally caps notes at five a month. Neither offers AI tagging or semantic search, so finding old saves depends on your own discipline either way. The bottom line is to match the tool to the verb: if your backlog problem is "where did I put it," get Raindrop; if it's "I never actually read it," get Instapaper.
