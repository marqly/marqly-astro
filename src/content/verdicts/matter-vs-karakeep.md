These two make opposite bets about what saving is for. Matter bets on consumption: a design-obsessed, Apple-first queue where articles arrive parsed and beautiful, newsletters flow in through Gmail or a dedicated address, YouTube videos and podcasts come transcribed with time-synced text, and everything can be narrated in voices good enough for a commute. Karakeep bets on accumulation: an open-source, Docker-deployed bookmark-everything app where AI files what you hoard — automatic tags, summaries, Meilisearch-backed full-text plus semantic search — and full-page archiving (screenshots, PDFs, even yt-dlp video downloads) makes the collection permanent.

**Choose Matter if:**

- You want to open an app and read or listen, with no server, keys, or configuration
- You're on iPhone, iPad, or Mac and value the best text-to-speech in the category
- Highlight syncing to Notion, Obsidian, and Readwise closes your note-taking loop

**Choose Karakeep if:**

- Running Docker doesn't scare you and free-forever on your own hardware sounds right
- You want AI organization — auto-tagging, summaries, semantic search — that Matter simply doesn't have
- Privacy is strict: with a local Ollama model, your data never leaves your machine

Bottom line: the gaps are mirror images. Matter lacks Android, a public API, and any AI organization at all; Karakeep lacks Matter's reading polish, text-to-speech, and effortless onboarding — its hosted Cloud remains in public beta with a 10-bookmark free cap, so it only truly shines self-hosted. Ask whether your saved links are a queue to enjoy or a library to control, and the answer names your tool.
