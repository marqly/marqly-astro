This is the classic hosted-versus-sovereign choice in read-it-later. Instapaper is a polished commercial service: save from anywhere, read in the category's finest typography, sync offline, push to Kindle. Wallabag is a French open-source project maintained since 2013 that you can run on your own server for nothing, or let wallabag.it host for €11 a year — with no feature gating anywhere, plus rule-based auto-tagging, annotation, a full REST API, and exports to ePub and PDF.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- The hours you spend reading should be beautiful — its reading view, speed reading, and mobile text-to-speech playlists remain unmatched here
- Kindle is your endgame; Premium's direct delivery beats manual ePub shuffling
- You want zero maintenance: no server, no PHP updates, no parsing configs — just a queue that works

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- Your reading data belongs on your own infrastructure, with the option of the near-free hosted plan when self-hosting palls
- You own a non-Kindle e-reader; ePub and PDF export on every plan is a standout feature
- Rule-based automatic tagging, RSS feeds of your saves, and a documented API fit a tinkerer's workflow

Bottom line: Wallabag is the value pick and the sovereignty pick at once — €11/year with nothing paywalled undercuts Instapaper Premium roughly five-fold, and the open-source license means the project cannot be acquired out from under you. What it cannot offer is Instapaper's refinement; the apps are dated and neither tool has any AI. Readers who prize the experience pay for Instapaper. Readers who prize the principle run Wallabag and pocket the difference.
