Price tags this far apart usually mean different products, and that holds here. Readwise Reader is the deluxe reading machine: one inbox swallowing articles, RSS, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, and YouTube, with Ghostreader AI on call and highlights streaming into Notion, Obsidian, and Roam. Wallabag is the sturdy bicycle: an open-source project from France, maintained since 2013, that strips articles to clean text, tags them by rule, and exports them to ePub for your e-reader — for €11 a year hosted, or free on your own server.

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- Reading is central to your work and the AI layer, transcripts, and triage inbox genuinely compound your output
- The highlight-to-notes pipeline is the point — daily review and exports into your knowledge system justify the Full plan by themselves
- You want a fast, keyboard-first experience with active, well-funded development behind it

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- €11/year covering everything sounds better than $119.88 covering things you might not use
- Self-hosting appeals or your reading data should live under your control, with full JSON, CSV, ePub, and PDF export
- An e-reader is your endpoint; Wallabag's ePub and PDF exports are a standout, and rule-based auto-tagging quietly handles filing

Bottom line: Reader is objectively the more capable product, and heavy readers with note systems extract real value from every dollar. But Wallabag's utilitarian interface hides the category's best value — no feature gating, open code, a sustainability model built on cheap subscriptions and self-hosters rather than venture patience. If your usage is "save articles, read them, occasionally export," paying ten times more buys mostly headroom you will never occupy.
