mymind and Obsidian Web Clipper both refuse the social web — no feeds, no followers, no sharing — and then part ways on everything else. mymind is a designed experience: a cloud canvas where AI reads your images, tags your cards, and search replaces structure, all for a subscription. Obsidian's clipper is raw material: pages, selections, and highlighted passages become Markdown files in a local vault, free forever, organized only as well as you organize them.

**Choose mymind if:**

- You want the machine to do the librarian's job — automatic tagging and image recognition with literally zero setup.
- Your library is visual: inspiration, products, screenshots, and design references rather than long-form text.
- Polish matters — mymind is arguably the best-looking saving tool made, across web, Mac, iOS, Android, and three browser extensions.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Ownership is the whole point: plain files on your disk, readable by any editor, with no export step because there is nothing to escape.
- You clip to write — captured passages flow straight into the same vault as your notes, essays, and drafts.
- You would rather configure than subscribe: per-site templates automate titling and filing, and the Interpreter runs clip-time AI through your own key or a free local Ollama model.

Bottom line: this choice is about which resource you would rather spend. mymind charges $7.99 to $12.99 monthly and in exchange demands nothing — no folders, no templates, no maintenance. Obsidian charges nothing and demands involvement — structure, sync arrangements, and AI configured by hand. Aesthetes and the organizationally allergic will love mymind; writers and tinkerers who measure tools in decades will keep Obsidian.
