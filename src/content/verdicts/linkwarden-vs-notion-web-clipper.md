Ask what happens after the click. With Notion Web Clipper, a page is converted into Notion blocks and dropped into a database, where it gains properties, views, and filters and lives among everything else you run in Notion. With Linkwarden, the page is preserved — screenshot, PDF, and readable copy, archived the moment you save, so the bookmark still works when the original changes or vanishes. Notion treats a clip as material for your workspace; Linkwarden treats it as evidence worth keeping.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- Your team and projects already live in Notion, and a separate bookmarking app would just be another tab.
- Database-style organization — status columns, filtered views, relations — is how you like a reading list to work.
- Zero cost matters: the clipper is free and Notion's free plan is generous for personal use, with Notion AI available on the $20/member/month Business plan.

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- Sources must be citable later: archived copies in multiple formats, plus optional Wayback Machine push, guard against link rot Notion does nothing about.
- You want capture to be dependable — Notion's own docs concede clip formatting and content can go missing.
- Open source, self-hosting, and AI tagging (including local models) appeal, at $3/month hosted or free on your server.

Capture ergonomics favor neither perfectly. Notion's clipper is minimal to a fault — a destination picker with no tags, notes, or highlights at save time — and officially supports only Chrome and Safari now. Linkwarden's extensions span Chrome, Firefox, and Edge, and it supports highlights and annotations, but its mobile apps only shipped in late 2025 and remain young. If your saves are inputs to work you're doing in Notion anyway, stay in Notion. If your saves are a library you'll still need in five years, Linkwarden is the archivist.
