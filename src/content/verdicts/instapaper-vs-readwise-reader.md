Instapaper and Readwise Reader are the minimalist and the maximalist of read-it-later. Instapaper has spent since 2008 perfecting one thing: a quiet, beautifully typeset queue that syncs offline to iPhone, iPad, Android, and Kindle. Reader piles every content type — articles, RSS, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, Twitter threads, YouTube transcripts — into a keyboard-driven triage inbox, then layers Ghostreader AI on top to summarize, define, and answer questions about what you read.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- Reading is the whole job — its typography-first view remains the cleanest in the category, with speed reading and text-to-speech playlists on mobile
- You want a real free tier: unlimited saves and offline reading cost nothing, and Premium is $59.99/year versus Reader's $119.88
- Kindle delivery matters, or you are landing a Pocket export somewhere simple

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- Your inputs sprawl across feeds, newsletters, PDFs, and video, and one inbox to triage them all justifies the subscription
- Highlights are raw material — the daily review loop and exports to Notion, Obsidian, and Roam are the best in the business
- You want AI in the reading flow: Ghostreader summaries and Q&A, plus YouTube transcripts you can highlight like an article

Bottom line: Reader is the more powerful product by a distance, and its bootstrapped, profitable model makes long-term survival credible. But power has a cost in both dollars and density — the UI carries a real learning curve, and $119.88/year buys the whole Readwise bundle whether you need highlight syncing or not. Instapaper's restraint is a feature: for people who save a dozen articles a week and read them in peace, it does everything required at half the price or free.
