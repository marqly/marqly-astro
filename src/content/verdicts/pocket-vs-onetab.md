Pocket died in July 2025, and OneTab is very much alive — but this pairing mostly reveals how little the two overlap. OneTab does exactly one thing: collapse every open browser tab into a list with one click, cutting memory use by up to 95% by its developer's estimate. It stores URLs and titles, locally, in one browser profile. Pocket stored the articles themselves, synced them to your phone, and let you read offline. A tab parker can't inherit a read-it-later service's job.

**Choose Pocket if**

- Nothing to install — it went offline in July 2025 and all remaining data was purged that autumn.
- Every path back to it is closed, including the app stores.

**Choose OneTab if**

- Your actual problem is fifty open tabs, not a reading backlog: parking and restoring sessions is instant, free, and account-less.
- Privacy and simplicity outrank features — tab URLs never leave your machine unless you deliberately share a list, and the 2026 v2 rewrite added folders, in-list search, stars, tasks, and dark mode.
- You occasionally hand a set of links to someone: any list becomes a shareable web page in one click.

Understand the fragility before trusting it with anything precious. There's no sync (the promised encrypted version is still "coming soon"), no mobile apps, no page content, and no full-text search — and uninstalling the extension or clearing browser data can erase your list entirely. There's no importer either, so a Pocket export has nowhere to go here. The sensible conclusion isn't either/or: use OneTab as the free pressure valve for tab overload it was always meant to be, and give the reading half of your old Pocket habit to an actual read-it-later app like Instapaper or Matter, which can also absorb your export.
