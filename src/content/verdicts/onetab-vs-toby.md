Finally, a true head-to-head: both are tab managers, and they split the category along solo-versus-team lines. OneTab is the minimalist's answer — one button flattens every open tab into a text list, no account exists, nothing costs anything, and after a 2026 v2 rewrite the old warhorse even gained folders, search, stars, tasks, and dark mode. Toby is the organizer's answer — tabs become visual cards in collections and spaces, synced through the cloud, restored as whole sessions, and shared with teammates or the public.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You want tab relief with zero ceremony: no signup, no sync setup, no tier to outgrow, and unlimited saved tabs forever.
- Privacy by architecture appeals — URLs never leave your machine unless you deliberately publish a list as a shared web page.
- One desktop browser is your whole world, so the absence of mobile apps and sync costs you nothing.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Tabs are team property: shared spaces, member invites, org-wide collections, and no-account public links are the product's spine.
- You live across devices — extensions on three browsers, a web app anywhere, and iOS/Android companions keep collections in reach.
- Visual organization sticks better for you than lists, and Toby AI grouping and naming your open tabs sweetens the deal.

Bottom line: solo users should default to OneTab and pocket the savings — its real weaknesses are the missing sync and the fragility of a purely local list, so export backups regularly. Teams and multi-device workers should pay for Toby, which is the strongest collaborative tab manager available, while remembering its 60-tab free plan is a trial in all but name.
