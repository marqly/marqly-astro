This comparison ended abruptly in November 2024, when Omnivore's team joined ElevenLabs and the hosted service closed, deleting whatever its users had not exported. While it lived, Omnivore was the free, open-source counterweight to Reader: newsletters, RSS, PDFs, labels, a genuinely powerful search syntax, native mobile apps with text-to-speech — at zero cost. Reader was the paid professional alternative. Now it is largely the destination.

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- You want what Omnivore did, maintained and improved — the multi-format inbox, plus Ghostreader AI, YouTube transcripts, and the Readwise highlight pipeline into Notion, Obsidian, and Roam
- Sustainability matters after being burned once; a bootstrapped, profitable subscription business is the opposite of free-with-no-revenue
- Active development is worth paying for: $9.99/month billed annually, after a 30-day trial

**Choose an Omnivore fork if:**

- Self-hosting was always the appeal — the AGPL code survives on GitHub with over a thousand forks, and community projects offer deployments
- You can operate a heavy stack (PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, microservices) and accept volunteer maintenance with no roadmap promises

Bottom line: Omnivore's shutdown is the read-it-later category's clearest cautionary tale, and it reframes how to shop here. The feature list matters less than the funding model behind it, because features you rely on can vanish with thirty days' notice. Reader is expensive precisely because it is built to last. Users unwilling to pay that much should look at open-source options with real sustainability stories — Wallabag's hosted service, for one — rather than hoping another free hosted miracle appears.
