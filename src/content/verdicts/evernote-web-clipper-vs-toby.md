These two live in different tenses. Toby works in the present: your new-tab page becomes a board of visual collections, today's tab session stashes in one click and restores tomorrow, and a teammate opens your shared space without even creating an account. Evernote Web Clipper works in the past-for-the-future: it extracts pages from the browser and embalms them — content, screenshots, annotations — inside notebooks engineered so that the thing you clipped in March is findable in November.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Your unit of work is the session — sets of tabs per project or client that open, close, and pass between people all week.
- Team features pay their way: shared spaces, invites, and SSO have no counterpart in a clipping workflow.
- Losing a link's content is acceptable, because collections point at living sites you revisit rather than material you must retain.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Retention is the requirement — clipped content survives site deaths, redesigns, and paywalls because Evernote keeps the content itself.
- Search depth matters: OCR across images and PDF search reach places Toby, with no full-text search at all, cannot go.
- You capture in many forms — article mode, selections, annotated screenshots — rather than filing URLs.

Bottom line, stated plainly: this is an odd-couple comparison, and the right answer is usually "whichever matches your actual problem," not a points victory. Tab overload with collaborators means Toby at $4.50/member/month. A durable, searchable body of clipped knowledge means Evernote at its admittedly steep $99–$249.99/year. Buying one to do the other's job is how both products earn their bad reviews.
