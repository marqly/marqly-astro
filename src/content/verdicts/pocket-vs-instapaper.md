These two invented the category together — Read It Later (later Pocket) in 2007, Instapaper in 2008 — and for years they were near-twins: save an article from anywhere, read it later in a clean view, offline on your phone. The differences were temperamental. Pocket leaned social and algorithmic, with Discover recommendations and deep Firefox integration; Instapaper stayed quiet and typographic, obsessing over line spacing and adding send-to-Kindle. Mozilla ended the argument by pulling the plug in July 2025.

**Choose Pocket if**

- There is no scenario where you can — the service went offline on July 8, 2025, and any account data left unexported past October 8, 2025 was destroyed.
- Even the mobile apps are gone from both app stores.

**Choose Instapaper if**

- You want the least disruptive Pocket migration available: a direct Pocket import, the same save-read-archive rhythm, and a business that has run continuously since 2008.
- Reading is the point. Its typography-first reader remains the purest in the category, with dependable offline reading on iPhone, iPad, and Android.
- You own a Kindle — Premium delivers saved articles straight to it, alongside a permanent archive, speed reading, and text-to-speech playlists.

The familiar frustrations survive the switch, though. Like Pocket's free plan, free Instapaper can't search the full text of your own saves — that requires Premium at $5.99/month or $59.99/year — and notes are capped at five per month. There's no AI tagging or resurfacing, organization stops at folders, and parsing occasionally mangles complex pages. Those are quibbles against the bottom line: Instapaper is a stable, independently run service that outlived its old rival, and for anyone whose Pocket habit was fundamentally about reading saved articles in peace, it's the closest thing to not switching at all.
