Pocket no longer exists — Mozilla ended it in July 2025 — which leaves Notion Web Clipper as the survivor by default. Whether it's a good successor depends entirely on one question: do you already run your life in Notion? The clipper's entire value is that saved pages land in Notion databases, inheriting tags, properties, views, and filters, so a reading list can sit beside your projects and tasks. As a standalone saving tool, it's the thinnest of the major clippers.

**Choose Pocket if**

- You missed your chance permanently — the shutdown finished in 2025 and no user data remains.
- It survives only as the reason this page exists.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if**

- Your notes, projects, and tasks are already Notion databases and you want clipped links in the same system, organized with the same properties and views.
- Free matters: the extension costs nothing and Notion's free plan is generous for a personal reading list.
- You'll eventually want AI over your saves — Notion AI can chat with and summarize clipped content, though it's bundled only with the $20/member/month Business plan.

The compromises are real. Capture is minimal — no tags, notes, highlights, or screenshots at clip time, just a destination picker — and Notion's own documentation warns that formatting and content can drop out of clips. Official browser support has narrowed to Chrome and Safari, and saving into large databases can be slow. Migration is manual too, since there's no Pocket importer and Pocket's export was a CSV rather than bookmark HTML. Bottom line: Notion users get a serviceable free reading list inside the workspace they already open every day; everyone else — especially ex-Pocket readers who prized the clean reader and offline sync — should treat this as a database feature, not a reading app.
