Ownership versus service is the whole argument here. Obsidian Web Clipper converts the web into plain Markdown files on your own disk: pages, selections, or highlighter-marked passages, filed automatically by per-site templates that pull titles, tags, and metadata — total privacy, zero lock-in, nothing to export because nothing ever left your machine. Readwise Reader is the opposite bet: a subscription service that manages your entire reading intake — articles, RSS, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, YouTube — in a synced triage inbox with Ghostreader AI, then hands the valuable residue (highlights and notes) to tools like Obsidian.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Your knowledge lives in an Obsidian vault and clips should arrive as Markdown, pre-tagged by your templates
- Free and local matter: no subscription, no cloud, and it works on Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari — even Safari on iPhone
- You'll happily organize, sync, and search things yourself

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- You need a reading workflow, not just capture — triage, progress, offline apps on iOS and Android
- Built-in AI and YouTube transcripts beat configuring your own LLM keys
- Daily highlight review is worth $9.99/month to you

Bottom line: this is less either/or than most pairings. The clipper costs nothing and does one thing excellently — turning web pages into files you'll own forever. Reader costs $119.88/year and does many things — but its highlight export lands in Obsidian natively, so the honest recommendation depends on volume: casual clippers need only the free tool, while readers processing dozens of items a week will feel Reader's absence more than its price.
