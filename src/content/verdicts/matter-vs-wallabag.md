The core trade here is polish versus permanence. Matter is what happens when a small San Francisco team obsesses over the reading experience on Apple hardware: gorgeous typography, Apple Pencil highlighting on iPad, newsletters flowing in via Gmail, YouTube and podcasts transcribed with time-synced text, and narration good enough that articles feel produced. Wallabag is what happens when an open-source project survives every trend since 2013: a utilitarian, self-hostable reading queue with rule-based automatic tagging, annotations on selected text, RSS feeds of your saves, a full REST API, and exports to PDF and ePub that e-reader owners treasure.

**Choose Matter if:**

- You listen as much as you read — commutes, walks, dishes — and want the category's best voices
- You're all-Apple and want share-sheet saving, offline reading, and highlights syncing to Notion, Obsidian, and Readwise
- The free tier basics (unlimited library, parsing, tags) might be all you ever need

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- You want your reading data on your own server, or hosted for just €11/year
- You use Android, an e-reader, or anything outside the Apple fence
- An API and comprehensive exports (JSON, CSV, ePub, PDF) matter — Matter has no public API and limited library export

Bottom line: Wallabag's weaknesses are cosmetic — dated design, occasional parsing failures on script-heavy sites — while Matter's are structural: no Android, no API, and a $79.99 App Store annual price that quietly undercuts its own $60 web rate. If the reading experience is the product, buy Matter. If the reading archive is the product, Wallabag will still be standing in a decade.
