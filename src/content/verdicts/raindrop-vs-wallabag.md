These two answer different anxieties. Raindrop.io answers "how do I keep a thousand links organized?" with the most polished hosted bookmark manager available. Wallabag answers "how do I make sure no company can ever take my reading library away?" with open-source software you can run yourself — and after watching Pocket and Omnivore die, that's not a paranoid question. They also sit in different categories: Raindrop files bookmarks; Wallabag is a read-it-later queue with a stripped reading view and offline mobile apps.

**Choose Raindrop.io if**

- Polish and zero setup win: beautiful apps everywhere, instant onboarding, and a free plan with unlimited bookmarks, collections, and highlights.
- You organize visually and share publicly — nested collections with covers and shareable links go far beyond Wallabag's plain lists.
- You want integrations: an open API plus 2,600+ connections through services like Zapier and IFTTT.

**Choose Wallabag if**

- Ownership is non-negotiable: self-host for free, or pay €11/year at wallabag.it — either way the open-source software outlives any vendor decision.
- You read on an e-reader: exporting saves to ePub and PDF is a signature capability nothing in Raindrop matches.
- You want a true reading queue — clean article view, offline iOS and Android apps, rule-based auto-tagging — rather than a filing system.

The trade-offs are honest on both sides. Wallabag's interface feels dated, parsing fails on some script-heavy sites, and self-hosting means ongoing maintenance; Raindrop keeps full-text search and annotations behind Pro and can't match €11/year hosted pricing. Neither has meaningful AI. Pick by temperament: pragmatists who want things to just work choose Raindrop; principled self-hosters and e-reader owners choose Wallabag and sleep well knowing the shutdown story can never happen to them.
