Toby and Wallabag both answer "where do these browser pages go?" — but for different tenses. Toby handles the present: open tabs drag into visual collections on your new-tab page, whole sessions save and restore in one click, and spaces are shared with teammates or published as no-login public links. Wallabag handles the future: each saved page is parsed into clean article text and stored — on your server if you wish — to be read offline, annotated, and exported to an e-reader long after the tab closed.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Your work lives in tab sessions you need back tomorrow, per project, per client.
- Sharing is the point — team spaces, member invites, org-wide collections, and SSO on the Team plan.
- You'd enjoy Toby AI naming, sorting, and grouping your open tabs automatically.

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- Content outlasting the link matters: Toby stores bare links, so a dead URL means the page is gone, while Wallabag keeps the text.
- You want full-text search across everything you've saved — Toby has none.
- Open source and cost control appeal: free self-hosted, or €11/year, versus Toby's per-member subscriptions.

Pricing shapes the verdict more than usual here. Toby's free Starter plan caps at 60 saved tabs total — hit it and you can look but not add — and paid plans run $4.50 to $8 per member per month billed yearly, which is designed for teams and stings for individuals. Wallabag has no caps anywhere and one of the cheapest hosted plans in existence. A team that works out of shared tab sessions gets real value from Toby that Wallabag can't offer. A solo reader building a library gets almost nothing from Toby that a bookmark bar doesn't already do — and everything from Wallabag.
