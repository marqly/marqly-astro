Two open-source answers to keeping what you find online — with opposite architectures. Obsidian Web Clipper writes each capture as a plain Markdown file on your disk, shaped by per-site templates and destined to be linked, edited, and thought with inside a vault. Linkwarden stores saves on a server (yours or its $3/month Cloud) as full-page archives — screenshot, PDF, readable copy — browsable from any web browser, shareable publicly, and editable with teammates under per-user permissions. Files you own versus a library you host.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Clips are raw material for notes: highlighter mode captures marked passages, and templates auto-title, tag, and file by page metadata.
- Absolute portability wins — plain text on disk, no export step, no server, no company in the loop.
- Clip-time AI on your terms appeals: the Interpreter summarizes or extracts with your own API key or a free local Ollama model.

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- You save far more than you'll ever re-read, and want it archived, auto-tagged by AI, and full-text searchable without vault gardening.
- Collaboration or public sharing is required — Obsidian offers neither without Publish at $8/month.
- You need a web app plus iOS and Android apps that reach your whole library anywhere.

The friction profiles differ more than the philosophies. Obsidian's route means manual organization, no automatic tagging, and cross-device sync via Obsidian Sync at $4/month or DIY setups — and it only makes sense if you actually use Obsidian. Linkwarden's route means running Docker or paying for Cloud, mobile apps that are new as of late 2025, and search that's keyword-only. Writers and researchers who work their clips into ideas should take the clipper. Collectors who need everything kept, findable, and shareable should take Linkwarden.
