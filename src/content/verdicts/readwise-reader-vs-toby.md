Toby and Readwise Reader both rescue you from tab overload, but they rescue different things. Toby rescues the workspace: open tabs become drag-and-drop visual collections on your new-tab page, whole sessions stash and restore in one click, and collections group into spaces you can share with teammates — or publish as links anyone can view without an account. Toby AI will even name and sort your open tabs automatically. Reader rescues the content: articles, newsletters, RSS, PDFs, ePubs, and YouTube transcripts saved in full, triaged in a keyboard-driven queue, highlighted, summarized by Ghostreader AI, and exported to Notion, Obsidian, and Roam.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Your tabs represent projects, not reading — research sessions, client work, recurring tool sets
- You work on a team: shared spaces, member invites, and SSO on the Team plan are rare in this category
- Restoring a working context in one click would save you daily friction

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- Your tabs are unread articles and videos that deserve a real reading workflow
- You want the actual page content saved, searchable, and readable offline on iOS and Android
- Highlights flowing into your notes system matter

Bottom line: the free tiers expose each product's edges. Toby's Starter caps at 60 saved tabs total — workable for sessions, cramped for hoarding — while Reader offers no free tier at all, just a 30-day trial before $119.88/year. If links are placeholders for work, Toby earns its keep. If links are placeholders for reading, only Reader actually finishes the job.
