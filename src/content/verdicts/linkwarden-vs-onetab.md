The overlap here is thinner than it looks. OneTab, free since 2013 and rewritten in 2026 with folders, search, stars, and dark mode, does one thing brilliantly: it collapses every open tab into a local list, instantly relieving browser memory pressure. Linkwarden does the opposite of ephemeral: each saved link is archived as a screenshot, PDF, and readable copy on a server, tagged (optionally by AI), and made full-text searchable across the archived content itself. One empties your browser; the other fills a library.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- You want relief without commitment — no account, no limits, no cost, and one click to park everything.
- Local-only storage is a feature: tab URLs never leave your machine unless you deliberately share a list as a web page.
- Your saved tabs are short-lived by nature, destined for restore-or-delete within days.

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- Anything you save might matter in a year — archived copies survive dead links, and search digs through page content, not just titles.
- You need your library beyond one browser profile: web app, iOS and Android apps, and collaboration with per-user permissions.
- Open source and self-hosting appeal, or the $3/month Cloud fits your budget.

OneTab's convenience carries real hazards: your list lives in a single browser profile, uninstalling the extension deletes it, clearing browser data can wipe it, and the promised encrypted sync remains a "coming soon." There's also no content — URLs and titles only, so a dead link is a dead end. Linkwarden asks more upfront (a server or a small subscription, and no tab-session workflow at all) but loses nothing. Triage tabs with OneTab if you must; trust anything you'd grieve losing to a tool that actually archives it.
