Toby and Notion Web Clipper both promise order, but they order different things. Toby organizes the browser itself: open tabs become drag-and-drop collections on your new-tab page, sessions stash and restore whole, and spaces circulate through a team — even Toby AI pitches in, naming and grouping your open tabs automatically. Notion's clipper organizes what you take out of the browser: individual pages converted into database entries inside the workspace holding your notes, tasks, and wiki.

**Choose Toby if:**

- You operate in tab sets — the same fifteen tabs per project, opened and closed daily, ideally shared with whoever is working alongside you.
- Speed of capture wins: one click banks an entire session, versus clipping pages into Notion individually.
- Your saved links point at living tools and dashboards you revisit, so Toby's lack of content archiving costs you nothing.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- The page's content is the value — Notion converts and stores it (imperfectly at times), while Toby retains nothing but the link.
- Clips should join a larger system: database properties, filters, and views, plus workspace AI if you later pay for Business.
- Free matters — Notion's generous free plan versus Toby's hard 60-tab ceiling makes the zero-cost path one-sided.

Bottom line: an odd couple, and the honest advice is to match the tool to the tense of your problem. Present-tense chaos — too many live tabs, a team that needs the same set — is Toby's territory, and it is the strongest team tab manager available. Past-tense value — pages worth keeping, reading, and referencing — belongs in Notion. Plenty of workflows justifiably run both at once.
