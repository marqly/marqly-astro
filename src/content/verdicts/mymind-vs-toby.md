mymind and Toby both replace messy browser habits, but they aim at different messes. Toby targets the working day: open tabs become drag-and-drop collections on your new-tab page, whole sessions save and restore in one click, and spaces are shared across a team. mymind targets the long term: a private, beautiful canvas where saved images, links, and quotes are AI-tagged and searchable by what you remember about them. One tool is for what you are doing; the other is for what you want to keep.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Your tabs are your workflow — recurring project sets, client dashboards, research sessions that stash and restore on demand.
- Other people need in: shared spaces and no-account public links make Toby the collaborative option, while mymind shares nothing by design.
- Session management should be free-ish — the 60-tab Starter plan covers light use before the $4.50/member/month tier kicks in.

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save for future-you, not this-week-you: inspiration and references that need to resurface months later, findable by AI-powered search.
- Images matter — recognition of objects, colors, and brands inside pictures is something Toby does not attempt.
- Privacy is the feature: no ads, no tracking, no social anything, funded purely by memberships.

Bottom line: this is a workflow tool versus a memory tool, and the overlap is thinner than the category labels suggest. Toby's links-only collections lose content to link rot and cannot be searched by text, which disqualifies it as an archive. mymind cannot save a tab session or share a board, which disqualifies it as a work surface. Pick by the problem: collaborative tab chaos means Toby, a growing private collection means mymind.
