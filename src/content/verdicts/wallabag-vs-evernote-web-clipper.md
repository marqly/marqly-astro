The comparison is really queue versus platform. Wallabag does one thing: it takes articles off the web, cleans them up, and holds them until you read, annotate, or export them. Evernote Web Clipper is a doorway into something much larger — a decades-old note system where clips sit beside meeting notes, PDFs, and screenshots, all indexed by search that reads text inside images and, on paid plans, inside documents. Since the v11 release in January 2026, Evernote also chats across your notes with an AI assistant and offers semantic search.

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- Saving and reading articles is the whole job — you don't need a second brain, just a queue.
- Price sensitivity is real: €11/year hosted, or free on your own server, versus Evernote's $99–$249.99/year.
- You want open-source software and full data ownership, with exports to ePub, PDF, JSON, and CSV.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- You live in a research-heavy workflow where clips, documents, and notes must be searchable together, OCR included.
- Capture flexibility matters — full page, simplified article, selection, bookmark, or annotated screenshot, from any of five browsers, with tags and a destination notebook chosen at clip time.
- You'll actually use the v11 AI layer: note-spanning chat, semantic search, meeting notes.

Two caveats keep this honest. Evernote's free plan — 50 notes, one notebook, one device — is a demo, not a tier; budget for a subscription or skip it entirely. And despite all that machinery, Evernote won't auto-tag your clips, while Wallabag at least automates filing with rules. Conversely, Wallabag's utilitarian interface and absent AI make it feel spartan next to Evernote's polish. If bookmarking articles is the need, the €11 tool wins on sheer fit; if you're archiving a working life, Evernote justifies its price better than its reputation suggests.
