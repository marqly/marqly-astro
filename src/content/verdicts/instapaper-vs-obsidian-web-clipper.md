Where should a saved article live: in a queue or in your notes? Instapaper answers queue — a synced, typography-obsessed reading list that works offline on iPhone, Android, and Kindle, no setup beyond a login. Obsidian Web Clipper answers notes — pages, selections, and highlights converted to clean Markdown files on your own disk, auto-filed by per-site templates, optionally pre-processed by clip-time AI prompts through your own API key or a local Ollama model.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- Reading is a ritual, not research — the polished view, speed reading, and text-to-speech playlists exist for pure consumption
- You want cross-device sync without paying or configuring anything; the clipper needs Obsidian Sync at $4/month or DIY workarounds for that
- You do not use Obsidian — the clipper is pointless without the vault behind it

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Clipped articles become permanent knowledge: linkable, editable Markdown that no company shutdown can take away
- Automation delights you — templates pulling page metadata into titles and tags, plus Interpreter prompts that summarize or translate at capture
- Total cost matters: everything here is free and open source, versus $59.99/year for Instapaper's full feature set

Bottom line: these tools can genuinely coexist — plenty of people read in one place and archive keepers elsewhere — but as primary homes for saved articles they serve opposite temperaments. Instapaper asks nothing and gives comfort; the clipper asks for an Obsidian habit, template tinkering, and a sync plan, then gives ownership no hosted service can match. Convenience or permanence: that is the actual question.
