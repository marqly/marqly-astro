Line these two up and the category labels dissolve: they share a toolbar, not a purpose. OneTab is browser janitorial work done brilliantly — every open tab collapses into a plain local list, restorable individually or wholesale, with a 2026 v2 rewrite adding folders, search, stars, and dark mode to what was always a gloriously simple tool. Evernote Web Clipper is an acquisition pipeline: it pulls pages out of the browser entirely and deposits them, content and all, into a note platform built for retrieval a decade from now.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- The pain is right now — too many tabs, too little RAM — and a free, no-account fix beats any archive.
- Most parked tabs are disposable; you will reopen them soon or forget them without regret.
- You distrust clouds: OneTab transmits nothing anywhere unless you explicitly share a list as a web page.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- The clipped material has a future — research, receipts, references — and needs to be findable by content, not just by title.
- OCR earns its keep for you: text inside images and (on paid plans) PDFs becomes searchable, something a URL list can never offer.
- You already pay for Evernote, making the clipper a free extension of a platform you use anyway.

Bottom line: this odd couple pairs better than it competes. OneTab handles volume — the fifty tabs a day that deserve parking, not preserving. Evernote handles value — the two pages a week worth keeping permanently. The expensive mistake is confusing the two: paying Evernote prices for tab parking, or trusting OneTab's fragile local list with anything you would grieve losing.
