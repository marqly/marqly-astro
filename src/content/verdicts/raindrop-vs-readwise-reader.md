One of these is a library, the other is a pipeline. Raindrop.io wants to hold your links forever in tidy, visual collections; Readwise Reader wants to move content through you — a triage inbox for articles, RSS, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, and YouTube, with highlights flowing out to Notion, Obsidian, and Roam. The price gap is just as stark: Raindrop is free for unlimited everything with a $28/year Pro tier, while Reader costs $119.88/year with no free tier beyond a 30-day trial.

**Choose Raindrop.io if**

- You're a collector first: most saves are references to keep, not documents to finish, and nested collections with covers beat any inbox for that.
- You'd resent paying: the free plan covers unlimited bookmarks, collections, highlights, and devices — and Pro is roughly a quarter of Reader's price.
- Sharing matters: public collections and thousands of integrations have no equivalent in Reader, which offers no public sharing at all.

**Choose Readwise Reader if**

- You process serious reading volume and want one keyboard-first queue for every content type, including newsletters and RSS.
- AI while reading earns its keep for you: Ghostreader summarizes, defines, and answers questions against the text, and YouTube transcripts are readable and highlightable.
- Your notes system is the destination — the highlight-to-Notion/Obsidian loop with daily review is the strongest reason Reader exists.

Neither searches semantically — both are keyword-based, so retrieval still leans on your tagging habits. Both are healthy, actively developed businesses, so there's no shutdown-risk tiebreaker. Plenty of people run the two together, letting Reader burn down the reading queue while Raindrop archives the keepers. Forced to one: casual savers and visual organizers should keep the $120 and take Raindrop; daily heavy readers with a PKM habit will feel Reader pay for itself within a month.
