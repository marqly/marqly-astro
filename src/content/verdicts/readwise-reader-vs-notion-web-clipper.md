On paper these overlap; in practice they're a database versus a pipeline. Notion Web Clipper is a thin, free capture tool whose whole value is its destination — clips become pages in whatever database you choose, inheriting Notion's tags, properties, views, and filters, so a reading list can sit beside projects and tasks in one workspace. Readwise Reader is a self-contained reading machine: articles, RSS, newsletters, PDFs, ePubs, and YouTube transcripts land in a keyboard-driven triage inbox, Ghostreader AI summarizes and answers questions, and highlights export continuously — including, neatly, to Notion itself.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- You live in Notion already and want saved links where your notes and tasks are
- Free matters: the clipper plus Notion's free plan cover a personal reading list at zero cost
- You enjoy organizing with database properties and custom views

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- You save more than you read and need a queue that actively helps you catch up
- Highlighting, daily review, and clean article parsing across mobile and web justify $119.88/year
- You want AI at reading time without a $20/member/month Business plan

Bottom line: capture quality is the trap to notice. Notion's clipper offers no tags, notes, or screenshots at save time, official support has narrowed to Chrome and Safari, and fidelity varies enough that Notion documents it. If a rough copy filed in the right database is fine, the price is unbeatable. If you'll actually read what you capture, Reader is worth real money — and since it exports highlights to Notion anyway, some people sensibly run both.
