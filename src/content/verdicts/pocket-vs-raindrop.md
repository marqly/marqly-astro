Pocket and Raindrop.io solved different problems even when both were alive. Pocket was a reading queue: save an article, open it later in a stripped-down view, archive it, move on. Raindrop.io is a filing cabinet — nested collections, cover art, tags, and multiple view modes built for keeping thousands of links organized indefinitely. Since July 8, 2025, only one of them solves anything at all: Mozilla shut Pocket down, closed exports on October 8, 2025, and permanently deleted whatever remained.

**Choose Pocket if**

- You can't. The apps and extensions stopped working in July 2025, they have been removed from every store, and no workaround revives a deleted account.
- You're here out of nostalgia — the comparison is now historical.

**Choose Raindrop.io if**

- You kept your Pocket export ZIP and want those saves organized rather than just queued; Raindrop's Pocket importer reads the file directly, even on the free plan.
- You want unlimited bookmarks, collections, highlights, and devices for $0, with a $28/year Pro upgrade for full-text search, permanent page copies, and daily backups.
- You save more than articles — Raindrop handles any kind of link, shares collections publicly, and runs natively on web, Mac, iOS, Android, and every major browser.

The migration caveat is about reading, not collecting. Raindrop has no true offline mode and its reader is deliberately basic, so if Pocket was primarily where you read long-form on the train, Instapaper or Matter will feel closer to home. If Pocket was where links went to be found again someday, Raindrop is the strongest free landing spot left — assuming you exported in time. If you didn't, there is nothing to migrate; Mozilla's deletion was final.
