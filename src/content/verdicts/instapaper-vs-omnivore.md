Start with the fact that settles it: Omnivore no longer exists as a service. The open-source read-it-later favorite announced in October 2024 that its team was joining ElevenLabs, and the hosted platform shut down the following month, deleting whatever users had not exported by November 30. Comparing it with Instapaper today is really a question of what former Omnivore users — or people drawn to its free, open-source ideal — should do now.

**Choose Instapaper if:**

- You want a hosted queue that will still be there in five years — eighteen years of continuous, independently funded operation is the strongest longevity signal in this category
- Reading comfort ranks first: the typography-focused view, offline sync across iPhone, iPad, and Android, and send-to-Kindle on Premium
- You kept an Omnivore or Pocket export; Instapaper imports Pocket files directly and its free tier makes trying it costless

**Choose an Omnivore fork if:**

- Self-hosting is non-negotiable and you can run a genuinely heavy stack — PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, and multiple microservices
- You accept volunteer maintenance with no promises; the AGPL codebase has well over a thousand forks but no official successor

The bottom line carries a lesson Omnivore taught the whole category: free-with-no-revenue is a countdown, not a price. Omnivore was well-loved — clean reader, powerful search syntax, newsletters and RSS included, all at no charge — and none of it survived contact with its funding model. Instapaper is the duller, sturdier choice, and if search matters you will eventually pay $59.99/year for it. That trade, in hindsight, is the entire point.
