Only one of these tools still exists. Omnivore's hosted service closed in November 2024 after the team was acqui-hired by ElevenLabs, with a one-month export window before deletion. Raindrop.io, meanwhile, has shipped steadily since 2013. What remains of this matchup is really a migration briefing for ex-Omnivore users weighing Raindrop as a destination — and the philosophical gap matters for that decision. Omnivore was a free, open-source read-it-later app: a clean reader for articles, newsletters, and PDFs with labels and a powerful search syntax. Raindrop is a commercial bookmark manager: world-class at filing and retrieving links, merely adequate at reading them.

**Choose Omnivore if**

- You're a determined self-hoster: the AGPL code survives on GitHub with over a thousand forks — but the stack (PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, multiple microservices) is heavy, and forks are volunteer-run with no guarantees.
- Realistically, running a fork is the only path; the hosted service and its data are gone.

**Choose Raindrop.io if**

- You want a maintained, polished home with no server to babysit — apps on web, Mac, iOS, and Android, plus every major browser extension.
- Free actually means free: unlimited bookmarks, collections, highlights, and devices, with Pro at $28/year adding full-text search and permanent page copies.
- Sustainability matters after the Omnivore experience: Raindrop is funded by its subscribers, precisely the revenue Omnivore never had.

The practical bottom line: Raindrop can absorb an Omnivore export saved before the November 2024 deadline, but ex-Omnivore users should notice what changes. Raindrop has no offline reading mode and a basic reader, so people who loved Omnivore for reading may be happier on Wallabag or Readwise Reader. As a durable, organized library with a generous free plan, though, Raindrop is the safest hosted landing available.
