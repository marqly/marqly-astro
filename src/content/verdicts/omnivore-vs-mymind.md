This comparison has a one-sided answer baked in: Omnivore's hosted service closed in November 2024 after the team joined ElevenLabs, and unexported data was deleted at the end of that month. mymind, funded entirely by memberships, is alive and actively developed. Even when both were running, they were opposites — Omnivore was a free, open-source article reader with highlights, offline apps, and text-to-speech; mymind is a paid, deliberately single-player visual board where AI tags every card and recognizes objects, colors, and brands inside images.

**Choose an Omnivore fork if:**

- You are a committed self-hoster willing to run a demanding stack (PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, multiple microservices) maintained by volunteers.
- You want a genuinely free, open-source reading queue and accept the uncertainty that comes with community forks.

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save more images, screenshots, and inspiration than long articles, and want them searchable by what's in them.
- You want zero filing — no folders, no manual tags, AI does the sorting.
- Privacy matters: no ads, no tracking, no social layer.

Two honest caveats before switching. First, mymind supports no bulk import by design, so the export ZIP you (hopefully) pulled from Omnivore before the deadline cannot be brought over — you start from zero, or script something against the API that entered beta in mid-2026. Second, mymind is weak at exactly what Omnivore did best: there are no highlights, no offline reading, and AI summaries plus reading mode sit behind the $12.99/month Mastermind tier. If your Omnivore habit was reading articles, a maintained read-it-later app is the truer successor; if it was collecting visual ideas, mymind is the upgrade Omnivore never was.
