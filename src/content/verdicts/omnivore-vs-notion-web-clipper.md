Start with the disqualifier: Omnivore shut down in November 2024 when its team was acqui-hired by ElevenLabs, and hosted data not exported by the November 30 deadline is gone forever. What survives is AGPL-licensed code on GitHub and a set of community forks for self-hosters. Notion Web Clipper, meanwhile, is a small, free extension backed by a very alive company — and for former Omnivore users, Notion's free plan is one of the few hosted options that echoes Omnivore's price of zero.

**Choose an Omnivore fork if:**

- You want reading-first software with highlights and a proper article view, and you're equipped to self-host a stack built from PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, and microservices.
- Open source matters more to you than official support or guarantees.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- Your notes, projects, and tasks already live in Notion and a reading list belongs beside them, organized with database tags, views, and filters.
- Free matters: the clipper costs nothing and Notion's free plan is generous for personal use.
- You may grow into Notion AI — chat and summaries over clipped content come bundled with the $20/member/month Business plan.

Know what you're trading away. Omnivore offered highlights, offline mobile apps, text-to-speech, and a powerful search syntax purpose-built for reading. Notion's clipper captures a page and lets you pick a destination — no tags, notes, or highlights at clip time — and Notion's own documentation warns that formatting and content can go missing. Official browser support has also narrowed to Chrome and Safari. As a workspace with a reading list attached, Notion is excellent; as a reader, it's an approximation. Ex-Omnivore users who mostly collected reference links will settle in fine. Those who actually read their queue every evening should consider a dedicated read-it-later tool instead.
