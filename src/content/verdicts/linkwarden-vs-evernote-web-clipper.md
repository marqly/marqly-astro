Scope is the divider. Linkwarden is a bookmark manager that takes preservation seriously: each saved link is captured as a screenshot, PDF, and readable copy, organized into collections, optionally auto-tagged by AI, and searchable in full text. Evernote Web Clipper is the capture arm of a much bigger machine — a note platform whose search reads text inside images via OCR and inside PDFs on paid plans, and which since January 2026 includes an AI assistant that chats across everything you've stored. One is a specialized archive; the other is a general-purpose second brain with an excellent clipper attached.

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- Links are the unit of your work and you want them to outlive their sources — automatic multi-format archiving is the core feature.
- Budget matters: $3/month per user hosted, or free and fully featured on your own server, against Evernote's $99–$249.99 per year.
- You want open source, real exports, and the option to leave anytime.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Clips are one ingredient among notes, documents, and screenshots that must be searchable together, OCR included.
- Capture richness counts: five browsers, plus full-page, article, selection, bookmark, and annotated-screenshot modes with clip-time tags and notebooks.
- The v11 AI layer — chat over your notes, semantic search, meeting notes — would earn its keep.

Watch the traps on both sides. Evernote's free plan is effectively a demo at 50 notes, one notebook, and one device, and even paid plans won't auto-tag a clip — filing stays manual. Linkwarden, for its part, has no AI beyond tagging: no summaries, no chat, no semantic search, and no reading queue either. For saving and safeguarding links at a fair price, Linkwarden is the cleaner answer. For a paid, all-in workspace where clipped pages join a lifetime of notes, Evernote remains the deeper tool.
