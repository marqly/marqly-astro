Toby and Obsidian Web Clipper barely speak the same language. Toby's vocabulary is sessions, spaces, and teams: open tabs saved into visual collections on the new-tab page, restored tomorrow, or handed to a colleague via a link that needs no account. Obsidian's is vaults, files, and permanence: web pages distilled into Markdown you own outright, filed by templates, highlighted passage by passage, never touching anyone's cloud. One coordinates attention; the other accumulates knowledge.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Your browser is a shared workspace — project tab sets that teammates open, extend, and restore all week.
- Friction kills your habit: one click banks a whole session, and Toby AI names and groups tabs without configuration.
- You want reach without setup — Chrome, Edge, and Firefox extensions, a web app, and iOS/Android companions, versus Obsidian's requirement that you run its app at all.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- What pages say matters more than where they live — full content lands in your vault as searchable, permanent plain text.
- Privacy and ownership are hard requirements: local files, open source, zero telemetry stakes, and AI (the Interpreter) runnable on a free local Ollama model.
- Cost sensitivity is real — Obsidian stays free at any scale, while Toby's 60-tab ceiling arrives fast for a genuine tab hoarder.

Bottom line: an odd-couple matchup with a clean resolution. If your problem statement contains the words "team," "session," or "too many tabs," Toby is the answer and Obsidian would frustrate you. If it contains "keep," "search," or "mine," Obsidian's clipper wins and Toby's link-only collections would quietly lose your material to link rot. Name the problem first; the tool follows.
