The real question here is destination: your server or your workspace. Karakeep treats saved pages as a first-class library — AI-tagged, fully archived, searchable by text and meaning — hosted on hardware you control. Notion Web Clipper treats them as raw material for a workspace, dropping pages into databases where they sit beside projects and tasks and inherit Notion's tables, filters, and views. One is a dedicated instrument; the other is a feed into a general-purpose system.

**Choose Karakeep if:**

- Retrieval is the point — automatic AI tagging, summaries, and semantic search beat manually curated database properties for finding things months later.
- You want the content preserved, not just the link: Karakeep stores page copies, screenshots, and PDFs, while Notion's conversion can silently drop formatting.
- Open source and self-hosting appeal, along with a REST API, webhooks, and an MCP server for automation.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- Your team and your notes already run on Notion — a reading list inside the workspace you open every day beats a better one you never check.
- Database organization is a feature for you, not a chore: clipped links gain properties, filters, and custom views.
- You want zero infrastructure — no Docker, no API keys, and offline mode on all plans since 2025.

Bottom line: Notion's clipper is deliberately minimal, and Notion is fine with that — the investment goes into the workspace, with meaningful AI reserved for the $20/member/month Business tier. Karakeep inverts those priorities, pouring everything into capture, archiving, and search while asking you to be your own sysadmin. Notion loyalists should clip to Notion; everyone else who cares about their saved web content will be better served by Karakeep.
