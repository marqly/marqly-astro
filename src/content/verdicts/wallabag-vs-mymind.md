Few pairings in this space are this stark. Wallabag is French open-source infrastructure, built since 2013, that you can run on your own server; it strips articles to text, queues them, and pushes them to your e-reader. mymind is a designed object — a single flowing canvas of cards where AI recognizes the objects, colors, brands, and text inside every image you save, and where folders are banned on principle. One optimizes for ownership and reading; the other for beauty and effortless recall.

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- Your saves are articles and you read them — clean reader view, offline apps, notes on selected text, ePub export.
- You're migrating from elsewhere: it imports Pocket, Instapaper, and browser bookmark files, which mymind refuses to do at all.
- Control matters — self-host for free, or pay €11/year with every feature included.

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save screenshots, design inspiration, products, and quotes more than long-form text, and want to find them by describing what you remember.
- You want zero organizing effort — AI tags everything; there is nothing to file.
- You'll pay for polish and privacy: no ads, no tracking, no social features, membership-funded.

The costs of each philosophy are real. Wallabag's interface is plainly dated, its parser stumbles on script-heavy pages, and there's no AI anywhere. mymind's walls cut both ways: no bulk import means starting from scratch, nothing can ever be shared or collaborated on, the free plan stops at 100 cards, and the AI everyone praises requires at least the $7.99/month Student of Life plan — with summaries, reading mode, and the PDF analyzer held back for $12.99/month Mastermind. Export is a one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge. Readers and self-hosters should take Wallabag without hesitation; visual thinkers who feel that trade is worth it will love mymind anyway.
