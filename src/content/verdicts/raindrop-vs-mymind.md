These two tools embody opposite philosophies of saving. Raindrop.io is a filing cabinet: nested collections, tags, covers, and view modes you control, backed by an unusually generous free plan and apps on every platform. mymind refuses the cabinet entirely — everything lands as a card on one visual canvas, AI tags it automatically, and you find things later by describing them. One rewards diligence; the other forbids it.

**Choose Raindrop.io if:**

- You have an existing library to bring — it imports from Pocket, browser HTML, and most competitors, while mymind supports no bulk import at all
- You publish curated lists; Raindrop's public collections expose a whole board, whereas mymind sharing is limited to individual cards rather than collaborative collections
- Budget matters: unlimited free bookmarks versus a 100-card cap, and $28/year Pro versus $79–129/year for mymind's higher tiers

**Choose mymind if:**

- You save more images, screenshots, and inspiration than articles — its AI reads objects, colors, brands, and text inside pictures, something Raindrop cannot do
- Manual filing is exactly what you want to escape; mymind's zero-folder approach means organization simply happens
- Privacy is a selling point: no ads, no tracking, no social layer, funded purely by memberships

The bottom line: Raindrop.io is the safer and dramatically cheaper pick for the typical bookmarker, and the only viable one if you need bulk import, export flexibility, or public collections. mymind is a niche product executed beautifully — designers and visual collectors who live inside it tend to love it — but its deliberate walls (no bulk import, individual-card sharing rather than collaborative boards, one-way CSV export from desktop only) make it a commitment rather than a utility. Try mymind's free 100-card plan to see if the philosophy clicks; if you find yourself wanting folders, Raindrop was the answer all along.

*Raindrop feature check, October 6, 2026: [Stella's official documentation](https://help.raindrop.io/stella) confirms meaning-based retrieval, library chat, article and YouTube summaries, and user-approved organization in the Pro beta.*
