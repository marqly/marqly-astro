The clippers here are proxies for a deeper choice: cloud workspace or local vault. Notion Web Clipper is a thin funnel into a collaborative platform — clips become database pages with properties and views, synced to every device, shareable with a team, sitting one click from your tasks and wiki. Obsidian Web Clipper is a rich funnel into a private one — clips become Markdown files with remarkable capture tooling on the way in, and total ownership once they land.

**Choose Notion Web Clipper if:**

- Your workspace is the destination: reading lists gain database structure and live beside projects other people can see and edit.
- Sync should be free and automatic — Notion moves clips across all your devices at no cost, while Obsidian asks $4/month for the same.
- You may want workspace-wide AI later; Notion bundles chat, summaries, and AI search into its $20/member/month Business plan.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- The moment of capture matters — highlighter mode, per-site templates, and clip-time AI prompts embarrass Notion's bare destination picker.
- Files you own beat pages you rent: plain Markdown on disk carries zero lock-in and no dependence on a company's roadmap.
- You clip from your phone — the Safari extension on iOS and iPadOS clips straight to your vault, where Notion relies on share-sheet saves.

Bottom line: judged purely as clippers, Obsidian's is the better piece of software and it costs nothing. But clippers are never used purely — they serve the system behind them. Notion people should accept the weaker capture for the stronger workspace; Obsidian people already know which side they are on. If you belong to neither camp, start with Obsidian: free, reversible, and yours.
