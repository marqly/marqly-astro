Mozilla sunset Pocket in July 2025, handing Toby this contest on a technicality — but Toby competes in a different event. It's a visual tab manager built around the browser's new-tab page: open tabs save into drag-and-drop collections, whole sessions stash and restore in one click, and collections group into spaces you can share with teammates or publish as public links that need no Toby account. Pocket was about reading content later; Toby is about managing working sessions now.

**Choose Pocket if**

- Off the table since 2025 — the service closed in July and the export window that October.
- Only its exported CSVs outlived it.

**Choose Toby if**

- You work out of dozens of tabs and want them saved, restored, and organized visually — with Toby AI naming and grouping them automatically.
- Your team needs shared link collections: member invites, org-wide spaces, and SSO on the Team plan are features almost nothing else in this space offers.
- You want reach beyond Chrome — extensions for Edge and Firefox, a web app, and companion apps on iOS and Android.

For a former Pocket user, though, the gaps are structural. Toby stores links only — no page content, no offline reading, no highlights, no full-text search — so a dead URL means the content is simply gone, the very fragility Pocket's readers never had to think about. The free Starter plan also caps at 60 saved tabs total, with paid plans priced per member at $4.50/month billed yearly. The bottom line: teams drowning in shared tabs get real value here; solo readers mourning Pocket should route their export to Instapaper, Matter, or Raindrop.io and let Toby stay what it is — a session manager, not a library.
