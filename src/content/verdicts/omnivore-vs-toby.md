Omnivore's story ended in November 2024: an ElevenLabs acqui-hire, a one-month export window, then deletion of everything left on the servers. Toby, running since 2016 and still shipping updates as of June 2026, is the survivor here — but it plays a different sport. Omnivore was built for reading: parsed articles, highlights, offline apps, powerful search. Toby is built for working: open tabs saved into visual collections on your new-tab page, whole sessions stashed and restored in one click, and spaces shared with teammates or published as public links.

**Choose an Omnivore fork if:**

- You want a true reading library — article text, labels, highlights — and have the self-hosting chops for a PostgreSQL-and-Elasticsearch stack kept alive by volunteers.
- Unlimited free saves matter; forks inherit Omnivore's no-cost model, while Toby's free plan stops at 60 saved tabs.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Your browser is your workspace and you juggle projects as tab sessions you'll want back tomorrow.
- You work with others — shared spaces, member invites, and org-wide collections are rare in this category, with SSO on the Team plan.
- You want AI to do the sorting: Toby AI names, groups, and organizes open tabs into collections automatically.

The gap to respect is content. Toby stores links, not pages — no archiving, no highlights, no full-text search, so anything behind a dead URL is lost. Omnivore kept the words themselves. A former Omnivore user who mostly stockpiled work-in-progress tabs will find Toby a genuine upgrade with a team dimension Omnivore never had. One who actually read their queue each night will find Toby beside the point and should shortlist a living read-it-later app. Either way, staying on a shut-down platform's fork is only sensible for dedicated self-hosters.
