Both tools understand that browsers overflow, but they rescue different things. Toby rescues your working state: sessions of open tabs become visual collections on the new-tab page, restorable in one click, grouped into spaces you share with teammates or publish as links anyone can view without an account. Linkwarden rescues the pages themselves: every save is archived as a screenshot, PDF, and readable copy, auto-taggable by AI, and searchable in full text years later. Toby is a workbench; Linkwarden is a vault.

**Choose Toby if:**

- Save-and-restore of whole tab sessions is your daily rhythm — it's the best-in-class workflow here, and Linkwarden doesn't attempt it.
- Your team runs on shared context: spaces, member invites, org-wide collections, and SSO on the $8/member Team plan.
- A visual new-tab homepage beats a database of links for how you think.

**Choose Linkwarden if:**

- Links must survive: Toby keeps bare URLs with no archiving or full-text search, so dead pages take their content with them.
- You're solo and counting dollars — $3/month (or free self-hosted, open source) versus Toby's per-member pricing and 60-tab free cap.
- Research habits demand highlights, annotations, and search across what pages actually said.

The free tiers tell you who each product wants. Toby's Starter plan stops at 60 saved tabs — enough to feel the workflow, not enough to live in — and its 2024 forced migration of legacy free users still echoes in a 4.2-star Chrome rating. Linkwarden has no free cloud at all, but self-hosting grants every feature forever. A team that opens the same twenty tabs each sprint should buy Toby and stop fighting it. Anyone building a durable personal reference library should run — or rent — Linkwarden and let Toby's sessions stay ephemeral.
