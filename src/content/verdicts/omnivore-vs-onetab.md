First, the fact that ends the contest: Omnivore closed in November 2024 after an ElevenLabs acqui-hire, deleting all hosted data that wasn't exported in time. OneTab has been quietly alive since 2013 and even shipped a full v2 rewrite in 2026. Yet these tools never competed for the same job. Omnivore captured article content — parsed text you could read offline, highlight, and search. OneTab captures a moment: every open tab collapses into a list of URLs and titles, stored locally in your browser, and nothing more.

**Choose an Omnivore fork if:**

- Reading is the point — you want a clean article view, labels, highlights, and full-text search, and you're prepared to self-host a demanding volunteer-maintained stack.
- You need your saves on a phone; forks focus on the web, but OneTab has no mobile story at all.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- Your real problem is 60 open tabs eating memory right now — one click parks them all, with claimed memory savings of up to 95%.
- You want zero commitment: free, no account, no limits, and tab URLs never leave your machine unless you share a list.
- You'd rather trust a tool with a 13-year track record and two million Chrome users than a community fork.

There's an irony worth naming: both products charged nothing, but only one survived it. Omnivore's free-with-no-revenue model ended the moment its founders moved on; OneTab's tiny scope has kept it running since 2013. Still, don't confuse survival with equivalence. OneTab stores no page content, offers no search of what's inside pages, and a dead link means the content is simply gone. If Omnivore was your reading queue, OneTab is not its heir — pick an actively maintained read-it-later app and let OneTab handle the tab avalanche.
