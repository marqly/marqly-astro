This is renting a second brain versus owning a filing drawer. Evernote Web Clipper feeds a managed cloud platform that does heavy lifting for you — OCR across images, search inside PDFs, an AI assistant that converses with twenty years of product maturity behind it. Obsidian Web Clipper converts the web into plain Markdown on your own disk, where nothing is done for you and nothing is ever taken from you. Both capture well; they differ on who holds the archive and who pays whom.

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Your archive mixes web clips with scanned documents, PDFs, and images whose text must be searchable — Evernote's OCR has no Obsidian equivalent.
- You want a backend that just works: sync across web, Windows, Mac, iOS, and Android on paid plans, with no vault management.
- Chatting with your archive appeals — the v11 AI assistant answers questions across your notes, while Obsidian offers only clip-time AI prompts.

**Choose Obsidian Web Clipper if:**

- Zero cost and zero lock-in outrank convenience: free forever, plain files, no subscription creep — a real concern given Evernote's repeated price hikes under Bending Spoons.
- You capture passages, not pages — highlighter mode saves exactly what you marked, something Evernote cannot do on live pages.
- Automation via per-site templates and a bring-your-own-model Interpreter (including free local Ollama) fits your tinkering instincts.

Bottom line: recommend Obsidian first. It costs nothing, respects your data completely, and its capture tooling now rivals the paid incumbent. Move to Evernote only when a specific need — OCR, PDF search, managed sync, archive-wide chat — is worth up to $249.99 a year, and know that its 50-note free plan will not let you trial it seriously.
