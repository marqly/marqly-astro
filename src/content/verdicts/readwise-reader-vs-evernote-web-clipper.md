This matchup splits on what happens after you save. Readwise Reader treats a saved page as something to process: it lands in a triage inbox alongside newsletters, RSS, PDFs, and YouTube transcripts, gets read (with Ghostreader AI on call for summaries and questions), gets highlighted, and its best passages leave for Notion or Obsidian. Evernote Web Clipper treats a saved page as something to keep: five browsers' worth of capture modes — full page, simplified article, selection, or annotated screenshot, with tags and a destination notebook chosen at clip time — feeding a twenty-year-old archive whose search reads text inside images and, on paid plans, inside PDFs, now with a v11 AI assistant that chats across everything.

**Choose Readwise Reader if:**

- Reading is the job: keyboard-first triage, offline apps, and the category's best highlight-to-notes pipeline
- You want YouTube transcripts and ePub support in the same queue as your articles
- $119.88/year sounds better than Evernote Advanced's $249.99

**Choose Evernote Web Clipper if:**

- Your saves are reference, not reading — screenshots, receipts, PDFs, and pages you'll search for years later
- OCR search inside images and documents would genuinely get used
- You already live in Evernote; the clipper is the best front door to it

Bottom line: neither is honestly free — Reader has a 30-day trial and no free tier, while Evernote's free plan (50 notes, one notebook, one device) is effectively a demo. So pick by verb. If you save pages to read them, Reader repays its subscription daily. If you save pages to find them again in 2029, Evernote's heavyweight backend is what you're actually paying for.
