Confusing these two categories costs people their content, so the distinction matters. OneTab is a pressure valve: one click collapses every open tab into a local list of URLs and titles, reclaiming (by the developer's estimate) up to 95% of browser memory. Wallabag is storage: it fetches each page's article text, files it with tags, and keeps it readable offline, exportable to ePub, and searchable — on your own server if you choose. OneTab remembers that a page existed; Wallabag keeps what it said.

**Choose OneTab if:**

- The emergency is now — dozens of tabs, a wheezing laptop, and no desire to create an account or learn a system.
- Privacy through absence appeals: your tab URLs stay in the browser profile and are never transmitted anywhere unless you share a list.
- Free must mean free — no tiers, no caps, no upsell, since 2013.

**Choose Wallabag if:**

- Saved pages have value later: full-text search, tags, annotations, and offline mobile apps make a real library.
- You need access beyond one browser profile — web app, iOS, Android, e-reader.
- Link rot is unacceptable; OneTab's bare URL dies with the page it points to.

OneTab's fragility is the thing to respect. Uninstalling the extension deletes your stored tabs, clearing browser data can wipe them, there's no sync yet (the encrypted version is still "coming soon"), and there's no phone access at all. Wallabag's costs are of the opposite kind: a server to maintain or €11/year to pay, and a parser that occasionally fumbles complex pages. Honestly, these tools compose rather than compete — park tabs in OneTab, promote the ones worth keeping into Wallabag. But if you're picking one and your saves have any future value, pick the one that stores content.
