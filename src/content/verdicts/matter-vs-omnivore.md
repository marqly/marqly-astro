Start with the fact that decides everything: Omnivore no longer exists as a service. In late October 2024 its team announced they were joining ElevenLabs, and the hosted product shut down the following month — accounts closed, and any data not exported by November 30, 2024 was permanently deleted. Matter, by contrast, is alive and busy: a design-forward read-later app for iPhone, iPad, Mac, and the web, actively updated by a small subscription-funded team, with the strongest text-to-speech in the category, Gmail newsletter delivery, and time-synced transcription of YouTube videos and podcasts.

**Choose Matter if:**

- You want a read-later app that exists — with a free tier covering unlimited saves, strong parsing, and unlimited tags
- You're in the Apple ecosystem and value listening: human-like HD voices (Premium, $8/month or $60/year on the web) turn any article into audio
- Highlight syncing to Notion, Obsidian, and Readwise covers your note-taking

**Choose an Omnivore fork only if:**

- You're a committed self-hoster willing to run PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, and multiple microservices for a volunteer-maintained project
- Absolute data ownership outweighs polish, working mobile apps, and support

Bottom line: Omnivore's story is the strongest argument in this comparison. It was free, open source, and well loved — and free-with-no-revenue is exactly why it could vanish overnight when the team moved on. Matter's subscription model is the boring, durable alternative, though Android users should note it's Apple-first and offers no public API. For nearly everyone who used to love Omnivore, the practical successor is a living app, and between these two that's Matter without contest.
