Teachers do not have a filing problem, they have a workload problem. ## 2. Diigo — best free plan for verified educators

Diigo is the honest answer to "what is free for teachers." It publishes a free Teacher plan for verified educators that includes a class console, which is a real education product rather than a consumer tier that happens to be free. Its core strength is also the most classroom-shaped one here: multi-colour highlighting and sticky notes placed directly on live web pages, with groups that let a class annotate a shared library together. Outliners turn those highlights into a structured research document, which is a genuinely useful assignment format. Data stays portable through CSV, HTML and RSS export plus a web API.

The trade is that Diigo has no AI of any kind — no auto-tagging, no summaries, no semantic search — and it is visibly coasting. Its Chrome extension was last updated in 2024, the Android app has left Google Play, and the official blog domain is dead. The free tier is ad-supported and caps lifetime bookmarks and highlights, and Diigo's own pages state that limit inconsistently (500–1,000 bookmarks, 100–200 highlights). Cached pages, PDF annotation and full-text search are all paid, at $40/year.

**Who it's best for:** annotation-driven classes where students mark up a shared text, and any educator for whom free-for-teachers outweighs modern tooling. **Skip it if** you want retrieval by meaning, current apps, or an ad-free free tier.

- **Pros:** free Teacher plan for verified educators with a class console; live-page highlighting and sticky notes; collaborative group libraries; cheap paid tiers; portable exports and a web API.
- **Cons:** no AI at all; neglected product with a stale extension and no Android app; ad-supported free tier with limits its own pricing pages contradict; full-text search paywalled.
- **Price:** free (ad-supported, capped) · Standard $40/year · Professional $59/year · Business $10/user/month.

## 3. Raindrop.io — best free tier for volume

If the reason your bookmarks bar collapsed is sheer count, Raindrop.io is the cheapest fix: its free plan gives unlimited bookmarks, unlimited collections, and unlimited devices with no time limit, plus up to three highlights per bookmark and 100 MB/month of uploads. That is the most generous free tier in the category and it is a complete product rather than a demo. Public collections also make sharing a curated reading list as easy as sending a link, so the student-access problem is solved here too, just without Marqly's board-as-page framing.

What Raindrop does not do is find things for you. Organisation is world-class, but retrieval still depends on the tags and keywords you typed yourself; there is no semantic search at any price. Pro ($28/year) adds full-text search, Stella AI save-time tag and collection suggestions, permanent page copies, annotations with unlimited highlights in four colours, and daily backups — useful, and cheaper than Marqly Pro, but still keyword-shaped rather than meaning-shaped.

**Who it's best for:** teachers who save hundreds of links and want them organised beautifully for free, and who are willing to tag as they go. **Skip it if** you want a save to be findable later by describing it, or if you want video transcripts kept with the save.

- **Pros:** unlimited free bookmarks, collections and devices; polished apps on every platform including Mac, Windows and Linux; strong visual organisation with covers and nested collections; public collection sharing; Pro is the cheapest paid tier here.
- **Cons:** no semantic search at any price; capture is link-first, so video transcripts and article text are not kept; three-highlight cap on free.
- **Price:** free (unlimited bookmarks) · Pro $28/year or $3.25/month.

## 4. Notion Web Clipper — best if your school already runs Notion

For a school that already lives in Notion, the clipper is the path of least resistance: it is free, it works with any Notion account including the generous free plan, and clips land in databases, so every saved link inherits Notion's properties, views, filters and tags. A unit-planning database with a resource view is a genuinely good teacher artefact, and shared Notion pages solve the student-access problem without a second tool. Offline mode arrived in 2025 on all plans.

Two caveats matter for a classroom. The clipper itself is bare-bones — no tags, notes, highlights or screenshots at clip time, just a destination picker — and Notion's own documentation warns that clip fidelity varies and formatting can go missing, which is a real risk when you are capturing a worksheet. And the AI everyone wants is not in the plan most schools have: meaningful AI search and chat requires the Business plan at $20/member/month. Official browser support has also narrowed to Chrome and Safari.

**The education discount excludes most school teachers, which is worth stating plainly.** Notion's free Education Plan is for individual students and teachers at **accredited colleges and universities**. Its own eligibility page says you are *not* eligible if you are "a K-12 student or K-12 educator" — you can still use the ordinary Free Plan. It also requires a school email address from an institution listed in the World Higher Education Database (student IDs and other documentation are not accepted), the workspace is limited to **one member** with up to 100 guests, each school email can claim it once, and you must re-verify your status every year. Upgrading that workspace to Plus, Business or Enterprise permanently ends its education eligibility. For a primary or secondary school teacher, therefore, Notion is a paid decision rather than a free one.

**Who it's best for:** higher-education staff and students who qualify for the free Education Plan, and Notion-native schools already paying for it that want resources beside their notes, projects and tasks. **Skip it if** you teach K-12 and were counting on the free education plan, if you need reliable clip fidelity for formatted worksheets, or if AI search is the point and you are not on Business.

- **Pros:** clipper is free and works on the free plan; database properties give you views and filters for free; one workspace for notes, tasks and resources; official API and mobile share-sheet saving; offline on all plans; free Education Plan for eligible college staff and students.
- **Cons:** **the free Education Plan excludes K-12 educators and students**; it is one member per workspace with annual re-verification; no tags, notes or highlights at capture time; documented clip-fidelity problems; AI needs the $20/member/month Business plan; browser support narrowed to Chrome and Safari.
- **Price:** clipper free · Plus $10/member/month · Business $20/member/month (billed annually) · free Education Plan for individuals at accredited colleges and universities only.

## 5. Pinterest — best for visual subjects, worst for finding things again

For art, design, elementary classroom display, and any subject where the resource *is* an image, nothing here competes with Pinterest's discovery. Related pins and Lens visual search turn one save into a stream of similar ideas, boards organise visually with sections and collaborators, and there are no caps on pins or boards. It is entirely free, and it has the broadest platform coverage in this space.

It is also a social discovery product, not a private bookmark manager, and that distinction costs teachers real time. Promoted pins appear roughly every four to six pins with no paid way to remove them, so a planning session drifts into a feed. Link rot is endemic — pins routinely outlive the pages they point to or lead to spam blogs, which is exactly the failure you cannot afford the morning a lesson depends on a resource. Retrieval of your own saves is weak: keyword search over titles and descriptions only, with no tags and no full-text search. There is no text capture and no highlighting.

**Who it's best for:** visual subjects and idea discovery, as a complement to a real bookmark manager. **Skip it as your primary system** if you need to find a specific saved page again reliably.

- **Pros:** unmatched visual discovery; free with no caps; excellent visual board organisation; broadest platform support.
- **Cons:** ads roughly every 4–6 pins with no way to remove them; endemic link rot; weak retrieval of your own saves; no text capture or highlighting; awkward for articles.
- **Price:** free and ad-supported. There is no paid consumer tier.

## 6. Evernote Web Clipper — best if you are already in Evernote

Evernote's clipper is the most capable capture tool here: it works on all five major browsers, lets you add tags, remarks and a destination notebook at clip time, and offers full page, simplified article, selection, bookmark, and annotated screenshot modes. Its search is best-in-class, with OCR that reads text inside images and paid plans that search inside PDFs and documents. Evernote v11 added real AI — an assistant that chats across your notes, semantic search, and AI meeting notes.

The problem for a teacher is the free plan, which is effectively a demo: 50 notes, one notebook, one device. Sustained use requires paying, and Evernote Starter is $14.99/month or $99/year with Advanced at $24.99/month or $249.99/year — among the priciest in the category, with prices raised repeatedly under owner Bending Spoons. There is no automatic tagging, so clips still need manual filing, and you cannot highlight text on live web pages; markup happens inside notes after clipping.

**Who it's best for:** teachers already paying for Evernote who want web content, PDFs and screenshots searchable beside their notes. **Skip it if** you are choosing a bookmark manager on merit and budget — a heavyweight note platform is overkill for saving and finding links.

- **Pros:** five-browser clipper support; tags and destination at clip time; OCR and PDF search; real AI in v11; mature apps with offline on paid plans.
- **Cons:** free plan capped at 50 notes, one notebook, one device; expensive; no auto-tagging; no live-page highlighting; overkill for bookmarking alone.
- **Price:** free (50 notes) · Starter $14.99/month or $99/year · Advanced $24.99/month or $249.99/year.

## 7. Browser bookmarks — the free baseline every school already has

Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari bookmarks are free, instant, sync within their own ecosystem, and need no training. For a few dozen links that is a legitimate answer, and pretending otherwise wastes a teacher's evening.

They break at exactly the scale a school year creates. Organisation is folder-only, so a resource either lives in one place or gets duplicated; search is title-only, so the page you saved as "Untitled" is gone; nothing is captured, so a dead link takes the resource with it; and there is no way to find a page by describing it or to hand a collection to students without exporting a file and emailing it. Every other tool in this table exists because of those four gaps. If you are already past them, [import your browser bookmarks](/migrate) and keep the bar for the twenty links you use weekly.

**Who it's best for:** a small, stable set of links you use every week. **Skip it** the moment you are saving for next year's unit or sharing with a class.

- **Pros:** free; already installed; no learning curve; syncs inside its own ecosystem.
- **Cons:** folder-only organisation; title-only search; no capture, so link rot deletes the resource; not shareable with students.
- **Price:** free.

## What actually matters across a school year

Three failure modes decide whether a teacher's bookmark system survives to June, and none of them are about features.

**Filing cost.** Any system that asks for a decision at save time gets abandoned during a planning binge. This is why auto-tagging matters more than a clever folder taxonomy, and why Marqly gates it behind Pro while Raindrop asks you to build collections by hand and Evernote asks you to pick a notebook every time.

**Retrieval lag.** The gap between saving a resource and needing it is often ten months. Title-only search fails at that distance because you do not remember titles. Semantic search — describing "the interactive fraction widget" and getting the right save back — is the only capability in this table that closes the gap, and it is Pro-only in both Marqly and Evernote.

**Link rot.** A resource that 404s the morning you teach it is worse than one you never saved. Permanent page copies (Raindrop Pro), local save-as-PDF (Marqly, processed in Chrome and Edge), and cached pages (Diigo paid) are the three real defences here; browser bookmarks and Pinterest offer none, and Pinterest's link rot is endemic enough to plan around.
