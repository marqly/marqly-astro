**The short answer:** there is no single best tab manager in 2026 — there are two different jobs hiding under one label. If you keep 40 tabs open because you're **switching between projects**, you want a workspace manager: **Workona** (or **Toby** if you think in visual boards). If you keep 40 tabs open because you're **afraid you'll never find these pages again**, you want a tab saver: **Marqly**, which files closed tabs into a searchable library. **OneTab** is the best free panic button, and **Session Buddy** is crash insurance. Buy for the job you actually have.

## Workona — best workspace switcher

Workona treats your browser like an operating system: each project gets a workspace with its own tabs, docs, and app integrations, and switching projects swaps your whole tab set in one click. For consultants, founders, and anyone running parallel projects, it's the most complete tool in the category — and the one this roundup's other tools can't imitate.

The free plan now caps you at **5 workspaces** (it used to be more — Workona has tightened limits over the years), and Pro runs about **$8/month**. That's real money for a tab tool, but if you live in workspaces all day it buys back genuine focus.

**Pros:**

- True workspace switching — tab sets, docs, and cloud apps per project
- Tabs auto-save as you work; close the window and the workspace survives
- Syncs across devices via your account

**Cons:**

- 5-workspace free cap arrives fast for multi-project people
- ~$8/month is the priciest recurring cost here
- Heavyweight; there's a learning curve and a resource footprint

## OneTab — best free one-click declutter

OneTab has one move: click the icon, and every open tab collapses into a single list page, freeing memory immediately. It's free, it's been doing exactly this for over a decade, and it's still actively maintained in 2026. As a panic button, it's unbeatable.

As a *system*, it's where tabs go to be forgotten. The list has no tags, no meaningful search, no sync across devices — after a few months you have a thousand-line scroll of half-remembered links, which is the same problem you started with, relocated.

**Pros:**

- Completely free; no account, no cloud, no upsell
- Instant memory relief — one click and the tab bar is empty
- Dead simple; nothing to learn

**Cons:**

- No sync, no tags, no real search — retrieval is scroll-and-pray
- The list becomes its own graveyard at scale
- Local-only: lose the browser profile, lose the list (export early, export often)

## Toby — best for visual collection-builders

Toby replaces your new-tab page with visual boards of saved tabs, organized into collections — closer to Pinterest than to a list. It's pleasant, fast, and popular with teams that share collections of links (client resources, sprint docs). The catch arrived with its subscription model: the **free plan caps saved tabs (currently around 60)**, which serious users hit within weeks; the paid plan starts at **$4.50/month billed annually**, with a pricier team tier.

**Pros:**

- The nicest visual organization in the category
- Collections are shareable — genuinely useful for teams
- Cross-device sync via account

**Cons:**

- Free plan's saved-tab cap makes it a trial in practice
- Organization is manual — you drag tabs into collections yourself
- Search is basic; finding an old save relies on remembering which board you filed it on

## Session Buddy — best crash insurance

Session Buddy snapshots your browser sessions so a crash, restart, or accidental window-close doesn't cost you a tab set. It's free, Manifest V3-compliant, and still maintained in 2026 (v4.x). One honest caution: the 2024 v4 migration infamously lost some users' saved sessions, and the support forums remember. Treat it as a safety net, not an archive — and export anything you'd cry about.

**Pros:**

- Free, and does session snapshot/restore better than anything else
- Search across saved sessions; organize and edit them after the fact
- MV3-compliant and updated through 2026

**Cons:**

- Local storage — no cloud sync; a lost profile can mean lost sessions
- The v4 migration data-loss episode dented trust
- It preserves tab *sets*; it doesn't help you find one specific page by topic later

## Can you combine them?

Yes — and most heavy tab users end up with a two-tool stack, because the two jobs never stopped being different:

- **Workona + Marqly** is the power combo: workspaces hold the tabs you're actively working in, and anything interesting-but-not-urgent gets saved to Marqly instead of squatting in a workspace forever. Workspaces stay lean; nothing gets lost.
- **OneTab + Marqly** works for minimalists: OneTab as the instant panic button during a heavy session, Marqly's save-all-tabs for anything you actually want back. The tell is simple — if you'd be annoyed to lose it, it doesn't belong in a OneTab list.
- **Session Buddy + anything** is cheap insurance regardless of your main tool, since it protects the tabs you *haven't* saved yet.

The combination to avoid is two tools doing the same job — Toby *and* Workona, or OneTab *and* Session Buddy as your archive. You'll split your saves across two places and trust neither.

## How to choose

1. **You switch between client/project contexts all day** → Workona. Nothing else here does workspaces.
2. **You want the mess gone right now, for free** → OneTab. Accept that the list is write-only.
3. **You think visually or share link collections with a team** → Toby, budgeting for the paid plan once the ~60-tab cap bites.
4. **You've been burned by crashes** → Session Buddy, plus periodic exports.
5. **Your open tabs are things you intend to read or use later** → Marqly. Save them all in one click and let search-by-meaning handle "later."

OneTab and Session Buddy remain genuinely free, but both are local-first — the price you pay is sync and durability rather than dollars. Budget for one paid tool in whichever job matters more to you, and let free tools cover the other.
