When Mozilla turned off Pocket's servers on **July 8, 2025**, and closed the final export window on **October 8, 2025**, it marked the end of an 18-year era. More than 20 million users who relied on the little red icon to save articles, recipes, and videos were cut loose.

Mozilla did not build a successor. Instead, the market fragmented into specialized tools. Some former users wanted an exact clone of Pocket's clean reading view; others wanted a permanent knowledge base with modern AI; and privacy-conscious users wanted self-hosted servers they control.

We tested seven prominent replacements across six key criteria: **export file ingestion, distraction-free reading, organization effort, search intelligence, platform availability, and pricing**. Here is how they rank by job-to-be-done.

## 1. Marqly — Best for AI Organization & Semantic Search

If Pocket's biggest flaw was that it became a "read-never" graveyard where you saved thousands of links you could never find again, Marqly is the direct upgrade.

Marqly imports legacy Pocket export files (`ril_export.html` or CSV) in about two minutes with your original tags and timestamps intact. Once imported, Marqly's AI does the filing: it automatically generates concise summaries and indexes content semantically.

The critical difference is **search by paraphrase**: instead of guessing the exact title keyword you saved in 2022, you search by concept (*"that essay on the history of typography"* or *"the video showing sourdough hydration techniques"*). Marqly finds the right save even with zero exact word matches.

- **Reader Mode:** Distraction-free clean reader view with text highlighting in 6 colors.
- **YouTube Integration:** Saves YouTube videos with synchronized transcripts and in-page AI chat.
- **Pricing:** Free tier stores up to 2,000 bookmarks with whole-library search; Pro is $72/year ($6/mo) or $39 for the standing first-year offer.
- **Best for:** Anyone who wants their Pocket backlog organized automatically without sorting into folders.

## 2. Instapaper — Best Pure Article Reading Experience

For readers whose only requirement is a quiet, minimalist reading screen, Instapaper remains the most direct spiritual successor to Pocket.

Instapaper keeps things deliberately simple: save an article from your browser extension or mobile share sheet, open it in an uncluttered reading view with customizable typography, and read offline. It also supports wireless Kindle digest delivery.

- **Where it wins:** Typography, speed reading mode, and automatic Kindle syncing.
- **Where it falls short:** Search is keyword-only, organization is flat (no nested folders), and it does not handle non-article saves (like videos, tweets, or web applications) well.
- **Pricing:** Free tier; Premium is $2.99/mo or $29.99/yr.
- **Best for:** Dedicated long-form readers with Kindle devices who treat their saves as a disposable reading queue.

---

## 3. Readwise Reader — Best for Power Readers & Highlight Workflows

If you consume heavy reading volumes across newsletters, Substack posts, academic PDFs, EPUBs, and RSS feeds, Readwise Reader is the power-user choice.

Reader is built like an email inbox with keyboard-first navigation (`E` to archive, `L` to read later). Its Ghostreader AI summarizes long documents, defines complex terms inline, and answers questions about the text.

- **Where it wins:** Unified inbox for RSS, PDFs, and newsletters; deep two-way sync of highlights into Obsidian and Notion.
- **Where it falls short:** Expensive ($119.88/year), no permanent free tier, and a dense, intimidating UI for casual reading.
- **Pricing:** Bundled in Readwise Full at $9.99/mo billed annually ($12.99/mo monthly).
- **Best for:** Researchers, academics, and note-takers whose reading feeds directly into PKM software.

---

## 4. Raindrop.io — Best Traditional Folder-Based Bookmark Manager

Raindrop.io is the reigning category leader for general-purpose link saving. If your primary goal is building a structured library of web bookmarks organized into nested collections, tags, and custom icons, Raindrop delivers a reliable experience across all devices.

- **Where it wins:** Polished web, desktop, and mobile apps; generous free plan; nested collections and public sharing.
- **Where it falls short:** Search is keyword-based rather than semantic; manual effort required to file links into collections.
- **Pricing:** Free tier with unlimited links; Pro is $28/year.
- **Best for:** Users who enjoy organizing by hand into structured folders and don't need AI summaries.

---

## 5. Karakeep (Formerly Hoarder) — Best Self-Hosted Solution

For privacy advocates and technical users who refuse to trust third-party clouds after Pocket's sudden shutdown, Karakeep is the leading open-source alternative.

Running as a lightweight Docker container on a home server or VPS, Karakeep provides full-page archiving (text, screenshots, PDFs, and yt-dlp video downloads), AI auto-tagging using local Ollama models or OpenAI API keys, and Meilisearch hybrid full-text search.

- **Where it wins:** 100% data ownership, local AI inference, zero subscription fees.
- **Where it falls short:** Requires Docker administration, server maintenance, and BYO API keys.
- **Pricing:** 100% free and open source. Hosted beta available at $4/mo.
- **Best for:** Self-hosters and developers wanting a private, on-device AI bookmark vault.

## The Migration Reality: Exported vs Unexported

Before choosing a replacement, check which group you belong to:

1. **You already have your Pocket export file:**
   You downloaded `ril_export.html` or `pocket-export.zip` before the October 8, 2025 deadline. You are in great shape. Follow our [Pocket to Marqly migration guide](/migrate/pocket) to import your file directly, or use our free [Pocket Export Converter](/tools/pocket-export-converter) to inspect and clean your file.
2. **You never downloaded your Pocket archive:**
   Mozilla's servers have deleted all user databases, and data recovery through Pocket is impossible. However, if you had Pocket synced with Firefox or exported bookmarks from Chrome, you can export your browser bookmarks HTML file and import that into Marqly.
