## What is mymind?

mymind calls itself "the extension for your mind" — a single private place where you throw everything you want to remember: images, articles, quotes, products, notes, screenshots. There are no folders, no tags to maintain, no filing decisions. The AI reads what you save, tags it automatically, and everything lives on one endless visual board of cards.

Two design decisions define the product. First, **no organizing, ever** — the founders treat manual filing as a bug, not a feature, and the app has no folder tree at all. Second, **absolute privacy** — no social layer, no sharing, no collaboration, no ads, no tracking. It's deliberately a party of one.

You save through browser extensions and mobile apps, and everything syncs to the web app. The aesthetic is the calmest in the category — closer to a moodboard than a database.

## Key features

- **AI auto-tagging.** Save anything and mymind identifies what it is — a book, a product, a tweet, an article about typography — and tags it without your input. In our experience this is among the better implementations of auto-tagging; it's the reason "no folders" mostly works.
- **Text recognition in images.** Screenshot a quote and mymind reads the text inside the image, so it's searchable later. Genuinely useful for people who screenshot everything.
- **Smart spaces.** Instead of folders you make, mymind assembles collections automatically around topics it detects. You can also spin up a space from a search.
- **Serendipity.** A resurfacing mode that shows you old saves you'd forgotten — pleasant, and occasionally actually useful.
- **Mastermind-tier AI.** The $12.99/month plan adds AI summaries of saved articles, a reading mode, article backup (a saved copy that survives link rot), PDF analysis, video files up to 500MB, and "Same Vibe," which finds visually similar cards.
- **Privacy by architecture.** Your mind is private, full stop. There's no way to share a board with anyone — which is either a feature or a dealbreaker depending on who you are.

What's *not* here matters too: no in-page highlighter (saving a quote means clipping it, not [highlighting it on the page](/blog/how-to-highlight-text-on-any-website)), no YouTube summaries or transcripts, no public sharing, no team features, and no API.

There's a free trial and a guest mode that's capped by storage rather than time, but no permanent free tier. Two honest observations about this table:

1. **The Bookmarker plan is a trap for this product.** Paying $4.99/month for mymind *without* AI removes the entire reason the no-folders philosophy works. Either pay for Student of Life or use something else.
2. **The features most competitors include at their base price — summaries, article backup — sit in the $129/year tier.** That's roughly 2.7× what Marqly Pro costs per year and 4.6× Raindrop Pro.

## Strengths

- **The calmest, best-designed tool in the category.** Nothing else comes close visually. If ambience affects whether you actually use a tool, that's worth real money.
- **Auto-tagging that mostly delivers on "no organizing."** The AI classification is good enough that you stop thinking about filing, which is the entire pitch.
- **Excellent for visual material.** Images, screenshots, products, design inspiration — the card canvas and image text recognition make mymind the strongest pure visual collector we've tested.
- **A principled privacy position.** No ads, no tracking, no social features, and a subscription model that means you're the customer, not the product.
- **Fast capture.** The extension and mobile apps make saving nearly frictionless.

## Weaknesses

- **No permanent free plan.** You cannot use mymind meaningfully without paying. Most competitors — Raindrop, Marqly, Glasp — have real free tiers.
- **Retrieval leans on tags and visual memory, not meaning.** mymind's search is good at "show me that red sneaker" and weaker at "the article arguing remote work needs fewer meetings." If your library is mostly long-form text you'll feel this within months. Our roundup of [AI bookmark managers](/blog/best-ai-bookmark-manager-2026) covers why the query layer is the whole game.
- **Deliberately no collaboration or sharing.** You can't share a collection with a colleague or publish a board. For some users this is the point; for others it's disqualifying.
- **The best AI costs $129/year.** Summaries and article backup are table stakes elsewhere.
- **Import and export are thin.** mymind wants you to start fresh, which is a genuine philosophy — but if you're carrying years of Pocket or Raindrop history, moving in is clumsy compared to tools built for [migrating your existing saves](/blog/best-pocket-alternatives-2026).

## Who is mymind for?

**Get mymind if** you're a designer, writer, or visual thinker; your saves are mostly images, quotes, and inspiration; you find folders genuinely stressful; and you'll happily pay for calm, private software. Within that profile, it's arguably the best tool available.

**Skip mymind if** you need a free tier, you save mostly long articles you'll need to re-find by topic, you want to share collections with anyone, or you want highlights, transcripts, and summaries at a mainstream price. That describes most bookmark-hoarders we hear from, which is why the rating lands where it does.
