## What is Raindrop.io?

Raindrop.io is an all-in-one bookmark manager that's been around since 2013 — famously built and run by a very small team around its original developer, which makes its polish and cross-platform coverage all the more impressive. It saves anything with a URL — articles, videos, PDFs, images, whole files — and organizes it into nested collections with tags, filters, and a genuinely pleasant visual interface.

It runs everywhere: web, macOS, Windows, iOS, Android, and extensions for every major browser. That "everywhere" matters — Raindrop is one of the few premium-feeling bookmark tools with a first-class Android app.

Raindrop's philosophy is a *library*, not a reading queue. It's closer to a personal Pinterest-meets-filing-cabinet than a read-it-later app, though it has a permanent-copy and highlight story too.

## Key features

### Collections, tags, and filters

The organizational core. Nested collections with custom icons and covers, multi-tag support, filters by type, domain, date, and more. If you enjoy curating a structured library — and some people genuinely do — Raindrop's tooling for it is the best in the business.

### Save everything, from anywhere

Articles, videos, images, PDFs, music links, and file uploads (100 MB/month free, 10 GB/month on Pro). The browser extensions are fast, and the mobile share-sheet integration on both iOS and Android is solid. Nothing in this category saves a wider range of stuff with less friction.

### Highlights and annotations

You can highlight text on saved pages on any plan — highlights are unlimited even on free. Pro adds notes attached to highlights (annotations). It's a capable system, though highlights live inside Raindrop's copy of the page rather than persisting on the live web page when you revisit it.

### Permanent copies and full-text search (Pro)

Pro keeps a permanent copy of every page you save, so your library survives link rot. Pro's full-text search is genuinely deep: it covers the entire content of every bookmark, including PDFs, EPUBs, and even YouTube transcripts. Within its keyword paradigm, it's excellent.

### AI assistant and suggestions (Pro)

The newest addition. Pro users get AI-suggested tags and collections when saving, and an AI assistant that can answer questions about saved content. It's a useful layer, but it's additive — the core search experience remains keyword matching, and there are no automatic summaries on saves.

### Sharing and collaboration

Public collections, shared collections with collaborators, and clean public pages. Free includes basic sharing; it's one of the better collaboration stories among personal bookmark tools.

## What Raindrop does well

- **The free plan is the best in the category.** Unlimited everything that matters, on every platform, forever. Most competitors' free tiers are trials in disguise; Raindrop's is a real product.
- **Cross-platform coverage, including Android.** Web, Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, and every major browser. Few competitors match this.
- **Media breadth.** It swallows anything — files, images, video links — where read-it-later tools only really want articles.
- **Pro is cheap and honest.** $27.99/year for permanent copies plus deep full-text search is excellent value.
- **A decade of stability.** Raindrop has been continuously developed since 2013 with export always available. As custodians of your data go, its track record is long.

## Where Raindrop falls short

- **Search matches keywords, not meaning.** This is the big one. If you remember a save as "that article about why open offices kill focus" but the title was "The war on deep work," Raindrop returns nothing. Full-text search (Pro) widens the net but it's still literal matching. Past a few hundred saves, retrieval becomes the bottleneck — we've written about [why that ceiling exists](/compare/marqly-vs-raindrop) in detail.
- **Organization is still manual by default.** Collections and tags are powerful, but you're the one filing. The AI suggestions in Pro help at save time; there's no automatic tagging sweep across your library, and no AI summaries to triage a backlog.
- **It's a library, not a reader.** The reading experience is serviceable, but if your workflow is "save now, read properly later," a dedicated read-it-later app does that half better.
- **Small-team risk.** The same tiny team that makes Raindrop impressively focused also means development priorities move at a small team's pace. The AI layer arriving late, and Pro-only, is an example.
