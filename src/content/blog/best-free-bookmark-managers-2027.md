**The short answer:** the best free bookmark manager in 2027 depends on which *shape* of free you can live with. **Raindrop.io** has the most generous classic free tier — unlimited bookmarks, collections and apps on every platform, limited by features rather than volume. **Marqly** offers a permanent free plan with no card, covering up to 100 bookmarks with whole-library keyword search, boards and six-colour highlighting, limited by tier because every AI feature is Pro. **Karakeep** and **Linkwarden** are free forever if you will self-host, limited by your ops time. **Obsidian Web Clipper** is completely free including commercial use, limited by the fact that sync and publishing cost extra. And **your browser's built-in bookmarks** are the baseline, limited by scale.

None of those are trials wearing a costume, which is the first filter this list applies. The second is honesty about the limit: every free tier is constrained by *something*, and the useful question is not "is it free?" but "what will make me hit the wall, and when?"

The context changed since the last version of this guide. Mozilla shut Pocket down on **July 8, 2025**, and its official export window closed on **November 12, 2025** — so in 2027 you are no longer migrating off a live service, you are rescuing an archive you already downloaded. That makes the free tiers below landing spots for an old export rather than replacements for a running app, and it makes portability a first-class criterion. See [the Pocket alternatives roundup](/blog/best-pocket-alternatives-2027) for that migration specifically.

| Shape | What runs out | Tools in this list |
|---|---|---|
| **Feature-limited** | Capabilities, not volume — you can save forever but not search or archive | Raindrop.io |
| **Volume-limited** | A hard cap on how much you can keep | Marqly (100), mymind (100 cards) |
| **Ops-limited** | Your time and a server bill, not the software | Karakeep, Linkwarden, Wallabag |
| **Add-on-limited** | The core is free; sync, sharing or AI are paid extras | Obsidian Web Clipper |
| **Scale-limited** | Organisation and search degrade as count grows | Browser bookmarks |

## 1. Raindrop.io — the most generous classic free tier

Raindrop's free plan is the category benchmark: **unlimited bookmarks, unlimited collections, tags and every app** — web, iOS, Android, desktop, and extensions for Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari — with no expiry. It also includes up to three highlights per bookmark and 100 MB/month of uploads, which is enough for a real reading habit rather than a demo. For pure link collecting with manual organisation, nothing free matches its polish, and public collections let you share a curated list from a link.

The honest caveat is the shape: Raindrop free is feature-limited, not volume-limited. Full-text search, permanent page copies, Stella AI save-time tag and collection suggestions, annotations and daily backups all live in Pro at $28/year — genuinely cheap. And even Pro's search stays keyword-shaped rather than semantic: you find saves by words in them, not by describing them from memory. That distinction is the whole thesis of our [AI bookmark manager guide](/blog/best-ai-bookmark-manager-2027).

**Pros:**

- Unlimited bookmarks, collections and devices, free, on every platform
- Polished, mature apps including Linux, plus public collection sharing
- Pro is only $28/year and adds permanent page copies — the best defence against link rot

**Cons:**

- Free search is titles and tags only; full-text costs Pro
- No semantic search at any price, so retrieval always depends on your own wording
- Organisation is entirely manual — you file into collections yourself

## 2. Marqly — the best free tier if you want highlights, boards and an AI path

Marqly's free tier is the manual half of the product and it is upfront about being that: one-click saving from the extension, a save-all-tabs button, a six-colour highlighter, boards, public board sharing, read mode, and keyword search across your entire library up to 100 bookmarks. No card required, and no expiry. **Every AI feature is Pro** — auto-tagging, summaries, semantic search, ChatVault and YouTube chat alike — so this is not the pick if free AI is what you want.

The free tier's real strength is the parts that are usually paid elsewhere. Boards can be **shared as public web pages that open with no signup**, which is free on every plan and is the reason teachers use it to hand a resource list to a class. The highlighter works on any website in six colours with no gate, and notes on highlights are free up to 10. Save-as-PDF captures a page screen-faithfully, processed locally in Chrome and Edge.

Where Raindrop free beats Marqly free is raw collecting at unlimited scale. Where Marqly free wins is the reading and highlight workflow, whole-library search from the first save, and an upgrade path where the paid tier changes how retrieval works rather than just raising a ceiling. Pro is $72/year (about $6/month billed annually) or $9/month, with a standing $49 first-year offer and $48 for verified students.

**Pros:**

- Six-colour highlighter, boards, public board sharing and whole-library keyword search at no cost
- Save-all-tabs and the side-panel library in Chrome and Edge
- Imports browser bookmark HTML, Raindrop HTML collections, and the `list.csv` inside a Pocket export archive
- Free signup with no card and no expiry; Pro stays affordable at $72/year

**Cons:**

- **Capped at 100 bookmarks on free** — a real ceiling for a heavy reader
- **No AI at all on free**; auto-tagging, summaries and semantic search are every one of them Pro
- No public API and no self-hosting, so data lives in Marqly's cloud
- Highlight notes capped at 10 on free

## 3. Karakeep — the best free self-hosted AI bookmarking

Karakeep (formerly Hoarder, renamed in 2025) is what happens when the self-hosted crowd builds an AI bookmark manager. Self-hosting is fully free and open source: save links, notes and images, and an LLM you configure — an OpenAI-compatible API key or a local Ollama model — **auto-tags everything**, with full-text and semantic search across it all. Extensions for Chrome, Firefox and Safari plus iOS and Android apps make it unusually complete for a self-hosted project, and local-LLM support means the AI can be private as well as free.

There is also a hosted option worth knowing about, because it changes the "free" answer: Karakeep Cloud is in public beta with a **$0 tier capped at 10 bookmarks** and a Pro tier at **$4/month**, though the exact annual price is not published. Ten bookmarks is a trial in all but name, so the genuinely free Karakeep is the one on your own server.

**Pros:**

- AI tagging and semantic search, free, under your own roof
- Local-LLM support means the AI can be private too
- Full apps and extensions — unusually complete for self-hosted
- A cheap $4/month cloud Pro if you later decide you do not want to run a server

**Cons:**

- You run the server, the updates and the backups
- AI costs surface eventually: API usage fees or local GPU horsepower
- The cloud free tier caps at 10 bookmarks, so hosted Karakeep is not really free
- Rougher edges and a smaller support surface than commercial apps

## 4. Linkwarden — the best free self-hosted archiver

Linkwarden is the preservationist's pick: open source under **AGPL-3.0 with all features included when self-hosted**, and built around **full-page preservation** — every bookmark gets an archived copy, so link rot cannot eat your library. That is the single strongest answer to dead links anywhere in this list, and for a 2027 reader sitting on an old Pocket export it matters more than it used to. It is collaborative, supports highlights and RSS, has real mobile apps, and offers optional AI tagging if you wire up a provider.

The cloud plan is **$3/month per user billed yearly (25% off) or $4/month billed monthly**, supports up to 30,000 links per user, and has no free tier beyond a trial. So "free Linkwarden" means self-hosted Linkwarden.

**Pros:**

- Free forever when self-hosted, with every feature included and your data on your disk
- Full-page archives of everything you save — the best defence against link rot here
- Active development, mobile apps, browser extensions, collaboration

**Cons:**

- "Free" costs a server (typically $5–15/month) plus your maintenance time
- AI features are optional bolt-ons rather than the core experience
- Cloud has no free tier beyond a trial, so the hosted route is paid from day one

## 5. Obsidian Web Clipper — completely free, including commercial use

Obsidian's clipper and the Obsidian app are **completely free, including for commercial use** — a policy in place since February 2025 and unusual enough to be worth stating plainly. Clips land in your own vault as Markdown files you own outright, which makes this the strongest portability story in the list: there is no export step, because your library was never in a vendor's format to begin with. For anyone already keeping notes in Obsidian, it turns bookmarking into part of a knowledge base rather than a separate silo.

The limiting shape is add-ons. Cross-device sync via Obsidian Sync starts at **$4/month billed annually ($5 monthly)**, and public sharing via Obsidian Publish costs **$8 per site per month**. So a single-device Obsidian bookmark vault is free forever; a synced, shareable one is not. Retrieval is also only as good as your own linking and search discipline — the clipper stores, it does not organise for you.

**Pros:**

- Completely free including commercial use, with a policy statement behind it
- Your library is plain Markdown in a vault you own — the best portability here
- Clips sit beside notes and backlinks, so bookmarks join a knowledge base
- Large plugin ecosystem for tagging, dataview-style queries and automation

**Cons:**

- Cross-device sync costs $4–5/month; public sharing costs $8/site/month
- No automatic organisation — you build the structure yourself
- No built-in semantic search or AI summaries without third-party plugins and your own keys
- A vault is a commitment; it is less convenient than a hosted app

## 6. Wallabag — the best free read-it-later, or €11/year hosted

Wallabag is open source under **MIT** and completely free to self-host. It started in France in 2013 and has been maintained continuously since, which makes it one of the longest-surviving projects in this category — a real consideration when the point of an archive is that it outlives the vendor. It is a read-it-later tool first: save a page and read a clean, distraction-free copy later, which is the behaviour Pocket refugees actually miss.

If you would rather not run a server, the official **wallabag.it** hosting is **€11/year**, with **€4 for 3 months** and a **€30/year supporter plan**; hosted plans include all features and fund development. That is the cheapest paid option in this entire article, and unlike a free tier it has no cap.

**Pros:**

- Free and open source (MIT) to self-host, with a 13-year maintenance history
- Clean read-it-later rendering — the closest thing to what Pocket did
- Official hosting at €11/year with all features included
- No AI lock-in and no proprietary format

**Cons:**

- No AI of any kind: no tagging, summaries or semantic search
- Self-hosting is real setup and maintenance work
- Smaller ecosystem and fewer native apps than the commercial options
- Reading-focused, so it is weaker as a general bookmark organiser

## 7. mymind — free forever at 100 cards, if you do not need to import or share

A correction to the previous version of this guide: **mymind does have a permanent free plan.** Every account starts on a free guest plan capped at **100 cards**, and it is free forever rather than a timed trial. That puts it in the same volume-limited shape as Marqly's free tier, and it is a genuinely pleasant way to save visually — fast, private and image-first.

For a research or bookmarking workflow, though, two design decisions are close to disqualifying. **There is no bulk import by design** — you cannot bring in Pocket, Raindrop or browser bookmarks, which rules it out as a landing spot for an old export. And **nothing can be shared**: no public boards, no collaboration of any kind. Export is a one-way CSV plus files and works only from desktop Chrome or Edge. Paid tiers start at The Bookmarker ($4.99/month, no AI), then Student of Life ($7.99/month or $79/year, AI tagging), then Mastermind ($12.99/month or $129/year, AI summaries, reading mode, article backup and the PDF analyzer).

**Pros:**

- Free-forever guest plan rather than a countdown
- Fast, private and excellent for image-heavy saving
- AI tagging arrives at a mid price point rather than the top tier

**Cons:**

- **No bulk import by design** — cannot ingest a Pocket, Raindrop or browser export
- **Nothing can be shared**, so it is useless for group work or teaching
- Free caps at 100 cards; export is one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge only
- AI summaries need the $12.99/month tier

## 8. OneTab and Session Buddy — free tab rescue, not bookmark managers

Both are entirely free with no paid tier and no limits, and both solve a narrower problem than the rest of this list: too many open tabs. **OneTab** collapses them into a single list, needs no account, and got a full v2 rewrite in 2026 that added folders, in-list search, stars and tasks, plus dark mode; end-to-end-encrypted cross-device sync is announced as coming soon with no pricing stated, so whether sync stays free is genuinely unknown. **Session Buddy** saves and restores tab sessions, needs no account for local use, and added optional cloud sync in v4 that requires signing in with no paid plan announced.

They belong in a free-bookmark-manager list because a lot of people searching for one are actually drowning in tabs. But neither captures page content, neither searches inside a save, and neither is where you want a four-year research library to live. Use them as the intake valve and something else as the archive.

**Pros:**

- Completely free, no paid tier, no limits, no account required
- Solve the open-tabs problem instantly and well
- OneTab's 2026 v2 rewrite added folders, search and tasks

**Cons:**

- Not bookmark managers: no content capture, no in-page search, no tagging depth
- Cross-device sync is either not free or not shipped, depending on the tool
- A list of URLs is still a list of URLs — link rot applies in full

## 9. Browser bookmarks — the free baseline everyone already has

Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all ship a bookmark manager: free, instant, synced within their own ecosystem, and exportable as a standard HTML file. For a few dozen stable links — banking, tools, docs — they are genuinely fine, and pretending otherwise would be marketing.

They collapse at scale for four reasons: **folder-only organisation** (every save is a filing decision), **title-only search** (describe a page and find nothing), **no content capture** (a dead link is a dead end), and **ecosystem lock** (Safari bookmarks do not follow you to Chrome). When any of those starts to hurt, that is the signal to graduate — and the exported HTML file imports directly into Raindrop, Linkwarden, Karakeep or Marqly.

**Pros:**

- Free, built in, zero setup, syncs across devices inside one ecosystem
- Standard HTML export means you are never locked in
- Perfectly adequate for a small, stable set of links

**Cons:**

- Folders and title search stop working past a few hundred saves
- No tagging, no summaries, no content search, no archiving
- Cross-browser sync does not exist

## What free actually feels like at 100, 500 and 1,000 saves

The limiting-shape table predicts behaviour; here is what it looks like in practice.

**At 100 saves**, everything here is fine and the choice barely matters. Marqly's free tier and mymind's guest plan are exactly at their ceiling, so this is the last comfortable month on a volume-limited plan.

**At 500 saves**, volume-limited tiers have already pushed you to a decision. Feature-limited Raindrop still works if you filed carefully, and folder-only browser bookmarks start to hurt — this is where title-only search first fails you, because you no longer remember what you called things. Ops-limited self-hosting is invisible at this scale if it was set up once and left alone.

**At 1,000 saves**, the shape decides everything. Manual organisation without full-text or semantic search means retrieval is guesswork regardless of which tool you picked, which is why Raindrop's Pro full-text search and Marqly's Pro semantic search exist at all. Link rot has quietly removed a meaningful fraction of your library, and only Linkwarden's full-page archives or Raindrop Pro's permanent copies did anything about it. This is also the scale at which a Pocket-export rescue arrives, since those archives are typically hundreds or thousands of links rather than dozens.

## Tools that are not actually free

A roundup like this is only trustworthy if it names what it excluded, because these are the ones people assume are free:

- **Readwise Reader** — no permanent free tier at all. After a 30-day trial you must subscribe at $9.99/month billed annually ($119.88/year) or $12.99/month monthly. Reader is only in the Full plan; the cheaper Lite plan at $5.59/month billed annually covers classic highlight syncing without it.
- **Evernote** — the clipper extension is free, but it saves into Evernote, and Evernote's free plan is capped at 50 notes, one notebook and one device. That is a demo, not a tier you can live on. Starter is $14.99/month or $99/year.
- **Pinboard** — paid-only at $22/year, or $39/year with archiving. There is no free tier.
- **GoodLinks** — $9.99 one-time plus an optional $4.99/year. Cheap and perpetual, but not free.
- **Glasp** — free highlights are **public by default**; keeping them private requires Pro at $15/month or $150/year. Prices rose about 20–25% in May 2026 and subscriptions are explicitly non-refundable.
- **Pocket** — shut down July 8, 2025, with the export window closed November 12, 2025. It is in this list only as the archive people are still rescuing.

## Moving between them (you are never stuck)

The exits in this category are unlocked, and in 2027 that matters more than it used to because so many libraries are rescued archives rather than living accounts. Every browser exports bookmarks as a standard HTML file, and that file imports into Raindrop, Linkwarden, Karakeep and Marqly alike. Marqly imports browser bookmark HTML, Raindrop HTML collections, and the **`list.csv` inside a Pocket export archive** — its importer reads that CSV, not the HTML preview file, which is a `<ul><li>` layout rather than the Netscape bookmark format and does not parse. Follow the [Pocket migration guide](/migrate/pocket) or the [Raindrop migration guide](/migrate/raindrop), or start from the universal [Migration Center](/migrate).

Two limits worth knowing before you import into Marqly: files cap at 10 MB on free and 30 MB on Pro with 10,000 bookmarks per file, and folder structure flattens to at most two board levels. Original save dates are not preserved — imported items take the import date — and highlights and article text do not come across. Auto-tagging an import is Pro.

Before importing anything, inspect and clean the file with the free [Bookmark File Viewer](/tools/bookmark-file-viewer) and [Duplicate Bookmark Finder](/tools/duplicate-bookmark-finder) from the [free tools directory](/tools). Practically, that means the cost of choosing "wrong" today is an afternoon, not a lock-in. Start with the free tier that matches your biggest pain, use it for a month at real volume, and switch if it does not hold.

A last honest note: "free" always has a shape. Raindrop's is limited by features, Marqly's and mymind's by volume, Karakeep's, Linkwarden's and Wallabag's by your ops time, Obsidian's by paid add-ons, and your browser's by scale. Pick the shape you can live with. If you want manual organisation with web highlights, boards you can publish, and the option to add semantic search later, start on the free plan: [get started free](https://app.marqly.com), import your existing bookmarks, and see whether 100 saves is enough before paying anything.
