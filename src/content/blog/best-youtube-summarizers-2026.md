How we compared: we ran each tool against the same three videos — a 12-minute tutorial, a 90-minute podcast, and a 20-minute lecture with dense terminology — and scored the workflow, not just the output. Summary text was consistently fine everywhere; the tools separated on friction (clicks between "watching" and "summarized"), on what survives afterwards, and on how quickly the free tier runs out.

There's also a bookmark button in YouTube's own action row that saves the video to your library **with its transcript attached** — so weeks later, semantic search can find "that video about sourdough starters" from what was *said in it*, not just its title.

## NoteGPT — best for students and study materials

NoteGPT is a learning workspace: paste a YouTube link (it handles other platforms too) and get a summary, transcript, chat, mind maps, and flashcards. That artifact generation is its real edge — nothing else in this roundup turns a lecture into study materials as directly. The trade-off is that everything happens in NoteGPT's own workspace, not on YouTube, and the quota system takes reading: the free tier is a small monthly allowance, Pro is $9/month for 1,000 basic quotas, and the Unlimited plan runs $19.92/month billed annually ($239/year).

**Pros:**

- Mind maps, flashcards, and notes — the best study-output pipeline here
- Handles more than YouTube (PDFs, audio, other platforms)
- Chat with the video content included
- Pro at $9/month is reasonably priced for what it bundles

**Cons:**

- Lives in a separate workspace — constant tab-switching while you watch
- Quota/credit system is confusing; heavy users end up on the $239/year plan
- More tool than you need if you just want a TL;DR before committing to a video

## Recall — best for very long videos and knowledge bases

Recall summarizes YouTube videos up to 10 hours (with transcript; ~5 hours when it must transcribe audio), supports 60+ languages, and files every summary into a personal knowledge base that auto-links related concepts. The free plan includes unlimited saves and 10 AI summaries a month; Plus is $10/month billed annually for unlimited summaries, and a $38/month Max tier serves power users.

**Pros:**

- Handles marathon content — lectures, podcasts, conference talks — that chokes other tools
- Knowledge-base linking between summaries is genuinely clever
- Decent free plan: 10 summaries/month with unlimited saving

**Cons:**

- Summaries live in Recall, not on the watch page
- No transcript-grounded chat; it's summary-and-archive, not Q&A
- Jump from $10/month to $38/month is steep if you outgrow Plus

## Eightify — best cheap, instant TL;DR

Eightify is the one-trick pony that does its trick well: click the button on a watch page, get a timestamped summary of key ideas in seconds. Free covers 3 videos per week up to 30 minutes each; unlimited costs $4.95/month or $3.95/month billed annually — the cheapest dedicated summarizer here. There's no chat, no library, no transcript workflow; you get the gist and move on.

**Pros:**

- Fast, timestamped summaries right on the watch page
- Cheapest paid plan in the roundup
- iOS and Android apps alongside the Chrome extension

**Cons:**

- Free tier is tight: 3 videos/week, 30-minute cap
- No chat, no search, no library — summaries are disposable
- Nothing carries forward; every video is a fresh start

## Glasp — best if you already highlight with it

Glasp bundles YouTube summaries into its social highlighting ecosystem: transcripts and summaries on the watch page, with your notes flowing into your Glasp profile. Free gets you 3 basic summaries a day; the Pro plan — $15/month or $150/year after the May 2026 price increase — raises that to 1,000 advanced summaries a month plus AI chat credits. As a *standalone* summarizer that's expensive; as an add-on to a highlighting habit it's fine.

**Pros:**

- Summary + transcript on the watch page, free tier included
- Integrates with Glasp's highlight library and social features
- Generous Pro quota (1,000 advanced summaries/month) if you're all-in

**Cons:**

- $15/month is hard to justify for summaries alone
- Chat is credit-limited rather than unlimited
- Highlights and profile are public by default on the free plan

## ChatGPT — the free manual method (and its ceiling)

The DIY route: grab the video's transcript, paste it into ChatGPT (or Claude or Gemini), and prompt "summarize this, then answer my questions." It works, it's flexible, and the free tiers handle short videos. The friction is real, though — you fetch the transcript yourself, very long videos exceed context limits and need chunking, timestamps get lost, and nothing is saved anywhere. It's a workflow you rebuild from scratch every single video.

**Pros:**

- Free (or the $20/month Plus you may already pay for)
- Total control over the prompt — any format, any language, any depth
- Chat is the whole product, so follow-up questions are natural

**Cons:**

- Manual transcript-fetching every time; long videos need chunking
- No timestamps linking back to the video, no watch-page integration
- Nothing persists — no library, no search, no history tied to the video

Price out your actual volume before assuming free is free.
