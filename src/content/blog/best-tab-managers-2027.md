**There is no single best tab manager in 2027, because three different jobs hide under one label.** If you keep forty tabs open because you are **switching between projects**, you want a workspace manager: **Workona**, or **Toby** if you think in visual boards and share collections with a team. If you keep them open because **a crash would cost you an afternoon**, you want session insurance: **Session Buddy**. If you keep them open because you are **afraid you will never find these pages again**, you want a tab saver: **Marqly**, which files closed tabs into a library you can search by meaning. **OneTab** remains the best free panic button, and its 2026 rewrite made it meaningfully better.

Buy for the job you actually have. The most common mistake in this category is purchasing a workspace manager to solve a retrieval problem, which gives you five beautifully organised projects and a thousand links you still cannot find.

## Three different jobs hide under "tab manager"

Name yours before you shop, because the tools do not overlap.

1. **Workspace switching.** You have four or five live projects, each with its own set of tabs, docs and tools, and you want to swap contexts in one click. The tabs are *in use*. → **Workona**, or **Toby** for a visual and team-friendly version.
2. **Session insurance.** You work in bursts across many windows and the failure you fear is a crash, a stray Cmd-W, or a forced browser restart. The tabs are *in use* and you want them recoverable. → **Session Buddy**.
3. **Retrieval.** The tabs are *not* in use. They are open because closing them feels like losing them, and the failure you fear is not finding the page again in three weeks. → **Marqly**, with **OneTab** as the free, write-only version of the same idea.

The diagnostic is one question: look at your tab bar and ask whether you will reopen these tabs **today**. If yes, you have a workspace or an insurance problem. If no, you have a retrieval problem, and stashing them in another list will not fix it.

## 2. Workona — best workspace switcher

Workona treats your browser like an operating system: each project gets a space with its own open tabs, saved links, cloud files, notes and tasks, and switching projects swaps your whole context in one click. For consultants, founders and anyone running parallel projects it is the most complete tool in the category, and nothing else here imitates it. Sessions save automatically with cloud backups — hourly snapshots and 90-day session history on paid plans — so a crash does not lose a window. Spaces sync across devices including native iPhone and iPad apps that can show the tabs open on your desktop, and collaboration is real: shared spaces, real-time editing, templates, and SSO and SCIM at the enterprise tier.

The free plan is capped at **5 spaces**, cut from 10 in late 2024, plus a space-complexity limit — a retroactive change that understandably stung existing users and turns free into more of a trial funnel. **Pro starts at $7/month billed annually ($84/year)** and **Team from $8/user/month billed annually with a three-user minimum**; Workona does not display monthly rates on its pricing page, and no trial length is published. Verified nonprofit and education users get **50% off**.

Two cautions from the fact sheet. The **Chrome extension has not shipped an update since January 2025**, and 2026 reviews report crashes, hangs and occasional failed tab recovery — most of Workona's logic lives in the server-side web app, which explains the gap but does not fully excuse it. And everything is in Workona's cloud: there is no offline mode, no Safari support, no Android app and no public API.

**Who it's best for:** people and teams juggling several live projects who want each one's tabs, docs, notes and tasks kept together and synced. **Skip it if** you are local-first, you need Safari or Android, or your tabs are things you intend to read later rather than work in now. See [Marqly vs Workona](/compare/marqly-vs-workona).

- **Cons:** free plan cut from 10 spaces to 5 in late 2024; the Chrome extension has not updated since January 2025 and 2026 reviews report crashes; cloud-only with no offline mode; no Safari, no Android, no public API; heavier than a simple tab parker at large tab counts.
- **Price:** free (5 spaces) · Pro from $7/month billed annually ($84/year) · Team from $8/user/month billed annually, three-user minimum · 50% nonprofit and education discount.

## 3. Toby — best for visual collections and teams

Toby replaces your new-tab page with visual boards of saved tabs organised into collections, grouped into spaces that can be shared with teammates or published as public links requiring no Toby account. It is the nicest visual organisation in the category and the strongest team story after Workona, with shared spaces, member invites, org-wide collections and SSO on the Team plan. **Toby AI names, sorts and groups your open tabs into collections automatically** — a correction to our 2026 entry, which described organisation as purely manual. Reach is broad for a tab manager: Chrome, Edge and Firefox extensions, a web app, and iOS and Android companions.

The catch is the free plan. **Starter caps you at 60 saved tabs total**, and over the limit you keep read access but cannot add more — a cap serious users hit within weeks. Legacy free accounts were **force-migrated to Starter in November 2024**, and the backlash is still visible in the extension's reviews. Paid plans are per member: **Productivity $4.50/member/month billed yearly ($54/year)** or $6 monthly, and **Team $8/member/month billed yearly ($96/year)** or $10 monthly with SSO and centralised billing. Per-member pricing gets steep for a solo user.

Toby stores links rather than page content, so there is no archiving, no highlighting and no full-text search, and nothing survives link rot. There is no Safari extension, no offline mode, no public API, and the mobile apps are viewers rather than workspaces — session save and restore lives in the desktop extension.

**Who it's best for:** visual thinkers and teams that share link collections, who will pay for the paid tier from the start. **Skip it if** you are solo and budget-minded (60 tabs is not a library), you use Safari, or you need the content of the pages rather than the links. See [Marqly vs Toby](/compare/marqly-vs-toby).

- **Pros:** the best visual organisation here; public sharing needs no Toby account; real team features with shared spaces, invites and SSO on Team; Toby AI groups open tabs automatically; Chrome, Edge and Firefox extensions plus a web app and mobile companions; long track record since 2016, still updated as of June 2026.
- **Cons:** free plan caps at 60 saved tabs and the November 2024 forced migration left lasting resentment; stores links only, so no archiving, highlights or full-text search; per-member pricing is steep solo; no Safari extension and no offline mode; no public API.
- **Price:** free Starter (60 saved tabs) · Productivity $4.50/member/month billed yearly or $6 monthly · Team $8/member/month billed yearly or $10 monthly.

## 4. OneTab — best free one-click declutter

OneTab has one move: click the icon and every open tab collapses into a single list page, freeing the memory those tabs were holding. It is **entirely free — no paid tier, no limits, no account and no upsell**, which is rare in this category and the reason it has survived since 2013. It is also more private than the alternatives: tab URLs are never transmitted to the developers unless you deliberately share a list, and any list can be published as a hosted web page you can open on another device.

Our 2026 edition undersold it. A **full v2 rewrite in 2026 added folders, in-list search, stars, tasks and dark mode**, and the Chrome and Firefox extensions were both updated in July 2026. Retrieval within a list is no longer scroll-and-pray. What has not changed is everything around the list.

The limits are structural. **There is no sync** — an end-to-end-encrypted cross-device sync is announced as coming soon with no pricing stated, so whether it stays free is unknown. **There is no mobile or web app at all**: no iOS app, no Android app, and nothing to log into, since there are no accounts; the Safari listing is a Mac-only app that has not been updated since late 2024 and lags well behind Chrome. It stores **URLs and titles only** — no page content, no snapshots, no full-text search — so a dead link means the content is gone. And there is real data-loss risk: **uninstalling the extension deletes your stored tabs**, and clearing browser data can wipe them too.

**Who it's best for:** anyone drowning in open tabs who wants a free, private, zero-setup way to park them. **Skip it if** you need sync, phone access, or the pages themselves to survive — and export early, since the only export is a plain-text list of URLs and titles. See [Marqly vs OneTab](/compare/marqly-vs-onetab).

- **Pros:** completely free with no account, no limits and no upsell; instant memory relief in one click; the 2026 v2 rewrite added folders, search, stars, tasks and dark mode; strong privacy — URLs are never transmitted unless you share; share-as-web-page; plain-text export and a huge install base mean near-zero lock-in.
- **Cons:** no sync, with the promised encrypted version still "coming soon"; no iOS app, no Android app and no web app; the Mac Safari port is stale; URLs and titles only, so dead links lose the content; uninstalling deletes your tabs.
- **Price:** free. No paid tier exists.

## 5. Session Buddy — best crash insurance

Session Buddy snapshots whole windows rather than collapsing tabs into a flat list: you name a session, close everything, and reopen the arrangement later with tabs in place. It also keeps a running record of your open windows, which makes it a practical recovery tool after a crash or an accidental close. It is **completely free — no paid tier, no cap on saved sessions, and no account needed for local use**, with roughly 900,000 users and updates through 2026.

Two corrections and one caution. The correction in its favour: **v4 added optional cloud sync** that requires signing in, with no paid plan announced, so our 2026 "no cloud sync" con is out of date. The correction in its favour again: **export is genuinely good** — JSON, HTML, CSV and plain text, with almost no lock-in. The caution is the one that matters: the **2024 v4 redesign drew sustained reports of sessions going missing or not carrying over from v3**, and long-time users also describe v4 as heavier and slower than the minimal tool they chose. Export a backup before you update, and periodically after.

It is also narrower than it looks. Session Buddy is **Chromium-only — Chrome, Edge and similar browsers** — with no Firefox add-on, no Safari extension, no mobile app and no web app. It stores tabs, not content: no page archiving, no highlights, no AI and no full-text search. And there is no public sharing; you can export or copy a session's links, but there is no shareable hosted page.

**Who it's best for:** anyone who works in bursts across many windows and wants to snapshot, name and reopen whole sessions for free. **Skip it if** you use Firefox or Safari, you need phone access, or your real problem is finding one specific page by topic three weeks later. See [Marqly vs Session Buddy](/compare/marqly-vs-session-buddy).

- **Pros:** completely free with no limits and no account for local use; best-in-class window-arrangement restore; automatic recovery after a crash; local-first by default with optional v4 cloud sync; exports to JSON, HTML, CSV and plain text; actively maintained with updates through 2026.
- **Cons:** the 2024 v4 redesign produced sustained reports of missing sessions; v4 is heavier and slower than v3; Chromium-only with no Firefox, Safari or mobile; stores tabs not content; no public sharing; local storage is fragile unless sync is on.
- **Price:** free. No paid tier exists.

## Can you get your tabs out? Export paths compared

Tab tools are where people discover lock-in last, usually after a browser profile is lost.

- **Session Buddy** — JSON, HTML, CSV and plain text. The strongest export here, and the HTML option means any bookmark manager can read your sessions.
- **Toby** — JSON, HTML and TXT from Settings → Data. Only the JSON preserves notes and tags, so an HTML export is lossy. No public API.
- **OneTab** — a plain-text list of URLs with titles, one per line. No HTML, JSON or CSV. Portable in the sense that a text file is portable, and nothing more.
- **Workona** — a "Download my data" export whose format is not officially documented and which is **not browser-importable bookmark HTML**; community scripts exist specifically to convert it. No public API, so automated ongoing export is not an option.
- **Marqly** — a CSV export button in the sidebar and profile menu carrying **URL, title, description and tags**. The free plan exports your newest 100 bookmarks and Pro exports the whole library, including an option to export only what is new since your last export. Highlights, notes, boards and dates are not in that CSV; for a complete copy of your account including highlights, notes and boards, email support@marqly.com.

The honest ranking: Session Buddy and Toby are easy to leave, OneTab is easy to leave but there is little to leave with, Workona is the hardest exit in this list, and Marqly sits in the middle — a real self-serve CSV for your links, and a support request for everything else. Because OneTab and Session Buddy store locally by default, exporting regularly is a backup routine rather than a nicety.

## Can you combine them? Two-tool stacks that work

Yes, and most heavy tab users end up with two, because the jobs never stopped being different.

- **Workona + Marqly** is the power combo: spaces hold the tabs you are actively working in, and anything interesting-but-not-urgent gets saved to Marqly instead of squatting in a space forever. Spaces stay lean; nothing gets lost.
- **OneTab + Marqly** works for minimalists: OneTab as the panic button during a heavy session, Marqly's save-all-tabs for anything you want back. The test is simple — if you would be annoyed to lose it, it does not belong in a OneTab list.
- **Session Buddy + anything** is cheap insurance regardless of your main tool, since it protects the tabs you have not saved yet. It is free and it does not conflict with the others.

The combination to avoid is two tools doing the same job — Toby *and* Workona, or OneTab *and* Session Buddy as your archive. You will split your saves across two places and trust neither.

## What actually matters in 2027

**Name the job before you name the tool.** Workspace switching, session insurance and retrieval are different problems with different winners, and the expensive mistake is buying for the wrong one. Workona is the best workspace switcher here and we would recommend it to a consultant without hesitation; it will not help you find an article in November that you parked in March.

**Check the exit before you commit a year of tabs.** Workona's export is undocumented and not browser-importable; Toby's HTML export drops notes and tags; OneTab gives you a text file.
