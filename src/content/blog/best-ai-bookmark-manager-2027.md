An **AI bookmark manager** saves links and then uses AI to make them usable later — auto-tagging what you save, summarising it, letting you search by meaning instead of exact keywords, and (increasingly, in 2027) reorganising the library on your behalf. For most people the best pick is **Marqly**, because it is built around semantic search first rather than adding it to a filing cabinet. The rest of this guide is about how to tell real AI from marketing, because that gap has got wider, not narrower.

"AI bookmark manager" was a crowded label in 2026 and is a saturated one in 2027. Almost every tool puts AI on the box, and the words mean very different things: a suggested tag at save time, a summary generated on demand, a vector index you can query in natural language, or a chatbot bolted onto a link list. Some of those change how you use a library and some change nothing at all. Two things have made the confusion worse rather than better. First, the migrations — Pocket shut down on July 8, 2025 and Omnivore in November 2024, so a lot of people are choosing a new home for an old archive and are being sold AI as the reason. Second, the arrival of *agentic* tools that can edit your library rather than only read it, which introduces a genuinely new evaluation axis: can it be trusted, and can it be undone?

## What an AI bookmark manager should actually do

Four layers, in the order they matter:

1. **Retrieval — semantic search.** You find a save by describing it. This is the layer that changes everything, because the failure mode of every large bookmark library is not losing links, it being unable to find them. Everything else is convenience.
2. **Agency — the AI can change the library.** New as a mainstream expectation in 2027. Retagging, renaming, merging duplicates, moving items between boards, surfacing dead links. This is the difference between a tool that helps you search a mess and one that cleans it up.
3. **Organisation — auto-tagging.** Saves get tagged without you deciding anything, so filing cost drops to zero. This is what makes a large library survivable.
4. **Triage — summaries.** A backlog becomes skimmable, so you can decide what is worth reading rather than feeling guilty about all of it.

Most tools deliver one of these and market all four. The most common substitution is a **summary feature presented as intelligence**: summarising a page you have already found is useful, but it does nothing for the 4,000 saves you cannot locate.

## Six tests that separate real AI from marketing

You can run all of these in an evening, and they will tell you more than any feature list.

**1. The paraphrase test.** Save three articles. Wait a day — long enough that you have genuinely forgotten the wording. Then search for one of them using a description that shares no words with its title. If it surfaces, semantic search is real. If you get nothing, the search is keyword matching with a nicer interface.

**2. The plan test.** Find which tier the AI actually lives on. The dominant pattern in this category is a real AI feature behind a paid plan while the free tier has none at all — and the pricing page often buries this in a comparison table rather than stating it. Marqly is explicit that all of its AI is Pro; several competitors are not.

**3. The import test.** Does the AI apply to what you imported, or only to saves made after signup? This decides whether an old archive becomes searchable or stays a pile. In Marqly, auto-tagging an import is a Pro feature, so a free import lands with your original tags intact and nothing added — stated plainly, because it is the kind of detail that ruins a migration if you discover it afterwards.

**4. The agency test.** Can the AI change your library, and — the important half — **can you undo it?** An assistant that renames 300 tags with no rollback is a hazard rather than a feature. Look for proposal-before-apply, an explicit write mode, and a stated undo window.

**5. The privacy test.** Does your saved content leave your machine to be processed? Hosted AI means yes. The only mainstream way to get AI over bookmarks with your library staying local is self-hosting with a local model, which is what Karakeep offers and nothing else here does.

**6. The cost test.** AI features usually run on credits, caps or your own API key, and the caps are frequently unpublished. Ask what happens at 1,000 saves rather than 10. A tool that is free at demo scale and expensive at library scale is not free.

## 2. Karakeep — the only genuinely private AI option

Karakeep (formerly Hoarder, renamed in 2025) is the outlier in this table and deserves the position because it answers the privacy test in a way no hosted tool can. It is free and open source to self-host with all features included, and it auto-tags everything using **an LLM you configure** — an OpenAI-compatible API key, or a **local Ollama model**. With Ollama, your saved content is tagged and indexed on your own hardware and never leaves it. That is a real category of one.

It also has full-text and semantic search across everything, apps for iOS and Android, and extensions for Chrome, Firefox and Safari — unusually complete for a self-hosted project. The trade is that you are the operator: server, updates, backups, and eventually the cost of AI, whether API fees or local GPU horsepower. The hosted Karakeep Cloud is in public beta with a **$0 tier capped at 10 bookmarks** and a Pro tier at **$4/month**, so the free-with-AI version is the one on your own server.

**Who it's best for:** technical users and privacy-first readers who want AI over bookmarks without handing a vendor their library. **Skip it if** you do not want to administer software — the ops cost is real and it is the reason most people do not stay.

- **Pros:** AI tagging and semantic search, free, under your own roof; local-LLM support means genuinely private AI; full apps and extensions; a cheap hosted exit at $4/month.
- **Cons:** you run the server, updates and backups; AI costs surface eventually; cloud free tier caps at 10 bookmarks; rougher edges and a smaller support surface than commercial apps.
- **Price:** free self-hosted · Cloud Pro $4/month (annual price not published).

## 3. Readwise Reader — best AI for people who actually read

Readwise Reader is the premium reading system: articles, PDFs, email newsletters and ebooks, with best-in-class highlighting and a spaced-repetition review loop that resurfaces your highlights over time. Its AI covers summaries and YouTube handling, and highlights flow into Notion or Obsidian, which is a genuinely powerful pipeline for anyone writing from what they read.

Two things keep it out of the top two here. **Search is keyword-based rather than semantic**, so the retrieval problem this article is about is not the one Reader solves. And **there is no permanent free tier** — after a 30-day trial you must subscribe at $9.99/month billed annually ($119.88/year) or $12.99/month monthly for the Full plan, with Reader only in Full; the cheaper Lite plan at $5.59/month billed annually covers classic highlight syncing without it. Tagging is manual.

**Who it's best for:** researchers, students and writers who highlight heavily and want those highlights resurfaced, and who will pay for it. **Skip it if** retrieval by meaning is your problem, or if you need free. See [Readwise Reader vs Marqly](/compare/marqly-vs-readwise-reader) for the head-to-head.

- **Pros:** the most complete reading pipeline; outstanding highlighting plus spaced-repetition review; handles every format; AI summaries and YouTube support; exports into Notion or Obsidian.
- **Cons:** keyword rather than semantic search; no permanent free tier; among the most expensive here; Reader requires the Full plan; manual tagging; dense UI with a real learning curve.
- **Price:** no free tier · Full $9.99/month billed annually or $12.99/month monthly · Lite $5.59/month billed annually.

## 4. mymind — best zero-effort AI tagging for visual savers

mymind's pitch is "no organising, ever," and it is the purest expression of the auto-tagging layer: you save articles, images, quotes and products, and its AI tags and surfaces them without any structure from you. There are no folders or collections by design, which some people find freeing and others find maddening. It is fast, private-feeling and visually excellent.

The AI is tiered, and the tiers matter. The **free guest plan caps at 100 cards** and is free forever rather than a countdown. AI tagging starts at **Student of Life ($7.99/month or $79/year)**; **The Bookmarker ($4.99/month) has no AI features at all**; AI summaries, reading mode, article backup and the PDF analyzer require **Mastermind ($12.99/month or $129/year)**. So the "AI auto-organisation" mymind is known for is not a free feature.

Two design decisions rule it out for a migration: **there is no bulk import by design** — you cannot bring in Pocket, Raindrop or browser bookmarks — and **nothing can be shared**, with no public boards and no collaboration. Export is a one-way CSV plus files from desktop Chrome or Edge only.

**Who it's best for:** visual thinkers and creatives who save images and inspiration more than articles and never want to file anything. **Skip it if** you have an existing library to import, need to share, or want free AI.

- **Pros:** genuinely zero-effort AI tagging; fast and visually strong; free-forever guest plan rather than a trial; good for image-heavy saving.
- **Cons:** no bulk import by design; nothing can be shared; AI needs paid tiers and summaries need the top tier; free caps at 100 cards; export is one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge.
- **Price:** free guest plan to 100 cards · The Bookmarker $4.99/month (no AI) · Student of Life $7.99/month or $79/year · Mastermind $12.99/month or $129/year.

## 5. Glasp — best AI for highlighting in public

Glasp is built around public highlighting: your highlights form a shareable knowledge profile and you can see what other people underlined. For learning in a cohort that is a real feature rather than a gimmick, and its YouTube AI is strong for lecture-heavy material. Free includes unlimited **public** highlights.

The AI limits are tight and the privacy model is inverted from what most people expect. Free AI caps are **three YouTube summaries a day and twenty PDF chat messages a month**. Keeping highlights **private requires Pro** — you pay for privacy, not for capability. Pro is **$12.50/month billed yearly ($150/year)** or **$15/month billed monthly**, with an Unlimited tier at $30/month billed yearly ($360/year), and Glasp publishes a **40% discount on Pro for students and educators** with a short verification step. Prices rose about 20–25% in May 2026 and subscriptions are explicitly non-refundable. There is no full-page archiving, no full-text search and no offline mode, so it is a highlight layer rather than an archive, and it is run by a two-person team, which is a longevity consideration for anything you would rely on for years.

**Who it's best for:** students and learners who want to highlight publicly and discover what others marked. **Skip it if** your highlights are private by default, or if you need archiving and real search.

- **Pros:** unlimited free public highlights; a shareable knowledge profile; strong YouTube AI; published 40% discount on Pro for students and educators.
- **Cons:** private highlights require Pro; tight free AI caps; prices rose 20–25% in May 2026 and are non-refundable; no archiving, full-text search or offline; two-person team.
- **Price:** free (public highlights) · Pro $12.50/month billed yearly ($150/year) or $15/month monthly · Unlimited $30/month billed yearly ($360/year) · 40% off Pro for students and educators.

## 6. Notion Web Clipper — best AI if you already pay for Business

For a Notion-native user, clipping into a database means every saved link inherits properties, views, filters and tags, sitting beside notes, projects and tasks. The clipper is free and works with any Notion account including the generous free plan, offline arrived on all plans in 2025, and there are native apps for web, Mac, Windows, iOS and Android.

The AI caveat is the important one and is widely misunderstood: **meaningful AI search and chat over clipped content is bundled with Business and above at $20/member/month.** Free and Plus get a trial of it. So a Plus subscriber at $10/member/month does not have the feature that made them choose Notion for bookmarks. Separately, the clipper is bare-bones at capture time — no tags, notes, highlights or screenshots, just a destination picker — Notion's own docs warn that clip fidelity varies and formatting can go missing, and official browser support has narrowed to Chrome and Safari.

**Who it's best for:** Notion-native users on Business who want resources inside their existing workspace. **Skip it if** you want AI without the top tier, or a dedicated reader. See [Marqly vs Notion Web Clipper](/compare/marqly-vs-notion-web-clipper) and the [Notion Web Clipper alternatives](/blog/notion-web-clipper-alternative) guide.

- **Pros:** free clipper that works on the free plan; database properties give views and filters; one workspace for everything; offline on all plans; official API and mobile share-sheet saving.
- **Cons:** AI search and chat require Business at $20/member/month; no tags, notes or highlights at capture; documented clip-fidelity problems; browser support narrowed to Chrome and Safari; slow saving into large databases.
- **Price:** clipper free · Plus $10/member/month · Business $20/member/month (billed annually).

## 7. Evernote — best AI over documents and images, worst free tier

Evernote is the odd entry here because its AI advantage is not over bookmarks but over *documents*. Its search is best-in-class with OCR that reads text inside images, and paid plans search inside PDFs; v11 added an assistant that chats across your notes, semantic search, and AI meeting notes. If your saved material is scanned handouts, receipts and screenshots rather than articles, nothing else in this table finds it.

The free plan is the problem: **50 notes, one notebook, one device** — a demo, not a tier. Starter is $14.99/month or $99/year and Advanced $24.99/month or $249.99/year, among the priciest here, with prices raised repeatedly under owner Bending Spoons. There is no automatic tagging, so clips still need manual filing, and you cannot highlight text on live web pages.

**Who it's best for:** people whose archive is documents and images and who already pay for Evernote. **Skip it if** you want a bookmark manager, or anything free.

- **Pros:** best-in-class OCR and PDF search; real AI assistant with semantic search over notes; five-browser clipper; mature apps with offline on paid plans.
- **Cons:** free plan is 50 notes, one notebook, one device; expensive; no auto-tagging; no live-page highlighting; a heavyweight note platform is overkill for bookmarks.
- **Price:** free (50 notes) · Starter $14.99/month or $99/year · Advanced $24.99/month or $249.99/year.

## The one that is not an AI bookmark manager: Raindrop.io

Raindrop.io appears in almost every AI-bookmark-manager list and should not, so it is worth being explicit. It is an excellent bookmark manager with **the most generous free tier in the category** — unlimited bookmarks, collections and devices, with apps on every platform including Linux. Pro at $28/year adds full-text search, **Stella AI save-time tag and collection suggestions**, permanent page copies, annotations and daily backups.

Stella's suggestions are real automation and genuinely useful. But they happen at *save* time and they are suggestions, and **search remains keyword-based at every price**. Raindrop cannot find a save you paraphrase. If your problem is "I can never find anything I saved," Raindrop will give you a much nicer place to lose things. It is the right pick for free unlimited collecting and the wrong pick for AI retrieval — see [Marqly vs Raindrop](/compare/marqly-vs-raindrop).
