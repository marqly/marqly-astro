## What changed since our 2026 review

Five things, and one of them is significant enough that a reader of the old edition would be misinformed today.

1. **mymind has an API now.** Our 2026 edition listed "no API" among the things mymind does not have. That was accurate then and is wrong now: a read/write API entered beta in **July 2026**. It is young, it is not an import screen, and it does not reverse the no-bulk-import philosophy — but it does mean a determined user can script their way in and out, which was not true a year ago.
2. **The Student of Life yearly price is $79, not $72.** Our fact sheet calls this out explicitly because the two numbers are easy to transpose, and $72 happens to be the price of a competing annual plan. mymind's mid tier is **$79/year or $7.99/month**.
3. **The platform list is now complete.** Web, Mac, iOS and Android, with extensions for **Chrome, Edge and Safari**. Firefox is the interesting gap: the add-on was pulled from Mozilla's store, and Firefox support now means manually installing an extension file. If you live in Firefox, that is a real friction point the 2026 edition did not name.
4. **We under-credited mymind's search.** The old edition said retrieval "leans on tags and visual memory, not meaning." Our current fact sheet records both full-text search and semantic search as present. The honest 2027 framing is narrower than either claim: mymind retrieves well over what its AI can see in a card — objects, colours, brands, and text recognised inside images — and there is no highlight-and-note layer on top of a live page for it to search, because mymind does not have one.
5. **A higher tier we quoted in 2026 is no longer verified.** The 2026 edition listed a $299/year Newton plan as "coming soon." Our October 2026 fact sheet does not carry it, so we are not repeating the number. Treat any four-figure mymind tier as unconfirmed until mymind publishes it.

What did *not* change is the part that decides whether mymind is right for you: the 100-card free cap, the absence of bulk import, the absence of sharing, and the reservation of summaries and article backup for the top tier.

## What is mymind?

mymind calls itself "the extension of your mind" — one private place where you throw everything you want to remember. Images, articles, quotes, products, notes, screenshots, PDFs. There is no folder tree, no tag vocabulary to maintain, and no filing decision at save time. The AI reads what you saved, tags it automatically, recognises what is inside it, and everything lives on one endless visual canvas of cards.

Two design commitments define the product, and both are load-bearing.

The first is **no organising, ever.** The founders treat manual filing as a bug rather than a feature, so the app has no folder tree at all. This is not laziness dressed as philosophy — it is a genuine position, and for a certain kind of saver it is liberating. It is also a position that only works if the automatic tagging is good, which is why the AI-free tier is such a strange product.

The second is **absolute privacy.** No social layer, no sharing, no collaboration, no ads, no tracking. Revenue comes only from memberships. It is deliberately a party of one, and the company has been explicit that this is the point rather than a gap on the roadmap.

The aesthetic is the calmest in the category — closer to a moodboard than a database. For people who have abandoned three bookmark managers because the interface made them feel behind, that is not a soft benefit. It is the reason they will actually use the thing.

## Key features

- **AI auto-tagging.** Save anything and mymind classifies it — a book, a product, a tweet, an article about typography — without input from you. This is the mechanism that makes no-folders survivable, and it starts on the Student of Life plan.
- **Text recognition inside images.** Screenshot a quote and mymind reads the text in the image, so it becomes searchable later. This is the single most useful feature in the product for anyone who screenshots instead of clipping, and it is why visual material retrieves better in mymind than in most text-first tools.
- **Object, colour and brand recognition.** The AI does not stop at words. It identifies what is in an image, which makes "that red chair" and "the Nike thing" viable queries in a way keyword matching never allows.
- **Smart spaces.** Instead of folders you construct, mymind assembles collections automatically around topics it detects. You can also spin a space out of a search.
- **Serendipity.** A resurfacing mode that shows you old saves you have forgotten. Pleasant, occasionally genuinely useful, and the closest thing mymind has to a review system.
- **Reading mode and article backup — Mastermind only.** Reading mode, a saved copy of the article that survives link rot, AI summaries and the PDF analyzer all sit on the $12.99/month tier.
- **Save as PDF and annotation notes.** Both present, which our 2026 edition did not credit.
- **Privacy by architecture.** Nothing can be shared. There is no public board, no collaborator seat, no link that opens a collection to someone else. Whether that is a feature or a dealbreaker is entirely a function of who you are.

What is genuinely absent matters just as much. There is **no in-page highlighter** that persists on the live web page — you clip a quote into a card instead, and cards can carry highlighted text and notes, but the page itself stays untouched (see our guide to [highlighting text on any website](/blog/how-to-highlight-text-on-any-website) for why that distinction matters). There is **no YouTube summary or transcript tooling**, **no chat with your saves**, **no tab saver**, **no offline reading**, and **no self-hosting**.

## What each plan actually buys you

Read the tiers as three different products rather than three sizes of one.

**Guest, $0, 100 cards.** Enough to evaluate the canvas and the feel of saving into it, which is the right thing to evaluate — but not enough to evaluate retrieval. A hundred cards is a fortnight of normal saving for a heavy clipper, and you will not have accumulated the volume where search quality becomes visible. There is no time limit, so you can sit on it indefinitely; there is also no reading mode and no advanced AI, so you will not see the two features that justify the price.

**The Bookmarker, $4.99/month.** Visual bookmarking without AI. As above: the one plan in the ladder we cannot recommend, because it pays for the aesthetic and skips the engine.

**Student of Life, $7.99/month or $79/year.** The entry point to the actual product. Unlimited cards, AI tagging, image text recognition, smart spaces. If you want mymind and you do not read long articles inside it, this is the tier to buy, and the annual price is the sane way to buy it — $79 versus $95.88 paid monthly.

**Mastermind, $12.99/month or $129/year.** Adds AI summaries, reading mode, article backup against link rot, PDF analysis, larger video files, and Same Vibe for visually similar cards. This is the tier you need if mymind is where you read, rather than where you collect. It is also the tier where the price comparison against the rest of the market starts to sting.

## The free guest plan: what 100 cards really means

The cap is easy to misread, so here it is precisely. mymind's free tier is a **guest plan limited to 100 cards, with no time limit** — it is not a trial that expires, and it does not ask for a card. Paid plans remove the cap.

What that means in practice:

- **It is a permanent tier, and permanent is better than a trial.** You can keep it forever and you will never be locked out of what you already saved.
- **One hundred cards is a sample, not a library.** For a designer clipping references it is roughly two weeks. For a researcher it is an afternoon.
- **You will hit the wall at exactly the moment the product starts to work.** Serendipity and smart spaces need volume before they surprise you; 100 cards is below that threshold, so the free tier cannot show you the feature that justifies paying.
- **The AI is not in it.** Auto-tagging, image text recognition and smart spaces all start on Student of Life. Summaries and reading mode start on Mastermind. The free guest plan is a look at the canvas, not a look at the intelligence.

If a real free tier is a hard requirement, the market has better answers. Raindrop.io gives unlimited bookmarks, collections and devices with no expiry at all; our [free bookmark manager roundup for 2027](/blog/best-free-bookmark-managers-2027) ranks the options that do not run out.

## Import and export: the portability question

This is the section our 2026 edition did not write, and in 2027 it is the one that decides the purchase for a lot of readers. Two shutdowns reshaped this market — **Omnivore closed in November 2024** and **Mozilla shut Pocket down on July 8, 2025**, with Pocket's official export window closing on **November 12, 2025**. Anyone choosing a saving tool now is choosing it with the knowledge that the tool might not outlive them.

**Getting in: you cannot, in bulk.** mymind supports no mass import — not from Pocket, not from Raindrop, not from browser bookmark HTML. This is a deliberate product position, not an oversight: the company argues a fresh start beats migrating clutter. The read/write API that entered beta in July 2026 makes a scripted import technically possible, but there is no official import tool and no supported path. If you have 4,000 saves sitting in an archive and the job is to make them findable, mymind cannot do that job. Our [Pocket alternatives guide for 2027](/blog/best-pocket-alternatives-2027) covers the tools that can, and the [import-fidelity study](/research/bookmark-import-fidelity) shows what actually survives a real migration rather than what a vendor claims survives one.

**Getting out: one-way, and only from two browsers.** From your account page you can download all your files — images and PDFs — plus a `cards.csv` containing your notes, quotes, bookmark links and text highlights. Two limits define it: **export only works from desktop Chrome or Edge**, and **there is no re-import format**. So the export is a backup, not a migration path. If you leave mymind in three years, you leave with a CSV and a folder of images and you rebuild by hand or by script.

**The durability verdict.** mymind's revenue model is memberships only — no ads, no tracking, no data licensing — which is a genuinely sound business for a small company and a better foundation than the free-with-no-revenue model that killed Omnivore. But durability is not only about whether the company survives. It is about whether your library survives a decision to leave, and on that measure mymind is the weakest of the major tools we cover: no way in, one way out, and the way out is browser-restricted.

## Strengths

- **The calmest, best-designed tool in the category.** Nothing else comes close visually. If ambience affects whether you actually open the app, that is worth real money and most reviews undervalue it.
- **Auto-tagging that mostly delivers on "no organising."** The classification is good enough that you stop thinking about filing, which is the entire pitch and the reason the product can exist without folders.
- **Unrivalled for visual material.** Object, colour and brand recognition plus text-inside-image means a screenshot library is genuinely searchable here. Text-first tools cannot match this.
- **A principled privacy position that is structural, not a setting.** No ads, no tracking, no social features, funded only by memberships. Nothing to accidentally turn on.
- **Fast, frictionless capture.** The extensions and mobile apps make saving nearly effortless, which is the habit that everything else depends on.
- **A permanent free tier rather than a trial.** Small, but it never expires and never asks for a card.

## Weaknesses

- **No bulk import, ever.** The disqualifier for anyone with an existing library. Not a rough edge — a stated philosophy.
- **Nothing can be shared.** No public board, no collaborator, no link that shows a collection to a colleague, a class or a client. For a designer sending references to a client, or a teacher handing a reading list to students, that is disqualifying on its own.
- **The best AI costs $129/year.** Summaries, reading mode and article backup are all Mastermind. Competitors include equivalents at $72 and $28.
- **The free tier cannot demonstrate the product.** One hundred cards is below the volume where smart spaces and serendipity become interesting.
- **No offline reading.** Our fact sheet records none. If you read on planes or in tunnels, look elsewhere.
- **Export is one-way and browser-restricted.** Desktop Chrome or Edge only, `cards.csv` plus files, and no re-import format.
- **Firefox is a second-class citizen.** The add-on was pulled from Mozilla's store; Firefox support means installing an extension file manually.
- **No YouTube tooling and no chat with your saves.** Increasingly standard elsewhere at lower prices.

## Who should skip mymind

Real disqualifiers, in the order people hit them:

- **You have an existing library.** Pocket archive, Raindrop collections, ten years of Chrome bookmarks — none of it comes in. This is not a temporary limitation and it is not on a roadmap.
- **You share anything with anyone.** Client reference boards, class reading lists, a shared research collection with a co-author. mymind has no sharing surface of any kind. If you teach, our [guide to bookmark managers for teachers in 2027](/blog/best-bookmark-managers-for-teachers-2027) explains why a view-only public page is the feature that decides this.
- **Your saves are mostly long articles you need to re-find by argument.** mymind retrieves well over what its AI can see in a card. If your library is prose and you search it by meaning weeks later, a semantic-first tool is built for that job and mymind is not.
- **You read offline.** There is no offline mode.
- **You live in Firefox.** Support means installing an extension file by hand after the add-on was pulled from Mozilla's store.
- **You want highlights on the page.** There is no in-page highlighter; you clip instead.
- **$129/year for summaries and article backup is more than you will spend.** Reasonable position. Both are included at $72/year and $28/year elsewhere.

And who should *not* skip it: designers, art directors, writers collecting references, and anyone whose saves are predominantly images, quotes and products — starting from an empty library, happy to pay for calm, and never needing to show the collection to another person. Inside that profile mymind is arguably the best tool available, and the aesthetic is not a frivolous reason to choose it. A tool you enjoy opening is a tool you keep.

## Verdict

mymind in 2027 is what it was in 2026 — the most beautiful private saving tool on the market — with a sharper set of edges now that we have measured them. The free guest plan is a 100-card sample. The AI that makes the no-folders philosophy work starts at $79/year, and the summaries and article backup that competitors include at base price cost $129/year. Nothing can be shared. Nothing can be imported in bulk. Export is a one-way `cards.csv` from desktop Chrome or Edge. The new read/write API in beta is the first genuine loosening of that posture, and it is worth watching.

Choose mymind when your material is visual, your library is starting fresh, and privacy and calm are worth a premium. Choose something else when you are migrating, sharing, reading offline, or counting the five-year cost.
