**The best bookmark manager for students in 2027 is Raindrop.io if the budget is zero,** because its free tier is unlimited bookmarks and collections with no time limit and it is a complete product rather than a demo. **Marqly is the best pick if the problem is finding a source again at citation time,** since semantic search retrieves a save by describing what you remember — and verified students pay $48 for the first year instead of $72. **Weava is the cheapest paid path at 70% off for students,** and **Readwise Reader has the largest discount on a full-featured tool at 50% off for anyone in academia.**

Student bookmarking is a different problem from professional bookmarking, and most roundups miss it. You are not managing a permanent library, you are managing a four-year arc on a budget that resets every September, and the decisive moment is not saving — it is 11pm three weeks before a deadline when you need a source you half-read in October and cannot remember the title of. Every tool below is scored on four things: what it costs across a degree, whether it publishes a real student discount, what its free tier actually lets you do, and whether it can return a source from memory rather than from an exact phrase.

One scope note before the list. **Zotero and Mendeley are reference managers, not bookmark managers,** and they are the right tool for citations, PDFs and bibliography output. They are not in our verified competitor data layer, so we do not compare their pricing or features here — treat that as a separate decision, and expect to need both. If you are choosing for a classroom rather than for yourself, see [the teacher roundup](/blog/best-bookmark-managers-for-teachers-2027).

## 2. Raindrop.io — best free tier, full stop

Raindrop.io wins the row that matters most on a student budget: its free plan gives unlimited bookmarks, unlimited collections and unlimited devices with no time limit, plus up to three highlights per bookmark and 100 MB/month of uploads. That is not a capped preview — it is a complete product, which is rare in this category and the reason it is the default recommendation for anyone who will not pay. Apps exist for web, Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS, Android and every major browser extension, so a student moving between a library desktop and a phone is covered.

What you do not get is retrieval by meaning. Organisation is genuinely world-class, but finding something still depends on the tags and keywords you typed yourself; there is no semantic search at any price. Pro ($28/year, or $112 across four years) adds full-text search, Stella AI save-time tag and collection suggestions, permanent page copies, annotations with unlimited highlights in four colours, and daily backups. Permanent page copies are the underrated one for a student: a source that dies mid-degree stays readable.

**Who it's best for:** any student who wants unlimited free bookmarking and will tag as they go. **Skip it if** you need to find a source from a vague memory of its argument rather than its words.

- **Pros:** unlimited free bookmarks, collections and devices; apps on every platform including Linux; strong visual organisation with nested collections; public collection sharing; Pro is the cheapest paid tier here and adds permanent page copies.
- **Cons:** no semantic search at any price; capture is link-first, so transcripts and article text are not kept; three-highlight cap on free; organisation discipline is entirely on you.
- **Price:** free (unlimited) · Pro $28/year or $3.25/month. No published student discount.

## 3. Readwise Reader — largest discount on a full-featured tool, and no free tier

Readwise Reader is the most capable reading system here: it handles large volumes across articles, newsletters, PDFs and video, with highlights flowing into Notion or Obsidian — a genuinely powerful pipeline for a student writing a dissertation. There is real AI covering summaries, YouTube handling and chat over what you have saved.

It also has the largest discount of any full-featured tool here — Weava's 70% is steeper, but Weava is a Chrome-only highlighter with real maintenance risk, not a library. And Readwise's discount is easy to miss because it is not shown as a tier on the pricing page. **Readwise gives 50% off to students and other members of academia:** you email hello@readwise.io to say you are a student *before* subscribing, then subscribe through the Readwise website. Applied to the Full plan that takes $119.88/year to about $60/year, or roughly **$240 across four years** — cheaper than Marqly's student path and far cheaper than Glasp's.

The blocker is that there is no way to try it for nothing. **There is no permanent free tier** — after a 30-day trial you must subscribe, at $9.99/month billed annually ($119.88/year) or $12.99/month monthly for the Full plan. Reader is only in the Full plan, so the cheaper Lite plan at $5.59/month billed annually covers classic Readwise highlight syncing without the Reader app at all. Search is keyword-based rather than semantic, tagging is manual, and there is no public sharing of collections.

**Who it's best for:** a postgraduate or heavy reader who consumes large volumes, wants highlights flowing into a note system, and qualifies for the 50% academic discount. **Skip it if** you need free, or if retrieval by meaning is your actual problem.

- **Pros:** the most complete reading pipeline; **50% off for students and academics** — the largest discount here; handles articles, newsletters, PDFs and video; highlights export into Notion or Obsidian; real AI summaries and chat.
- **Cons:** **no free tier at all** after a 30-day trial; the discount requires emailing before you subscribe, so it is not self-serve; Reader requires the Full plan; keyword rather than semantic search; manual tagging; no public sharing.
- **Price:** no free tier · Full $9.99/month billed annually ($119.88/year) or $12.99/month monthly · **about $60/year with the 50% academic discount** · Lite $5.59/month billed annually.

## 4. Glasp — best if you want to highlight in public

Glasp is the only tool here built around public highlighting: your highlights form a shareable knowledge profile, and you can discover what other people underlined. For a student in a cohort, that is a real study feature rather than a gimmick. Free includes unlimited public highlights, and its YouTube AI is strong for a lecture-heavy course.

Its discount is published and specific: **40% off Pro for students and educators**, billed yearly, with verification that takes a couple of minutes. Pro is **$12.50/month billed yearly ($150/year)** or **$15/month billed monthly**, so 40% off brings a year to about **$90** — roughly $360 across four years. There is also an Unlimited tier at $30/month billed yearly ($360/year).

The catch is that privacy costs money. **Free highlights are public by default, and keeping them private requires Pro** — a hard sell at list price, though the 40% discount changes the maths considerably. Free AI limits are tight at three YouTube summaries a day and twenty PDF chat messages a month. Prices rose about 20–25% in May 2026 and subscriptions are explicitly non-refundable. There is no full-page archiving, no full-text search and no offline mode, so it is a highlight layer rather than an archive — and it is run by a two-person team, which is a real longevity risk for something you would rely on for four years.

**Who it's best for:** students who want to learn in public and share highlights with a cohort. **Skip it if** your highlights are private by default, or if you need archiving and full-text search.

- **Pros:** unlimited free public highlights; a shareable knowledge profile; discovery of what others highlighted; strong YouTube AI; **published 40% student and educator discount**.
- **Cons:** private highlights require Pro; tight free AI limits; prices rose 20–25% in May 2026 and are non-refundable; no archiving, full-text search or offline; two-person team.
- **Price:** free (public highlights) · Pro $12.50/month billed yearly ($150/year) or $15/month monthly · Unlimited $30/month billed yearly ($360/year) · **40% off Pro for students and educators**.

## 5. mymind — best for visual saving, worst for a research workflow

mymind is fast, private and visually excellent, and its free guest plan caps at 100 cards but is free forever rather than a timed trial — a distinction worth noting in a category full of trials. Student of Life is $7.99/month or $79/year and unlocks AI tagging; Mastermind at $12.99/month or $129/year adds AI summaries, reading mode, article backup and the PDF analyzer. The Bookmarker tier at $4.99/month has no AI features at all.

One naming trap worth flagging: **"Student of Life" is a plan name, not a student discount.** There is no verified-student offer — anyone can buy that tier at that price. It is simply the mid-priced AI tier with a lifestyle brand attached, and it is easy to mistake for an education plan when scanning a pricing page.

For a research workflow, though, two design decisions are close to disqualifying. **There is no bulk import by design** — you cannot bring in Pocket, Raindrop or browser bookmarks, so a student migrating off four years of accumulated bookmarks starts from zero. And **nothing can be shared**: no public boards, no collaboration of any kind, which rules it out for group projects. Export is a one-way CSV plus files and works only from desktop Chrome or Edge. It is a superb personal visual save layer and a poor student research system.

**Who it's best for:** design and arts students who save more images and inspiration than articles and value privacy and aesthetics over organisation. **Skip it if** you need to migrate an existing library, share with a group, or export easily.

- **Pros:** fast, private, visually strong; free forever guest plan rather than a timed trial; AI tagging from the mid tier; good for image-heavy saving.
- **Cons:** **no bulk import by design**; **nothing can be shared** — no public boards or collaboration; export is one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge only; AI summaries need the $12.99/month tier; free caps at 100 cards; no actual student discount despite the tier name.
- **Price:** free guest plan to 100 cards · The Bookmarker $4.99/month · Student of Life $7.99/month or $79/year · Mastermind $12.99/month or $129/year.

## 6. Weava — the steepest student discount, on a product that looks neglected

Weava is built for exactly the student workflow of highlighting PDFs and web pages side by side and generating citations, and its free tier includes unlimited highlights and notes. It is also completely free at the entry level — the site's own words are "it's free and always will be" — with Premium at **$3.99/month** (the $7.99/month figure on its pricing page is struck through as the former price).

The discount is the steepest percentage in this category and it is published plainly: **70% off Premium for students**, claimed by emailing education@weavatools.com from a student email address. That takes $3.99/month to roughly $14/year, or about **$57 across four years** — the cheapest paid path here by a wide margin. Educators are handled differently and less transparently: Weava offers "special offers and packages for classes" through a contact form, with no published percentage, so a student discount and an educator discount are not the same thing here.

The reason it ranks below Readwise and Glasp despite the price is maintenance risk, not price. Its Chrome extension had not been updated since February 2024, the site's copyright reads 2022, the former iOS app is delisted, and reviews report disappearing highlights and folders, with the last 100 Chrome reviews averaging around 2.9 stars. It is Chrome-only — no Firefox or Safari extensions, no working mobile apps, no API. The free tier shows ads and caps storage at 100 MB with no sub-folders. And highlights are tied to exact URLs, so they vanish when a page's address changes, which is a genuine flaw for four-year research where sources move and get re-pathed.

**Who it's best for:** Chrome-only students who highlight PDFs and web pages together, want citations generated, and are paying so little that the maintenance risk is an acceptable bet. **Skip it if** you use Firefox or Safari, need mobile, or cannot afford to lose annotations when a source URL changes.

- **Pros:** **70% student discount — the steepest percentage here**, claimed by email from a student address; free unlimited highlights and notes; built for side-by-side PDF and web annotation; generates citations; about $57 across four years with the discount.
- **Cons:** strong neglect signals (extension stale since Feb 2024, copyright 2022, iOS app delisted); reported disappearing highlights and folders; Chrome-only with no working mobile apps or API; ads and a 100 MB cap on free; highlights break when a URL changes; educator pricing is not published.
- **Price:** free (ads, 100 MB) · Premium $3.99/month (formerly $7.99/month) · **students 70% off via education@weavatools.com** · educator and classroom packages on request.

## 7. Browser bookmarks — the free baseline that fails at dissertation scale

Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari bookmarks cost nothing, need no signup, and sync inside their own ecosystem. For a first-year reading list of thirty links, they are a legitimate answer and installing anything else is procrastination.

They fail at the scale a degree creates. Organisation is folder-only, so a source either lives in one place or is duplicated across profiles. Search is title-only, which is precisely the wrong model when you cannot remember the title. Nothing is captured, so a dead link removes the source from your bibliography at the worst possible moment. There is no highlighting, no semantic search, and no way to find a page by describing it. Every tool above exists because of those four gaps — and the moment you are writing something with citations, [import your browser bookmarks](/migrate) into a tool that captures the page rather than just the link.

**Who it's best for:** a light first-year reading list. **Skip it** the moment citations are involved.

- **Pros:** free; already installed; no learning curve; syncs within its own ecosystem.
- **Cons:** folder-only organisation; title-only search; no capture, so link rot deletes a source; no highlighting; no semantic search.
- **Price:** free.

For most students the honest read is a two-tool setup: a reference manager for citations and bibliography, plus one of the above for everything you saved but have not cited yet. If you only pay for one thing across a degree, the question is whether your failure mode is cost (Raindrop.io free) or retrieval (Marqly on the student year).

## What actually matters across a degree

**Verification friction.** Every student discount here is gated, and none of them is a simple checkbox at signup. Marqly verifies a university email at checkout. Readwise requires you to email hello@readwise.io *before* subscribing and then subscribe through its website. Weava requires you to email education@weavatools.com from a student address. Glasp runs a short verification for 40% off Pro billed yearly. Two of those four involve waiting on a human, so apply early rather than at the moment your card is due. Confirm the discount is granted before you rely on it in a budget, and re-confirm at renewal, since a discount that applied in year one may not in year three.

**Price movement.** This category repriced inside the window this article covers. Glasp rose about 20–25% in May 2026 and made subscriptions explicitly non-refundable; Evernote has raised prices repeatedly under Bending Spoons. A four-year cost estimate built on today's list price is an optimistic one, so prefer annual billing you can cancel over a multi-year commitment.

**Data portability.** You will graduate, and the tool may not survive you. Marqly exports CSV carrying URL, title, description and tags — Free exports the 100 most recent, Pro the whole library — with a complete account copy including highlights, notes and boards available by email to support@marqly.com. Diigo exports CSV, HTML and RSS with a web API. mymind's export is one-way CSV from desktop Chrome or Edge only. Weava highlights are tied to exact URLs and break when a page moves. Check the exit path before the import, not after.

**Link rot.** A source that 404s during your dissertation is worse than one you never saved. Permanent page copies (Raindrop Pro), local save-as-PDF (Marqly, processed in Chrome and Edge), and article backup (mymind Mastermind) are the real defences; browser bookmarks and Weava offer none.
