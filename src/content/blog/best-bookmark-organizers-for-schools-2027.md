**The best bookmark organizer for a school department in 2027 is Marqly Teams if the priority is a shared resource library that students can open with no accounts,** at $9 per seat per month with a three-seat minimum and a licence that turns read-only rather than deleting anything when it lapses. **Notion is the best choice when your school already runs it,** because you inherit the identity and admin controls a district IT team expects. **Diigo is the best budget option,** with a free Teacher plan for verified educators and a Business tier at $10 per user per month.

Buying for one classroom and buying for a department are different problems, and most "best bookmark manager for schools" articles answer the wrong one. A single teacher needs low filing overhead and a way to hand a reading list to students. A department head, a librarian, or an IT lead needs something that survives staff turnover, that can be billed on one line, that can be administered by someone who is not the person who built it, and that does not take a year of curated resources hostage when a licence is not renewed.

This guide is scored on those institutional constraints, not on consumer features. If you are choosing for your own classroom instead, read [the teacher roundup](/blog/best-bookmark-managers-for-teachers-2027), which weighs video handling and filing load. If you are a student, [the student guide](/blog/best-bookmark-managers-for-students-2027) weighs four-year cost.

## 2. Notion — best when your school already runs it

If your school or district already uses Notion, the procurement question is largely settled before you read a comparison: you get an identity model, an admin console, guest access, and a workspace your staff already know, and a bookmark collection is just another database. Notion Plus is $10 per member per month and Business is $20 per member per month billed annually, with the clipper itself free and functional on Notion's generous free plan. Offline mode arrived in 2025 on all plans, and there are native apps for web, Mac, Windows, iOS and Android.

Two limits matter for a school. First, the AI is a Business-tier capability — meaningful AI search and chat over clipped content is bundled with Business and above, while Free and Plus get only a trial of it, so the $10 tier does not buy the feature most procurement documents are actually asking for. Second, the clipper is thin at capture time: no tags, notes, highlights or screenshots, just a destination picker, and Notion's own documentation warns that clip fidelity varies and formatting can go missing — a real risk when the resource is a formatted worksheet or a table-heavy handout. Official browser support has also narrowed to Chrome and Safari, which excludes Firefox and Edge staff.

**Who it's best for:** schools already on Notion that want resources beside notes, tasks and unit plans, and that can budget Business if AI search is a requirement. **Skip it if** you need reliable capture of formatted teaching materials, if your staff are on Firefox or Edge, or if you want AI without the top tier.

- **Pros:** clipper is free and works on the free plan; database properties give shared views, filters and permissions; the identity and admin model a district expects; native apps everywhere; offline on all plans; official API.
- **Cons:** AI search and chat require Business at $20/member/month; bare-bones capture with no tags, notes or highlights; documented clip-fidelity problems; browser support narrowed to Chrome and Safari; large databases can be slow to save into.
- **Price:** clipper free · Plus $10/member/month · Business $20/member/month (billed annually) · a free Education Plan exists but is limited to individuals at **accredited colleges and universities** — Notion states explicitly that K-12 students and K-12 educators are not eligible, and it requires a school email listed in the World Higher Education Database, one member per workspace, and annual re-verification. For a K-12 district, budget for paid seats.

## 3. Diigo — best budget option with a real educator plan

Diigo is the only tool here that publishes education-specific pricing at both ends: a free Teacher plan for verified educators with a class console, and a Business tier at $10 per user per month for an institution. Its education-shaped strength is collaborative annotation — multi-colour highlighting and sticky notes placed directly on live web pages, with groups that let a class or a department annotate a shared library together, and outliners that turn those highlights into a structured document. Standard is $40/year and Professional $59/year for individuals, adding unlimited highlights, cached pages, full-text search and PDF annotation. Data stays portable through CSV, HTML and RSS export plus a web API, which is a genuine procurement point: an exit path that does not require a support ticket.

The institutional risk is that Diigo is visibly coasting. Its Chrome extension was last updated in 2024, the Android app has left Google Play, and the official blog domain is dead. There is no AI of any kind, the free tier is ad-supported with lifetime caps that Diigo's own pages state inconsistently (500–1,000 bookmarks, 100–200 highlights), and SSO is not published. A department should treat it as a low-cost annotation tool with a clear exit plan rather than as long-term infrastructure.

**Who it's best for:** budget-constrained departments that need collaborative annotation and a free tier for verified educators, and that can accept an ageing product. **Skip it if** you need current apps, AI retrieval, or identity management.

- **Pros:** free Teacher plan for verified educators with a class console; Business at $10/user/month; live-page highlighting and sticky notes; collaborative groups; cheap individual tiers; portable exports and a web API.
- **Cons:** neglected product with a stale extension and no Android app; no AI at all; ad-supported free tier with self-contradictory caps; SSO not published; full-text search paywalled.
- **Price:** free (ad-supported, capped) · Standard $40/year · Professional $59/year · Business $10/user/month.

## 4. Raindrop.io — best free baseline for a staff room

For a department that will not get a budget line at all, Raindrop.io is the most usable free option: unlimited bookmarks, unlimited collections and unlimited devices with no time limit, plus up to three highlights per bookmark and 100 MB/month of uploads. Public collections let a shared reading list open from a link, which solves the staff-room and student-access problem without any provisioning. Apps exist for web, Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS, Android and every major browser extension, so a mixed-device staff is covered.

The institutional limit is that Raindrop has no team product to speak of in the verified data: pricing is per person at $28/year or $3.25/month, with no published team admin, seat billing, or identity management. A department adopting it is adopting N individual accounts and a convention, not an administered workspace. There is also no semantic search at any price — Pro adds full-text search, Stella AI save-time tag and collection suggestions, permanent page copies, annotations and daily backups, but retrieval still depends on the tags your staff typed.

**Who it's best for:** a staff room or a small department that wants a free, well-built shared collection and can live without central administration. **Skip it if** you need one bill, admin roles, or retrieval by meaning.

- **Pros:** the most generous free tier in the category; public collection sharing; polished apps on every platform including Linux; strong visual organisation with nested collections; cheapest paid tier here.
- **Cons:** no team admin, seat billing, or published identity management; no semantic search at any price; three-highlight cap on free; organisation depends entirely on manual tagging discipline across a whole staff.
- **Price:** free (unlimited bookmarks) · Pro $28/year or $3.25/month per person.

## 5. Evernote — best for a searchable PDF and image archive

Where a school's problem is documents rather than links — scanned handouts, PDF worksheets, photographed whiteboards — Evernote's search is the strongest here. OCR reads text inside images, paid plans search inside PDFs and documents, and the clipper works on all five major browsers with full page, simplified article, selection, bookmark and annotated screenshot modes. Evernote v11 added real AI: an assistant that chats across notes, semantic search, and AI meeting notes, which maps well to department-meeting minutes.

The cost structure is the obstacle. Evernote's free plan is effectively a demo at 50 notes, one notebook and one device, so an institution is buying per person: Starter at $14.99/month or $99/year, Advanced at $24.99/month or $249.99/year — among the priciest in the category, with prices raised repeatedly under owner Bending Spoons. Team admin and SSO are not published in the verified data, there is no automatic tagging, and you cannot highlight text on live web pages. For a department, per-seat Evernote at Advanced pricing is difficult to justify for bookmarking alone.

**Who it's best for:** a department whose archive is documents and images rather than links, and that already pays for Evernote. **Skip it if** the budget is per-seat and the need is shared link curation.

- **Pros:** best-in-class OCR and PDF search; five-browser clipper; tags and destination notebook at clip time; real AI in v11; mature apps with offline on paid plans.
- **Cons:** free plan is 50 notes, one notebook, one device; expensive per seat; no team admin or published SSO; no auto-tagging; no live-page highlighting.
- **Price:** free (50 notes) · Starter $14.99/month or $99/year · Advanced $24.99/month or $249.99/year per person.

## 6. Browser bookmarks and the district stack — the default you should choose deliberately

Most schools end up here by accident rather than by decision: bookmarks live in individual Chrome profiles managed by whatever the district's device policy syncs, and the department's real resource list lives in a shared Drive folder of pasted links. It is free, needs no training, and works until the first staff turnover.

The failure modes are institutional, not technical. Folder-only organisation means a resource lives in one place or is duplicated across every teacher's profile. Title-only search means the save labelled "Untitled" is unrecoverable. Nothing is captured, so a dead link silently removes a resource from the curriculum. And there is no shared, live collection — exporting a bookmarks file and emailing it produces a snapshot that is stale the same week. If your district already provisions Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, the pragmatic answer is often to keep the resource list in that stack where identity and admin already exist, and use a dedicated bookmark tool only for the teachers who hit the four limits above. See [how browser bookmark import works](/migrate) if you are consolidating profiles into one library.

**Who it's best for:** a school that wants zero spend and zero provisioning and accepts snapshots instead of a live shared collection. **Skip it** the moment a department needs one authoritative, current resource list.

- **Pros:** free; already deployed; no training; syncs inside its own ecosystem; managed by existing device policy.
- **Cons:** folder-only; title-only search; no capture, so link rot removes resources; no shared live collection; per-profile duplication across a department.
- **Price:** free.

## Which should a school choose?

- **A department that curates together and publishes to students, on a card-paid budget** → **Marqly Teams.** Three-seat minimum, every seat includes all Pro features, cancellation turns read-only and deletes nothing.
- **A school that already runs Notion** → **Notion.** You inherit identity, admin and a workspace staff know; budget Business if AI search is a requirement.
- **A budget-constrained department that needs collaborative annotation** → **Diigo.** Free Teacher plan for verified educators, Business at $10/user/month, with a portable exit path.
- **A staff room with no budget line** → **Raindrop.io.** Unlimited free bookmarks and public collections, without central administration.
- **A department whose archive is documents and images** → **Evernote.** Best OCR and PDF search, at per-seat prices that are hard to justify for links alone.
- **A district that requires SSO or SCIM** → **Notion or your existing Workspace stack.** Marqly, Diigo and Raindrop.io do not publish either.

The requirement that eliminates options fastest is identity. If your procurement document says SSO or SCIM, Marqly, Diigo and Raindrop.io are all out on the verified data, and the honest shortlist is Notion or whatever your district already provisions. If it does not, the decision reduces to cost per seat and whether students need accounts.

## What to put in the procurement document

Four questions will resolve most of this category faster than any feature matrix.

**What is the real seat minimum and the real bill?** Marqly Teams needs three seats and bills one Owner-paid amount in USD with no invoices or bank transfer. Notion and Diigo bill per member or per user. Raindrop and Evernote have no team billing at all, which means a department adopting them is managing N personal subscriptions on N personal cards — a common and usually unremarked failure in school tooling.

**Who administers it when the person who set it up leaves?** Marqly has Owner, Admin and Member, an activity log for Owners and Admins, and email invitations that hold a seat for seven days. Notion has a fuller model including Guest. Raindrop and Evernote have no team admin in the verified data. Staff turnover is a certainty; an unadministered collection is how a department loses a year of work.

**What happens on cancellation?** This is the question to ask first, because the answers differ most. Marqly turns read-only and deletes nothing, and you can resume with the same members and content. Anything that deletes on lapse should be treated as unsuitable for a curated archive regardless of price.

**Can students open it with no account?** Marqly public boards and Raindrop public collections both open from a link; a published Notion page does too; Diigo instead asks students to join a group; Pinterest boards are public but embedded in an ad-supported feed. If your rollout assumes a link in an LMS, verify the no-signup path before you buy seats.
