## Raindrop.io — the most generous classic free tier

Raindrop's free plan is the category benchmark: **unlimited bookmarks, unlimited collections, tags, and every app** — web, iOS, Android, desktop, extensions — with no expiry. For pure link collecting with manual organization, nothing free matches its polish.

The honest caveat is what's *not* in the free tier: **full-text search, permanent copies of saved pages, AI tag suggestions, and the duplicate finder all live in Pro** ($28/year — cheap, to be fair). And even Pro's search is keyword-based, not semantic — you find saves by words in them, not by describing them. That distinction is the whole thesis of our [AI bookmark manager guide](/blog/best-ai-bookmark-manager-2026).

**Pros:**

- Unlimited bookmarks and collections, free, on every platform
- Polished apps and a mature, decade-plus product
- Pro is only $28/year if you outgrow free

**Cons:**

- Free search is titles/tags only — full-text costs Pro, meaning search doesn't exist at any price
- Organization is manual: you file into collections yourself
- No summaries, no highlights-to-library flow on free

## Linkwarden — best free self-hosted archiver

Linkwarden is the preservationist's pick: open source (AGPL, 19k+ GitHub stars), self-hostable for free, and built around **full-page preservation** — every bookmark gets an archived copy, so link rot can't eat your library. It's collaborative, supports highlights and RSS, has real mobile apps, and offers optional AI tagging if you wire up a provider. Don't want to run a server? The cloud plan is $3/user/month with a 14-day trial.

**Pros:**

- Free forever when self-hosted; your data on your disk
- Full-page archives of everything — the best answer to dead links here
- Active development, mobile apps, browser extensions

**Cons:**

- "Free" costs a server ($5–15/month on typical hosts) plus your time
- AI features are optional bolt-ons, not the core experience
- Setup and maintenance are real work compared to signing up for Raindrop

## Karakeep — best free self-hosted AI bookmarking

Karakeep (formerly Hoarder, renamed in 2025; 24k+ GitHub stars) is what happens when the self-hosted crowd builds an AI bookmark manager: save links, notes, and images, and an LLM you configure — an OpenAI key or a local Ollama model — **auto-tags everything**, with full-text and semantic search across it all. Extensions for Chrome, Firefox, and Safari plus iOS and Android apps round it out. It's the closest free thing to the commercial AI tools, if you're happy administering it.

**Pros:**

- AI tagging and semantic search, free, under your own roof
- Local-LLM support means the AI can be private too
- Full apps and extensions — unusually complete for a self-hosted project

**Cons:**

- You run the server, the updates, and the backups
- AI costs surface eventually: API usage fees or local GPU horsepower
- Rougher edges than polished commercial apps; smaller support surface

## Browser bookmarks — the free baseline everyone already has

Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge all ship a bookmark manager: free, instant, synced within their own ecosystem, and exportable as a standard HTML file. For a few dozen stable links — banking, tools, docs — they're genuinely fine, and pretending otherwise would be marketing.

They collapse at scale, for four reasons: **folder-only organization** (every save is a filing decision), **title-only search** (describe a page and find nothing), **no content capture** (a dead link is a dead end), and **ecosystem lock** (Safari bookmarks don't follow you to Chrome). When any of those starts to hurt, that's the signal to graduate — and that exported HTML file imports directly into Raindrop, Linkwarden, Karakeep, or Marqly.

**Pros:**

- Free, built in, zero setup, syncs across your devices in one ecosystem
- Standard HTML export means you're never locked in
- Perfectly adequate for a small, stable set of links

**Cons:**

- Folders and title search stop working past a few hundred saves
- No tagging, no summaries, no content search, no archiving
- Cross-browser sync doesn't exist

## Moving between them (you're never stuck)

The quiet good news about this category in 2026: the exits are unlocked. Every browser exports bookmarks as a standard HTML file, and that file imports into Raindrop, Linkwarden, Karakeep, and Marqly alike. Raindrop collections export cleanly and import directly into Marqly; Pocket's final export files still import too. The self-hosted pair goes further — with Linkwarden and Karakeep your data literally sits in a database you own.

Practically, that means the cost of choosing "wrong" today is an afternoon, not a lock-in. Start with the free tier that matches your biggest pain, use it for a month at real volume, and switch if it doesn't hold. The only expensive mistake is staying with a system you've stopped trusting — that's how you end up with 2,000 saves in four places and confidence in none of them.
